### Score: 83/100 — [MagicPrompt: Ultra-Lightweight Prompt Tuning for Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14595)

**Authors:** Yinhan Zhang, Dinwei Tan, Xianghao Kong, Yue Ma, Yeying Jin, Anyi Rao  
*HKUST / HKUST(GZ) / Tencent — [Project](https://github.com/YinHan-Zhang/MagicPrompt)*

## TL;DR
MagicPrompt is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework for video diffusion models that achieves competitive generation quality with less than 1% trainable parameters, using attention-embedded soft prompts and a dual-space reward optimization strategy that stabilizes reward training across the full high-noise denoising schedule.

## Problem
Adapting large-scale video diffusion models (VDMs) to downstream tasks via existing PEFT methods remains problematic on two fronts: (1) methods like LoRA and adapter-based approaches still require millions of trainable parameters that become prohibitive on billion-parameter models, and (2) reward-based training for condition-guided tasks (image-to-video, control-to-video) is unstable at high-noise denoising stages because pixel-space rewards applied to noisy latent representations create optimization inconsistency. No prior approach solves both simultaneously.

## Key Contributions
- **Attention-Embedded Prompt Tuning (AEPT):** Injects trainable soft prompts directly into the Key and Value projections of attention layers, leaving Query projections and all backbone weights frozen. Steers generation with orders-of-magnitude fewer parameters than LoRA and prevents catastrophic forgetting of the pre-trained generative distribution.
- **Dual-Space Reward Feedback Optimization:** Applies pixel-space perceptual rewards (HPS/MPS) for visual quality at low-noise stages and latent-space self-supervised objectives at high-noise stages, solving the noise-incompatibility problem that breaks direct reward training on denoising trajectories.
- Competitive performance across text-to-video, image-to-video, and control-to-video tasks with strong cross-scale scalability across model sizes.

## Method
MagicPrompt freezes all backbone weights and inserts three lightweight components: visual prompt tokens prepended to Key/Value projections, shift-bias scalars for attention redistribution, and textual prompt tokens guiding cross-attention. Together these constitute AEPT, which steers attention without weight modification. The dual-space reward system then trains these prompts via a two-stage loss: pixel-space HPS/MPS rewards at timesteps close to clean data (low noise), and a latent-space denoising consistency objective at high-noise timesteps, ensuring gradient signal remains meaningful throughout the denoising schedule. Total trainable parameters remain under 1% of the full VDM.

## Why It Matters
Fine-tuning billion-scale video diffusion models for downstream applications — custom styles, new control signals, domain adaptation — is a core bottleneck for video generation deployment. MagicPrompt makes this practical on consumer hardware by reducing parameter count drastically (below LoRA) while also fixing the RL alignment instability that has blocked reward-based video generation fine-tuning. It plugs directly into existing VDM architectures and provides a practical path to customizing state-of-the-art video models like Wan2.1/2.2 without retraining.

### Score: 82/100 — [Hierarchical Denoising For Multi-Step Visual Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15278)

**Authors:** Zezhong Qian, Xiaowei Chi, Chak-Wing Mak, Tianze Zhou, Ruibin Yuan, Yuhan Rui, Hengzhe Sun, Zhuoqun Wu, Yuming Li, Siyuan Qian, Sirui Han, Shanghang Zhang  
*Peking University / HKUST / Beihang University / Fuzhou University — [Project](https://hierarchical-diffusion-reasoning.github.io/)*

## TL;DR
HDR (Hierarchical Denoising for Visual Reasoning) introduces a tree-structured latent hierarchy into streaming autoregressive video diffusion, enabling coarse-to-fine multi-step visual reasoning before committing to frame output — achieving a 76.2% improvement in task success over streaming AR diffusion baselines while remaining 54× faster than bidirectional diffusion.

## Problem
Video generation models are increasingly used for multi-step visual reasoning tasks (maze navigation, Tower of Hanoi, physics puzzles), but existing paradigms face a fundamental tension: (1) streaming autoregressive diffusion commits to each frame left-to-right and cannot revise earlier decisions, limiting logical consistency across long reasoning chains; (2) bidirectional diffusion allows global revision but requires dense full-sequence denoising at every step, making it 54× slower and incompatible with low-latency streaming. No existing approach achieves both coherent multi-step reasoning and efficient streaming generation.

## Key Contributions
- **Hierarchical Denoising (HDR):** Organizes video latents into a tree-structured hierarchy. Coarse hierarchy layers are maintained at higher residual noise levels, preserving multiple high-level planning hypotheses. Finer layers progressively reduce noise to instantiate concrete visual states. This gives the model an explicit revision space for global planning before committing to frames.
- **SHAP (Sparse Hierarchical Attention Pattern):** Each token attends only to its local temporal context and parent-level nodes in the hierarchy, enabling multi-scale information flow without dense full-sequence attention and preserving streaming generation efficiency.
- **Level-stratified multi-step reasoning benchmark:** Six out-of-distribution tasks (maze navigation, Tower of Hanoi, one-line drawing, sliding puzzle, Sokoban, water pouring) evaluating logical consistency over multi-step trajectories.
- Strong data efficiency: retains 82.9% of full-data success using only 2% of training data (vs. 52.0% for bidirectional diffusion).

## Method
HDR extends streaming autoregressive diffusion by inserting a tree of latent nodes between the causal context window and the output stream. At each generation step, the model maintains K hierarchy levels; level 1 (coarsest) operates at high residual noise and captures global planning states, while level K (finest) produces the actual output frame at near-zero noise. A layer-wise flow-matching objective trains each hierarchy level's denoising schedule independently, calibrated to its position in the tree. SHAP replaces standard temporal self-attention with fixed local windows plus upward-only communication to parent nodes, reducing attention computation per step. Real-world robot maze experiments validate transfer of the hierarchical planning ability to physical interaction.

## Why It Matters
As video models evolve into general visual reasoners (as demonstrated by Veo3 solving maze and physics tasks), the open challenge is combining global coherent planning with low-latency streaming output. HDR's tree-structured hierarchy directly resolves this tension by keeping high-level hypotheses "open" (partially denoised) while only committing finer levels to concrete frames. This is directly relevant to video generation systems that need to maintain narrative or task coherence over many steps, and to world models for robotics where agents need to plan while generating future frames in real time.
