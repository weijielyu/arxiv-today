### 1. Taming I2V models for Image HOI Editing: A Cognitive Benchmark and Agentic Self-Correcting Framework
**Score: 88/100** · [arXiv:2606.19073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19073)  
**Authors:** Jiayi Gao, Qingchao Chen, Yuxin Peng, Yang Liu  
**Code:** https://github.com/oceanflowlab/HOI-Edit

## TL;DR
The paper introduces HOI-Edit, the first benchmark for Human-Object Interaction image editing across three cognitive levels, and proposes SCPE (Self-Correcting Process Editing) — an agentic framework that harnesses Image-to-Video models' temporal capabilities for dynamic HOI editing via an iterative error-diagnosis playbook, achieving SOTA on interaction metrics and competitive performance with commercial systems.

## Problem
Standard image editing treats interactions as static pixel manipulations, failing at complex HOI editing which requires modeling dynamic, causally-grounded relationships between entities. No dedicated benchmark existed, and global metrics like CLIP score cannot evaluate entangled pair-wise interaction validity or physical plausibility. Editing methods can change appearance but cannot model *how entities interact* with physical and causal consistency.

## Key Contributions
- **HOI-Edit benchmark**: three hierarchical cognitive levels — L1 (Foundational Edits: dynamic verb modification + identity preservation), L2 (Context Spatial Understanding: entity selection and terminal placement), L3 (Causal and Physical Reasoning: process decomposition and physics adherence)
- **HOI-Eval metric**: region-sensitive pair-wise evaluation using bounding-box-grounded VLM Q&A ("Thinking with Pair-wise Regions"), enabling identity, interaction, and context-aware verification
- **SCPE (Self-Correcting Process Editing)**: agentic self-correcting framework with a dynamic Playbook mapping failure patterns (e.g., physics violations, trajectory errors) to validated prompting strategies, iteratively refined by a Video Analyst + Critic agent loop

## Method
SCPE uses I2V models as the editing backbone because video generation naturally models temporal dynamics, and crucially provides a "replay of the failure process" — failed generations reveal *why* errors occur (e.g., a hand trajectory steering toward the wrong target), enabling structured diagnosis. The agentic loop: (1) generate video from current prompt, (2) Video Analyst performs sample-wise error analysis, (3) Critic synthesizes global insights and incrementally updates the Playbook, (4) Playbook refines naive instructions into robust video prompts for the next iteration. Final editing output is key frames extracted from the generated video.

## Why It Matters
First framework to reframe image HOI editing as a dynamic temporal process, leveraging I2V models' native temporal capability. The agentic self-correcting architecture — failure diagnosis → playbook update → prompt refinement — is a generalizable pattern for any iterative generative editing task. SCPE outperforms all open-source SOTA editing models and is competitive with commercial SOTA (Google's Nano Banana) on interaction scores, demonstrating that agentic iteration can bridge the open/closed-source performance gap. Directly relevant to building robust world models that must understand contextual scene dynamics.

### 2. UniTemp: Unlocking Video Generation in Any Temporal Order via Bidirectional Distillation
**Score: 88/100** · [arXiv:2606.18702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18702)  
**Authors:** Lin Zhang, Sicheng Mo, Zefan Cai, Jinhong Lin, Zihao Lin, Jiuxiang Gu, Krishna Kumar Singh, Yuheng Li, Yin Li  
**Project:** https://lzhangbj.github.io/projects/unitemp/

## TL;DR
UniTemp is the first unified autoregressive distillation framework that trains a single video generation model supporting generation conditioned on past, future, or both temporal contexts — enabling bidirectional extension, inbetween generation, scene transitions, looping video, and visual story generation at real-time speed, while maintaining competitive quality with forward-only methods.

## Problem
All major autoregressive video diffusion models (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, Wan, Cosmos) support only forward (causal) generation, but real creative workflows require backward extension (generating a prologue for an existing clip), inbetween generation, and looping. The technical root cause: Causal 3D VAEs encode latents conditioned strictly on past context, causing severe inter-block discontinuities and flickering when generation proceeds backward.

## Key Contributions
- **UniTemp**: a bidirectional distillation framework yielding a single model for any-direction video generation with fast KV-cache inference
- **Blockwise anchor latents**: auxiliary latents that restore missing past context at block boundaries during backward generation, eliminating flickering artifacts without modifying the pre-trained VAE or teacher model
- **Five new generation modes** beyond forward: bidirectional video extension, inbetween generation, looping video, scene transition, and visual story generation — all in one model
- Training efficiency: distills from a frozen teacher (e.g., Wan2.1) without requiring real video data, sharing model weights across forward and backward tasks

## Method
Built on the Self-Forcing framework (asymmetric distillation from a multi-step teacher into a few-step autoregressive student). For backward generation, blockwise anchor latents — small sets of auxiliary latents representing preceding video tokens — are jointly denoised with the target block under full-sequence attention, providing a proxy for prior local context even when generating in reverse. At inference, temporal RoPE position indices specify conditioning positions (past, future, or both); KV caching avoids redundant computation. A unified training objective supervises both directions simultaneously via shared model parameters.

## Why It Matters
Solves a fundamental architectural limitation shared by all state-of-the-art video generation models that use Causal 3D VAEs. The anchor latent mechanism is lightweight and non-invasive — no VAE retraining, no teacher model changes — making it directly applicable to any existing distillation pipeline. Practically democratizes the full range of creative video workflows (story generation, loop creation, scene bridging) in a single efficient model, which is critical for agentic video creation pipelines like CHIEF or SCPE that need flexible temporal control over generated content.

### 3. Bridging Creative Intent and Visual Quality: Creator-Driven Recurrent Video Generation with Agentic Feedback Loops
**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2606.18591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18591)  
**Authors:** Denis Savytski, Aiden Lei, Heding Liu, Warren Yang, Sihan Liang, Alexander Liu, Zhe Zhao  
*(ICML 2026 Workshop on Human-AI Co-Creativity)*

## TL;DR
CHIEF (Creator-driven Hybrid Iterative Evaluation Framework) is a multi-agent agentic video generation system where persona-conditioned LLM agents simulate diverse audience perspectives and drive iterative video refinement — enabling non-expert students to produce a 10-minute film rated 4.1/5 by a live audience vs. 2.4/5 for the unrefined AI baseline.

## Problem
AI-generated videos lack narrative coherence and creative direction, problems that compound at longer durations. Unlike code generation where tests provide automatic feedback enabling self-improvement, video generation requires subjective assessment of plot, character, and narrative. Existing autonomous feedback loops use reward models capturing aggregated preferences rather than the diverse real viewer sentiment needed for creative storytelling.

## Key Contributions
- **CHIEF framework**: human-in-the-loop iterative video generation placing the creator at the center while automating subjective audience feedback
- **Persona-conditioned Feedback Agents**: multimodal LLM agents instantiated with auto-generated viewer personas (from YouTube comment histories for audience agents, Rotten Tomatoes reviews for critic agents), watching videos and providing category-specific per-keyframe and per-clip critique in parallel
- **Feedback Translator**: extracts structured issue tuples (description, category: narrative/pacing/character/visual/technical, urgency: low/medium/high), presents ranked summaries to creator, and converts the creator's plain-text revisions into prompt updates for the next iteration
- Validated with non-expert high school and college students producing 1-minute and 10-minute films

## Method
Modular agentic pipeline with three components: (1) Video Generator — a Prompt Orchestrator maintains consistent environment/character anchors across clips, generates keyframes via Imagen 4.0, interpolates clips with Veo 3.1, synthesizes music via ElevenLabs Music v1; (2) Feedback Agents — each persona is generated from 30 user comments via self-improving LLM critique, then instantiated as a Gemini 2.5 Flash agent that watches video and critiques from that persona's perspective; (3) Feedback Translator — decomposes raw feedback into structured tuples, aggregates by category with urgency ranking, and refines creator's natural-language revisions into prompt updates. The loop iterates until the creator is satisfied; selective clip regeneration avoids reprocessing unchanged segments.

## Why It Matters
Demonstrates that agentic multi-role feedback systems can substitute for human viewer panels in creative content evaluation, enabling rapid iteration without crowdsourcing. The persona-conditioned simulation approach captures the diversity of audience reactions that single reward models miss. Directly relevant to agentic AI research (area 1): the architecture — specialized LLM agents with defined roles, iterative self-improvement from failure analysis, human-AI co-agency — is a reusable pattern for any creative generative task. The 10-minute film case study shows that non-experts can leverage agentic AI systems to produce coherent long-form creative content.
