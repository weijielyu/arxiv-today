### 1. FLAT: Feedforward Latent Triangle Splatting for Geometrically Accurate Scene Generation
**Score: 83/100** · [arXiv:2606.24876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24876)
**Authors:** Orest Kupyn, Goutam Bhat, Philipp Henzler, Fabian Manhardt, Christian Rupprecht, Federico Tombari

## TL;DR
FLAT decodes surface-aligned triangle splats directly from frozen video diffusion model latents in a single feedforward pass, achieving substantially better geometric accuracy than 3DGS-based feedforward baselines while remaining competitive in visual quality — and producing geometry that works natively in game engines and simulation pipelines.

## Problem
Feedforward 3D scene generators decode volumetric 3D Gaussians from video diffusion latents, but Gaussians are semi-transparent volumetric blobs poorly suited to standard graphics pipelines (game engines, physics simulators) that require opaque surface representations like triangles or meshes. Converting Gaussians to meshes post-hoc is lossy and complex. Directly regressing flat triangle primitives is much harder: incorrectly oriented triangles contribute negligibly to rendered images, causing vanishing gradients and training instability.

## Key Contributions
1. **Triangle splatting from diffusion latents**: First demonstration that semi-opaque triangle splats can be decoded directly from video diffusion latent space in a single feedforward pass, producing geometry usable in standard graphics pipelines.
2. **Ray-centered rotation parameterization**: Stable primitive parameterization using a Cholesky-style shape transform and residual rotations around a ray-aligned frame, avoiding degenerate triangles that plagued prior attempts at feedforward flat-primitive prediction.
3. **Product window function**: Novel differentiable rendering function that improves gradient flow across triangle boundaries during training, resolving the core training instability of flat-primitive regression.
4. **Lightweight mesh conversion**: Optional refinement step converts the semi-transparent triangle soup into fully opaque, game-engine-ready representations supporting real-time rendering.
5. **Systematic representation comparison**: First fair evaluation of 3DGS, 2DGS, and triangle splatting under identical training conditions, providing clear tradeoff analysis for representation choice in feedforward scene generation.

## Method
FLAT builds a lightweight feedforward decoder on top of a frozen video diffusion model that already implicitly encodes multi-view geometric structure in its latent space. Given a single input image: (1) the frozen video diffusion backbone produces latents encoding the scene from multiple implicit viewpoints; (2) a per-token decoder predicts triangle splat parameters — each token emits a ray-centered triangle defined by a constrained Cholesky-style shape transform, residual rotations around a ray-aligned frame, and color; (3) differentiable rendering with the new product window function supervises training via photometric, depth, and normal losses; (4) an optional lightweight refinement step converts the resulting semi-transparent triangle soup into a fully opaque representation for game engines. The model is trained once, not optimized per scene.

## Why It Matters
3D content generation that directly outputs game-engine-compatible geometry — rather than requiring complex post-hoc Gaussian-to-mesh conversion — is a critical step toward deploying generative 3D models in simulation, robotics, AR/VR, and game asset pipelines. FLAT shows that video diffusion priors are rich enough to predict accurate surface geometry, not just novel views. The systematic comparison of Gaussian vs. flat-primitive representations under fair conditions is also a useful empirical reference for future work on feedforward 3D generation.

### 2. Sol Video Inference Engine: Agent-Native Full-Stack Acceleration Framework for Efficient Video Generation
**Score: 83/100** · [arXiv:2606.23743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23743)
**Authors:** Yitong Li, Junsong Chen, Haopeng Li, Haozhe Liu, Jincheng Yu, Ligeng Zhu, Ping Luo, Song Han, Enze Xie

## TL;DR
Sol Video Inference Engine uses parallel LLM-based skill agents and an integrator agent to automatically discover instance-optimal acceleration stacks for video diffusion models, achieving >2× speedup with near-lossless quality across three diverse video generation models (64B, 22B, 2B) without any manual per-deployment engineering.

## Problem
Accelerating video diffusion inference (via caching, sparse attention, quantization, token pruning, kernel fusion) is inherently instance-specific: the optimal recipe for a given (model, hardware, resolution, duration) combination does not transfer to other combinations. The search space over these factors is enormous, making manual performance engineering expensive, slow, and non-scalable as video models proliferate.

## Key Contributions
1. **Problem reformulation**: Casts video diffusion acceleration as instance-specific tuning rather than one-time design, recognizing that deployment heterogeneity (model architecture, hardware, inference config) makes global recipes ineffective.
2. **Full-stack acceleration framework**: Integrates five complementary techniques — cross-step caching, sparse attention, token pruning, quantization, and kernel fusion — in a unified, composable framework applicable across diverse architectures.
3. **Agentic optimization workflow**: Parallel "skill agents" independently optimize each acceleration technique axis for the target deployment; an "agent integrator" composes per-technique proposals via global search into a coherent stack; a human validator provides quality feedback to drive iteration.
4. **Demonstrated >2× speedup**: Validated on three architecturally diverse video models (64B Cosmos3-Super, 22B LTX-2.3, 2B SANA-Video) with near-lossless VBench quality scores, with little human engineering effort.

## Method
Sol Video Inference Engine operates as follows: (1) Given a deployment target (model + hardware + serving config), the system launches parallel skill agents — each an LLM equipped with code-execution tools — that independently search and optimize one acceleration technique (e.g., one agent tunes cache policies, another tunes attention sparsity patterns); (2) An agent integrator receives all per-technique proposals and performs global search to compose them into a coherent combined acceleration stack, resolving technique interactions; (3) A human validator checks visual output quality and provides feedback; (4) The integrator refines the stack based on validator feedback. This agent-human loop replaces the multi-team manual engineering previously required. The approach is training-free and model-agnostic.

## Why It Matters
This paper is a concrete demonstration of agentic AI applied to AI infrastructure: rather than humans manually engineering deployment optimizations for each new video model × hardware combination, a multi-agent system does it automatically. As video generation models scale (64B+ parameters) and deployment targets diversify, this agentic approach to inference optimization becomes essential. It is also a compelling instance of the "AI scientist" / automated research paradigm applied to systems engineering — the agents autonomously explore a large configuration space and synthesize solutions that humans would take weeks to find.

### 3. DramaDirector: Geometry-Guided Short Drama Generation
**Score: 80/100** · [arXiv:2606.24107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24107)
**Authors:** Hengji Zhou, Sijie Liu, Jianrun Chen, Xingchen Zou, Lianghao Xia, Liqiang Nie

## TL;DR
DramaDirector trains an LLM planner via GRPO with a geometry-aware retrieval reward to generate coherent multi-shot short drama videos, grounding each shot's composition in depth-pose references borrowed from a gallery of real drama shots rather than relying on underspecified text descriptions.

## Problem
Generating plot-aligned short drama videos requires precise cinematographic geometry (shot grammar, spatial layouts, dialogue focus) that LLM storyboards cannot adequately convey in text — downstream video generators receive ambiguous spatial cues, leading to inconsistent composition and poor faithfulness to the intended cinematic structure. Standard text-video alignment rewards cannot recover geometric information that was never in the text.

## Key Contributions
1. **Geometry-grounded storyboard planning**: Decouples each shot's storyboard into static visual conditions (depth-pose references retrieved from a real-shot gallery) and dynamic narrative conditions, providing concrete geometry anchors for video generation without requiring the LLM to verbalize spatial structure.
2. **GRPO with geometric retrieval reward**: Trains the LLM planner using GRPO, with geometry-aware text-visual alignment similarity as the reward signal, optimizing the planner to generate storyboards that have real cinematographic counterparts in the gallery.
3. **Multi-task SFT warm-up**: Adapts the planner on real short-drama storyboards via multi-task supervised finetuning to instill short-drama shot grammar before RL training.
4. **DramaBoard benchmark**: New benchmark from 35 live-action dramas, 2.8K episodes, and 81K annotated shots with structured storyboards and multi-dimensional evaluation protocols (faithfulness, consistency, controllability).

## Method
DramaDirector pipeline: (1) A real-shot gallery pre-indexes short-drama shots by depth maps and pose skeletons, capturing composition without appearance; (2) Given a plot, the LLM planner generates a structured storyboard that retrieves a depth-pose reference per shot from the gallery — this is the static visual condition; (3) First-frame generation is conditioned on the retrieved depth-pose reference; (4) An image-to-video model animates each first frame using the dynamic narrative condition (dialogue, action); (5) The planner is trained in two stages: multi-task SFT on real storyboards to learn short-drama conventions, then GRPO finetuning with geometry-aware retrieval similarity as the reward, pushing the planner to write retrievable — hence geometrically grounded — storyboards.

## Why It Matters
DramaDirector demonstrates a principled solution to the "planning-to-generation gap" in long-form video generation: rather than expecting text alone to convey geometric composition, it grounds the planner in a retrieval corpus of real visual geometry. The GRPO-based planner training with a visual retrieval reward is a reusable recipe for any agentic generation system where the planner must output specifications that downstream generators can faithfully execute. The benchmark also advances evaluation standards for narrative video generation.
