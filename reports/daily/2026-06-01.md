### 1. SlotMemory: Object-Centric KV Memory for Streaming Long-Video Generation
**Score: 86/100** · [arXiv:2605.31033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31033)

**Authors:** Weijia Dou, Hui Li, Jiahao Cui, Lei Zhou, Jingdong Wang, Siyu Zhu

## TL;DR
SlotMemory replaces temporal token sequences with semantic object-centric slots as the memory primitive in streaming video diffusion, shifting from "when did this happen" to "what is being represented." On 60-second interactive multi-prompt narratives it achieves a state-of-the-art quality score of 81.61 and a 22.8% relative improvement in dynamic consistency over the strongest existing streaming baseline.

## Problem
Streaming video generation models use temporal-centric memory (raw frames, chunk segments, unclustered tokens), causing identity drift and semantic inconsistency when characters exit and re-enter the frame or during multi-prompt transitions. The model cannot distinguish "ephemeral motion context to discard" from "persistent semantic entities to preserve," leading to catastrophic narrative degradation over 60-second rollouts.

## Key Contributions
1. **Object-centric KV memory**: decomposes the transformer's KV manifold into discrete, reusable semantic slots via temporally-initialized Slot Attention, enabling entity-level persistence across long video horizons independent of temporal position.
2. **Prompt-aware retrieval and eviction**: a scoring function balancing textual alignment vs. visual relevance determines which slots to retain or evict across prompt transitions, supporting coherent multi-narrative generation within fixed compute.
3. **Recursive read-write-update cycle**: integrates slot-conditioned KV writing, structured retrieval, and slot regularization directly into the diffusion transformer's attention stack, achieving 22.8% improvement in dynamic consistency on 60-second benchmarks.

## Method
SlotMemory integrates slot-based representation learning into a streaming video diffusion transformer (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B backbone). During memory writing, temporally-initialized Slot Attention partitions the transformer KV manifold into discrete semantic regions. These slots serve as routing addresses that index original, high-fidelity KV tokens—a hybrid approach preserving diffusion quality while providing structured entity-level retrieval. A prompt-aware scoring function calibrates textual-visual alignment to decide which slots to retain or evict, enabling coherent prompt transitions. The entire mechanism is embedded directly into the attention stack, making it architecture-compatible with any diffusion transformer.

## Why It Matters
Long-form consistent video generation is a key frontier for world models and interactive video. SlotMemory's insight—that semantic structure, not temporal capacity, is the key primitive for persistent video synthesis—is both principled and empirically validated. The SOTA 81.61 quality score and 22.8% dynamic consistency improvement on 60-second narratives set a strong new baseline; the GitHub release makes it immediately reproducible.

### 2. Lumos-Nexus: Efficient Frequency Bridging with Homogeneous Latent Space for Video Unified Models
**Score: 84/100** · [arXiv:2605.31603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31603)

**Authors:** Jiazheng Xing, Hangjie Yuan, Lingling Cai, Xinyu Liu, Yujie Wei, Fei Du, Hai Ci, Tao Feng, Jiasheng Tang, Weihua Chen, Fan Wang, Yong Liu

## TL;DR
Lumos-Nexus decouples reasoning alignment cost from visual quality cost in video unified models: a lightweight generator trains with the understanding block, then at inference time a novel Unified Progressive Frequency Bridging (UPFB) module progressively hands off to a large pretrained generator in the shared latent space, achieving strong reasoning-driven generation with high visual fidelity without expensive end-to-end training of the large model.

## Problem
Integrating large high-fidelity video generators (e.g., Wan2.1-14B) into unified training loops is computationally prohibitive, forcing connector-based video unified models to choose between reasoning quality and visual fidelity—neither the small nor the large generator alone satisfies both constraints.

## Key Contributions
1. **Two-stage train-then-bridge design**: trains only a lightweight generator with the understanding block; exploits a homogeneous latent space (same VAE between small and large generators) to enable inference-time transfer to the large model without re-training.
2. **Unified Progressive Frequency Bridging (UPFB)**: at inference, progressively hands off generation from the small generator (high-level semantic priors) to the large generator (low-frequency visual detail) using RMS alignment and energy rebalancing across the shared latent frequency space.
3. **VR-Bench**: a new benchmark for reasoning-driven video generation spanning 9 categories across physical world reasoning, commonsense reasoning, and embodied physical reasoning, filling a gap in existing evaluation suites.

## Method
Lumos-Nexus is a connector-based unified model where the understanding block (multimodal LLM) connects via a lightweight alignment connector to a small diffusion generator that is trained jointly. At inference, UPFB transfers generation to a large pretrained generator by progressively bridging the frequency characteristics—the small generator initializes high-frequency semantic structure from reasoning, and the large generator refines low-frequency visual detail. RMS normalization and energy rebalancing align the frequency statistics between generators in the shared VAE latent space, preventing distributional mismatch.

## Why It Matters
The train-small, infer-large strategy is a practically compelling approach to the cost barrier in video unified models. By treating the small generator as a semantic initiator and the large generator as a fidelity enhancer, Lumos-Nexus achieves substantial VBench gains and strong VR-Bench performance. The VR-Bench benchmark itself is a useful contribution to the community for evaluating reasoning-grounded generation.

### 3. CameraNoise: Enabling Faithful Camera Control in Video Diffusion through Geometry-Flow-Guided Noise Warping
**Score: 84/100** · [arXiv:2605.30774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30774)

**Authors:** Haoyu Zhao, Jiaxi Gu, Haoran Chen, Qingping Zheng, Yeying Jin, Hongyi Yang, Junqi Cheng, Yuang Zhang, Zenghui Lu, Huan Yu, Jie Jiang, Peng Shu, Zuxuan Wu, Yu-Gang Jiang

*Accepted at ICML 2026.*

## TL;DR
CameraNoise embeds camera trajectory directly into video diffusion's noise space via an appearance-agnostic Geometry-guided Reprojection Flow (GRFlow), establishing a proven one-to-one mapping between camera poses and the noise field, and significantly outperforming prior activation-injection camera control methods in visual quality and trajectory faithfulness.

## Problem
Existing camera-controllable video diffusion methods inject numerical pose parameters (Plücker embeddings, linear features) into diffusion activations, failing to faithfully represent subtle trajectory differences and causing structural distortions—especially under new viewpoints and speed variations. Optical-flow-based noise warping entangles scene appearance with motion, conflicting with text conditioning.

## Key Contributions
1. **Geometry-guided Reprojection Flow (GRFlow)**: an appearance-agnostic flow representation computed purely from camera parameters (independent of object motion or scene texture), with a proven one-to-one mapping between camera pose, GRFlow, and CameraNoise.
2. **CameraNoise**: warped Gaussian noise constructed from GRFlow, formulated as a PDE solved via bipartite graph, that encodes camera motion into the full diffusion noise space—persisting throughout the entire denoising process rather than only at intermediate activations.
3. **Dynamic extrinsic perturbation during training** for inference robustness, decoupling camera conditioning from scene appearance to avoid the semantic conflicts common in optical-flow-based approaches.

## Method
GRFlow projects per-frame camera parameters onto a grid plane and applies Lie algebra optimization to reduce jitter from pose estimation errors. The noise warping is formulated as a partial differential equation and solved via bipartite graph, establishing a one-to-one mapping (camera pose → GRFlow → CameraNoise). CameraNoise is combined with standard Gaussian noise and injected into the diffusion backbone, conditioning generation on camera motion at every denoising step. Dynamic perturbation of camera extrinsics during training improves generalization to novel trajectories.

## Why It Matters
Camera control is essential for cinematic video generation, virtual environments, and robotics simulation. Placing camera conditioning in the noise space (rather than activations) is a principled approach that out-competes MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, and other baselines in both trajectory accuracy and visual quality. Acceptance at ICML 2026 signals community validation of the approach.

### 4. RayDer: Scalable Self-Supervised Novel View Synthesis from Real-World Video
**Score: 83/100** · [arXiv:2605.31535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31535)

**Authors:** Ulrich Prestel, Stefan Andreas Baumann, Nick Stracke, Björn Ommer

## TL;DR
RayDer unifies camera estimation, scene reconstruction, and view rendering into a single self-supervised feed-forward transformer trained on unconstrained real-world video (including dynamic content), demonstrating clean power-law scaling with data and compute across three orders of magnitude and achieving zero-shot performance competitive with supervised approaches.

## Problem
Self-supervised NVS cannot scale reliably: multi-network pipelines make capacity allocation unpredictable and expensive; existing methods require static-scene data that is far less abundant than unconstrained real-world video; and dynamic video content destabilizes training, blocking access to the dominant and scalable form of visual data.

## Key Contributions
1. **Single-backbone unification**: consolidates camera estimation, scene reconstruction, and rendering into one feed-forward transformer, replacing multi-network pipelines to enable predictable and efficient scaling with no inter-network coordination overhead.
2. **Dynamic state nuisance variable**: a minimal learned variable absorbs time-varying scene content during training and is discarded at inference, enabling stable training on unconstrained real-world video without changing the static-scene NVS task.
3. **Empirical scaling laws**: demonstrates clean power-law scaling across 3 orders of magnitude of training data and 4 model sizes, with compute-optimal Pareto frontiers and outperformance of static-scene data mixtures.

## Method
Building on the RayZer lineage, RayDer uses a single transformer backbone for all NVS sub-tasks. Autoregressive pose learning and parallel-target attention improve camera geometry estimation. Local high-resolution layers enhance synthesis quality. A learned dynamic state variable absorbs dynamic scene content (people, cars, etc.) during training as a nuisance—it is never used at inference and does not reconstruct 4D content. Training on diverse unposed real-world video provides scalable supervision; pose accuracy itself scales predictably with data, model size, and compute.

## Why It Matters
Demonstrating that scalable self-supervised NVS requires better system design rather than more curated data is a foundational result. Unlocking unconstrained internet video as training data—the same data scaling recipe that drove language model breakthroughs—could similarly transform 3D understanding. The power-law evidence is especially compelling for the field's trajectory.

### 5. Crafter: A Multi-Agent Harness for Editable Scientific Figure Generation from Diverse Inputs
**Score: 83/100** · [arXiv:2605.30611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30611)

**Authors:** Haozhe Zhao, Shuzheng Si, Zhenhailong Wang, Zheng Wang, Liang Chen, Xiaotong Li, Zhixiang Liang, Maosong Sun, Minjia Zhang

## TL;DR
Crafter is a multi-agent harness for scientific figure generation that uses an evolving structured specification as persistent pipeline memory, enabling targeted correction of localized generation failures (garbled labels, misaligned connectors) across diverse figure types and input modalities, and further converts raster outputs into locally editable SVGs via the companion CraftEditor system.

## Problem
AI-generated scientific figures suffer from localized structural errors that neither stronger generators nor prompt rephrasing can fix: each generation attempt produces a different constellation of failures, and accumulating free-text corrections introduces contradictions. Existing systems narrow scope (text-only, single figure type) and produce non-editable raster outputs.

## Key Contributions
1. **Harness abstraction with structured specification memory**: five cooperating agents (intent reasoner, plan generator, image-generation backend, critic, specification refiner, convergence judge) share an evolving figure specification S as structured memory, enabling typed targeted corrections to individual failure points without conflicting natural-language patch accumulation.
2. **Diversity-driven plan exploration + verify-then-refine loop**: K candidate figure framings generated in parallel avoid premature commitment; critic emits directive diagnostics; convergence judge routes each round to accept, refine, or revert.
3. **CraftEditor + CraftBench**: companion raster-to-vector system converting figure outputs to locally editable SVGs via iterative SVG assembly; CraftBench benchmark spans 3 figure types and 4 input conditions with human quality annotation.

## Method
Crafter's intent reasoner seeds a structured figure specification S₀ from context and documentation. A plan generator proposes K candidate plans; an image-generation backend renders each; a critic emits directive diagnostics on specific components (labels, layout, arrows); a specification refiner writes typed edits to S; a convergence judge routes the round. CraftEditor applies the same harness pattern to raster-to-vector conversion via instruction-driven canvas cleaning followed by iterative SVG assembly with a judge ensemble ensuring structural fidelity.

## Why It Matters
Scientific figure generation is a high-value step in automated research pipelines—and a genuine bottleneck for the vision of AI-assisted paper writing. The harness approach addresses the root cause of AI figure generation failures (localized structured errors in compositions) rather than just scaling the backbone. The CraftEditor editable SVG output and CraftBench benchmark provide infrastructure for the community, and the multi-agent design generalizes to any structured visual composition task. Directly relevant to agentic AI research (priority area 1).
