## Top Picks (Score ≥ 85)

### 1. Gamma-World: Generative Multi-Agent World Modeling Beyond Two Players

**Score: 88/100** · [arXiv:2605.28816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28816)

**Authors:** Fangfu Liu, Kai He, Tianchang Shen, Tianshi Cao, Sanja Fidler, Yueqi Duan, Jun Gao, Igor Gilitschenski, Zian Wang, Xuanchi Ren (NVIDIA / Tsinghua University / University of Toronto / Vector Institute)

## TL;DR
γ-World is a generative multi-agent world model that scales video diffusion transformers to jointly simulate multiple independently-controllable, permutation-symmetric agents in shared interactive environments, enabling real-time 24 FPS multi-agent rollouts with zero-shot generalization from 2 to 4 players.

## Problem
Existing video world models are fundamentally single-agent simulators — they generate future observations conditioned on one action stream. Scaling to multi-agent settings introduces three structural challenges: (1) agents in a shared world are intrinsically permutation-equivalent, yet learned per-slot identities break this symmetry; (2) dense all-to-all cross-agent attention scales quadratically in the number of agents, becoming prohibitive beyond two players; (3) bidirectional diffusion transformers cannot stream rollouts in real time. No prior work addressed all three simultaneously.

## Key Contributions
1. **Simplex Rotary Agent Encoding**: A parameter-free extension of 3D RoPE that represents each agent as a vertex of a regular simplex in rotary angle space. All agents are exactly equidistant from each other (permutation-symmetric) while each retains a distinct rotary phase — enabling scalable agent identity without learned per-slot embeddings and supporting zero-shot generalization to unseen agent counts without retraining.
2. **Sparse Hub Attention**: Learnable hub tokens (K=8 per latent frame) mediate cross-agent communication via a two-hop agent→hub→agent path, reducing cross-agent attention cost from O(P²) to O(P) in the number of agents without sacrificing shared-world consistency.
3. **Causal Self-Forcing Distillation for Real-Time Rollout**: A bidirectional teacher is distilled into a block-causal student with KV caching, enabling action-responsive multi-agent video generation at 24 FPS — making the model viable as an interactive simulator.

## Method
γ-World is built on Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B (2B parameter video DiT with hidden dimension D=2048, 28 transformer blocks). The standard 3D RoPE coordinate is extended with a 4th agent axis: head dimensions are partitioned as (t, p, h, w) = (64, 32, 16, 16). Agent identities are assigned to simplex vertices from a fixed pool of size V=4 (supporting up to 4 agents), with random vertex assignment and slot permutation during training to prevent slot-specific overfitting. Per-agent actions (25 discrete/continuous fields for game, 10 continuous for robot) are encoded by a shared action encoder injected as per-block additive biases to transformer attention inputs. Sparse Hub Attention replaces dense cross-agent attention in the causal student, keeping each agent stream attending only to its own tokens and the K hub tokens. Training is a 3-stage process: (1) bidirectional teacher pretraining on 2-agent gameplay clips, (2) causal student pretraining with block-causal attention masks, (3) Self-Forcing distillation coupling a trainable fake score with a frozen teacher real score for single-step temporal block generation.

## Results
Evaluated on multiplayer Minecraft-style game environments (2 and 4 players, movement/mining/combat/building scenarios). The distilled γ-World achieves FVD=239.7, FID=30.9, LPIPS=0.273, PSNR=26.8, SSIM=0.811 — outperforming slot-based baselines (Solaris) and dense-attention variants on video fidelity, action controllability, and inter-agent consistency. The bidirectional teacher scores FVD=227.3 with near-identical FID/LPIPS. Ablations confirm both Simplex Rotary Agent Encoding and Sparse Hub Attention contribute independently. Critically, the model trained on 2 players generalizes to 4 players without any fine-tuning, validating the permutation-symmetric simplex design.

## Why It Matters
Multi-agent world models are a prerequisite for multiplayer game generation, multi-robot coordination, and building embodied AI research environments at scale. γ-World's permutation-symmetric agent representation and linear-cost hub attention directly resolve the structural bottlenecks preventing single-agent world models from scaling, establishing a replicable design recipe. The demonstration of zero-shot agent-count generalization — from 2 to 4 players — is particularly significant: it suggests a path toward unified world model checkpoints that scale to arbitrary agent counts, highly relevant to the broader agentic AI research agenda at the intersection of video generation and multi-agent systems.
