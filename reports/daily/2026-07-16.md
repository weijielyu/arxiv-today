### 1. VideoRAE: Taming Video Foundation Models for Generative Modeling via Representation Autoencoders

**Score: 85/100** · [2607.14088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14088) · [Project Page](https://zhxie0117.github.io/VideoRAE)

**Authors:** Zhihao Xie, Junfeng Wu, Xinting Hu, Junchao Huang, Li Jiang

## TL;DR
VideoRAE repurposes frozen Video Foundation Model features (V-JEPA 2, VideoMAEv2) as compact, semantically rich video latents — replacing the pixel-driven 3D-VAE bottleneck in video generation — and converges ~5× faster than standard autoencoder baselines while supporting both diffusion and autoregressive generation from a single shared latent space.

## Problem
Standard video autoencoders (3D-VAEs) optimize for pixel-level reconstruction via MSE and adversarial losses, which biases their latent spaces toward local appearance rather than high-level semantics or spatio-temporal dynamics. Downstream video generation models must then re-learn these dynamics from scratch, increasing training difficulty and data requirements. Meanwhile, powerful Video Foundation Models capture rich spatiotemporal structure but have not been adapted for generation tasks.

## Key Contributions
1. **VideoRAE architecture**: A frozen VFM backbone (no gradient updates) feeds multi-scale hierarchical features into a lightweight 1D self-attention projector that compresses them into highly compact generation-friendly latents.
2. **Unified continuous + discrete latents**: Supports Diffusion Transformers (continuous latents) and autoregressive models (discrete tokens via multi-codebook high-dimensional quantization) from the same representation.
3. **Local-and-global representation alignment objective**: During decoding, a frozen VFM teacher provides both local patch-level and global sequence-level alignment targets — improving semantic preservation and enabling training without KL regularization.

## Method
A frozen video foundation encoder extracts multi-scale features from input video clips. A lightweight 1D self-attention projector compresses these into compact latents. The decoder is supervised by both pixel-level reconstruction losses and the local-and-global VFM alignment objective against the frozen teacher. For discrete generation, multi-codebook high-dimensional quantization converts the latents into tokens. On UCF-101 class-to-video generation, VideoRAE achieves state-of-the-art gFVDs of 40 (AR) and 93 (DiT). In a controlled 2B-scale text-to-video study, replacing LTX-VAE with VideoRAE yields faster convergence and better VBench scores under comparable settings.

## Why It Matters
The core insight — that frozen VFM representations are already suitable as compact, generation-friendly video latents — inverts the usual assumption that autoencoders need to be trained from pixel supervision. If this direction holds at scale, it could substantially reduce the compute required to train high-quality video generation models by eliminating the semantic learning burden from the autoencoder stage.

### 2. Exploratory, Communicative, and Deployable: Vision-Driven Embodied Agents for Open-World Mobile Manipulation

**Score: 82/100** · [2607.13653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13653) · [Code](https://github.com/InternRobotics/REAL)

**Authors:** Boyu Mi, Mengchen Ma, Yifei Yao, Xing Gao, Junting Chen, Yangzi Li, Zihou Zhu, Guohao Li, Zhenfei Yin, Tai Wang, Yao Mu, Jiangmiao Pang, Hanqing Wang

## TL;DR
REAL is an agentic framework for open-world mobile manipulation that — without privileged simulator states — achieves 56.9% success on interactive disambiguation tasks (surpassing leading closed-source VLMs) and 78.3% end-to-end success across 60 real-world episodes on a dual-arm robot, demonstrating robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem
Existing embodied agent frameworks sidestep real deployment challenges by relying on privileged simulator states or assuming fully specified instructions. Real-world deployment demands active exploration of unknown environments, visual grounding of language to objects, and interactive disambiguation of underspecified user intents — none of which existing benchmarks stress-test adequately.

## Key Contributions
1. **Sim-to-real-consistent APIs**: Environment interfaces that work without oracle perception, eliminating the common simulation shortcut and enabling realistic training.
2. **REAL-Bench**: A benchmark of 241 tasks spanning active exploration, visual distraction, articulated manipulation, and interactive disambiguation — specifically designed to stress-test real-world deployment challenges.
3. **Hierarchical training pipeline (SFT → online RL)**: Aligns tool-use capabilities while preserving open-vocabulary reasoning; achieves 56.9% on interactive tasks (outperforming GPT-4o-level VLMs) and 78.3% real-world success across 60 zero-shot trials in unseen household scenarios.

## Method
REAL establishes simulation environments with sim-to-real-consistent APIs (no oracle perception) and a simulated user model for human-in-the-loop interaction during training. Task composition drives data collection for supervised fine-tuning, after which online reinforcement learning further optimizes agent performance. The hierarchical training pipeline learns to use tools (visual search, manipulation primitives) while maintaining open-vocabulary reasoning under extended exploration horizons. Physical deployment on a dual-arm mobile robot validates zero-shot transfer.

## Why It Matters
Long-horizon mobile manipulation with genuine open-world exploration and interactive disambiguation represents a hard frontier for embodied AI. REAL's strong zero-shot real-world transfer — enabled by sim-to-real-consistent design rather than privileged shortcuts — is a meaningful step toward agents that can actually be deployed in homes and workplaces, relevant to the broader agentic AI research agenda.

### 3. Boogu-Image-0.1: Boosting Open-Source Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation

**Score: 80/100** · [2607.13125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13125) · [Code](https://github.com/Boogu-Project/Boogu-Image)

**Authors:** Guoxuan Chen, Chufeng Xiao, Haoran Yang, Siyue Xie, Binxiao Huang, Ming Zhang, Cheuk Him Chau, Xinyu Fu, Yingzhao Lian, Tom S. Y. Li et al.

## TL;DR
Boogu-Image-0.1 is a competitive open-source model family (Base/Turbo/Edit/Edit-Turbo) for unified image understanding and generation that — through targeted improvements in model understanding, data quality, and agentic inference-time scaling — matches or surpasses other open-source models and approaches leading closed-source systems at only ~$400K theoretical training cost on 208M images.

## Problem
Closed-source systems (GPT-Image-2, Nano-Banana-Pro) achieve strong unified generation + editing performance through opaque system-level integration. The open-source ecosystem lacks a competitive unified model covering text-to-image generation, instruction-based editing, fast inference, and multilingual text rendering at comparable quality levels — limiting reproducibility and community progress.

## Key Contributions
1. **Four-variant open model family** (Base/Turbo/Edit/Edit-Turbo) released under Apache 2.0 with weights, code, and training recipes — covering high-quality generation, fast inference, instruction-based editing, and bilingual Chinese-English text rendering.
2. **Agentic inference-time scaling**: Demonstrates that scaling inference compute agentically (not just model size) substantially improves generation and editing quality, offering a compute-efficient alternative to massive training runs.
3. **Competitive efficiency**: Trained on only 208.62M unique images at ~$400K theoretical cost — approaching closed-source performance while remaining transparent and reproducible.

## Method
Boogu-Image-0.1 builds on improvements in the base VLM understanding capabilities, data curation and quality filtering, and training pipeline design for joint understanding + generation. Agentic inference-time scaling is applied at generation and editing time to boost quality without retraining. Evaluated comprehensively across standard benchmarks on all four model variants.

## Why It Matters
Releasing competitive unified image understanding + generation models with transparent training recipes reduces the research community's dependence on closed-source APIs for building upon state-of-the-art image generation. The finding that agentic inference-time scaling can close the gap to closed-source systems at modest training cost is directly relevant to efficient scaling strategies for image generation research.
