### 1. AAD-1: Asymmetric Adversarial Distillation for One-Step Autoregressive Video Generation

**Score: 88/100** · [arXiv:2606.03972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03972) · [Project](https://aad-1.github.io/)

**Authors:** Haobo Li, Yanhong Zeng, Yunhong Lu, Jiapeng Zhu, Hao Ouyang, Qiuyu Wang, Ka Leong Cheng, Yujun Shen, Zhipeng Zhang

## TL;DR
AAD-1 achieves state-of-the-art one-step autoregressive image-to-video generation by breaking the symmetry between generator and discriminator: a bidirectional discriminator with holistic video-level scoring detects global motion collapse that a causal discriminator misses, paired with a phased training schedule that bootstraps stability before adversarial refinement.

## Problem
Adversarial distillation for one-step autoregressive video generation suffers from two critical failures: (1) symmetric causal discriminators only see past context and cannot detect accumulated temporal drift, causing motion collapse where videos freeze at the initial frame; (2) training from scratch in a self-rollout setting is unstable because early one-step predictions lie far from the data manifold, compounding errors over time.

## Key Contributions
- **Asymmetric architecture**: the generator stays causal (preserving autoregressive sampling), while the discriminator uses full bidirectional spatiotemporal attention and produces a single holistic realism score for the entire sequence — enabling detection of long-range drift and motion collapse
- **Phased training**: Stage I (ODE initialization) → Stage II (distribution matching warm-up with DMD to bring student close to teacher manifold) → Stage III (asymmetric adversarial refinement), preventing instability from cold-start adversarial training
- **State-of-the-art VBench performance** in one-step autoregressive video generation, with ablations confirming both bidirectional attention and video-level scoring are essential

## Method
The generator uses a sliding-window causal architecture for autoregressive streaming, matching the structure of multi-step teacher models. The discriminator, by contrast, has full bidirectional attention over the complete spatiotemporal volume and outputs a single scalar realism score for the whole generated sequence. This asymmetry is critical: the discriminator can compare any frame against both past and future context, catching gradual drift that per-frame or causal-context scoring misses. Training proceeds in three phases: ODE solver initialization for stable starting weights, distribution matching distillation (DMD) as a warm-up to produce on-manifold predictions, and finally adversarial refinement using the asymmetric discriminator. Long-video generation uses a sliding window that extends seamlessly.

## Why It Matters
One-step video generation would reduce inference cost dramatically for streaming and real-time applications (gaming, world models). This paper identifies and fixes the root cause of motion collapse in adversarial video distillation — the discriminator must have access to global temporal context — providing a principled architectural principle for future one-step video generators. The phased training insight is also broadly applicable to any adversarial distillation setting involving sequential models.

### 2. Inference-Time Scaling for Joint Audio-Video Generation

**Score: 84/100** · [arXiv:2606.03183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03183) · [Project](https://jung-jaemin.github.io/ITS-AVGen-Proj)

**Authors:** Jaemin Jung, Kyeongha Rho, Inkyu Shin, Joon Son Chung

## TL;DR
This paper presents the first systematic study of inference-time scaling (ITS) for joint audio-video generation, showing that a multi-verifier framework with Adaptive Reward Weighting (ARW) substantially improves semantic alignment, perceptual quality, and audio-visual synchronization — without any additional model training.

## Problem
Inference-time scaling improves single-modality generation quality without retraining, but extending it to joint audio-video generation is non-trivial: the task requires simultaneously optimizing four heterogeneous objectives (text-video alignment, text-audio alignment, cross-modal semantic consistency, audio-video synchronization). Using a single verifier leads to asymmetric trade-offs and "verifier hacking" — optimizing one dimension at the expense of others.

## Key Contributions
- **Multi-verifier framework**: demonstrates that single-verifier ITS fails for joint AV generation due to reward hacking; identifies an optimal combination of complementary verifiers covering all quality dimensions
- **Adaptive Reward Weighting (ARW)**: a novel test-time optimization algorithm that treats reward aggregation as an online optimization problem, using learnable parameters to calibrate reward variances dynamically without requiring prior knowledge of reward distributions
- First comprehensive empirical study of ITS for multimodal generation on VGGSound and JavisBench-mini, showing significant improvements across all quality dimensions

## Method
Given a frozen joint audio-video generation model, ITS generates multiple candidate samples and ranks/selects them using verifier scores. The key challenge is aggregating scores from heterogeneous verifiers (video quality, audio quality, cross-modal alignment, synchronization metrics). ARW addresses this by framing reward aggregation as an online optimization: learnable aggregation weights are updated across candidates to account for differing reward scales and distributions, avoiding the reward dominance problem where one high-variance verifier overwhelms the selection signal. The system plugs into any existing joint AV generation model as a post-hoc test-time enhancement.

## Why It Matters
As joint audio-video generation models mature, inference-time scaling offers a training-free path to quality improvements proportional to compute budget — critical for high-stakes productions (film, games, VR). The multi-objective reward aggregation challenge identified here is fundamental to any multimodal generation task, and ARW provides a principled framework that should generalize beyond audio-video to other multi-objective settings.

### 3. Cosmos 3: Omnimodal World Models for Physical AI

**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2606.02800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02800)

**Authors:** NVIDIA (Niket Agarwal, Yogesh Balaji, Ting-Chun Wang, and 100+ contributors)

## TL;DR
Cosmos 3 is NVIDIA's flagship omnimodal world model family that jointly processes and generates language, image, video, audio, and action sequences within a unified mixture-of-transformers architecture, establishing a new state-of-the-art across understanding and generation tasks while serving as a general-purpose backbone for physical AI applications.

## Problem
Physical AI systems — autonomous vehicles, robots, embodied agents — require world models that can reason about and generate across the full sensorimotor loop: perceiving visual and audio context, generating predictive rollouts, and conditioning on action sequences. Prior work addresses individual modalities separately, requiring multiple specialized models that cannot share cross-modal context or unify diverse physical AI tasks.

## Key Contributions
- **Unified mixture-of-transformers (MoT) architecture** supporting flexible input-output configurations across language, image, video, audio, and action sequences in a single model
- **Subsumes multiple model families** — vision-language models, video generators, world simulators, and world-action models — into one framework, enabling emergent cross-modal reasoning
- **State-of-the-art** on a diverse suite of understanding and generation benchmarks; post-trained Cosmos 3 models ranked as best open-source Text-to-Image and Image-to-Video models

## Method
Based on the abstract, Cosmos 3 uses a mixture-of-transformers design where modality-specific expert routing handles heterogeneous token types while a shared backbone maintains cross-modal context. The flexible input-output architecture allows any modality combination to condition generation of any other, enabling applications ranging from text-to-video generation to action-conditioned world simulation. The model family is post-trained with RLHF-style feedback to align with human preferences for generation quality.

## Why It Matters
As a NVIDIA flagship release, Cosmos 3 is positioned to become a foundational model for the physical AI ecosystem. The unification of generation and understanding in one omnimodal backbone is significant for researchers working on video generation, embodied agents, or any system requiring multi-modal reasoning. For video generation research specifically, having a model that jointly understands and generates across modalities enables richer conditioning signals and opens new directions for controlled video synthesis.

### 4. Pixel Cube: Diffusion-based Portrait Video Relighting Through Realistic Lighting Reproduction

**Score: 81/100** · [arXiv:2606.02919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02919) · [Project](https://yufanzhang82.github.io/PixelCube/)

**Authors:** Yufan Zhang, Yu Ji, Ayo Ajiboye, Rundi Wu, Yu Guo, Changxi Zheng, Jinwei Ye

## TL;DR
Pixel Cube introduces a cube-shaped LED lighting system for high-speed portrait video data capture with ground-truth HDR illumination, and uses this data to train a video diffusion model for photorealistic, temporally consistent portrait video relighting conditioned on per-frame HDR environment maps.

## Problem
Relighting portrait videos requires both physical accuracy (realistic light-skin-hair interactions) and temporal consistency across frames. Existing learning-based methods rely on OLAT (one-light-at-a-time) setups that only capture static portraits, and no real-captured dynamic portrait dataset provides ground-truth light maps. Without paired dynamic data, generalization to novel subjects, motions, and lighting is poor.

## Key Contributions
- **Pixel Cube hardware system**: a novel LED-based cube stage for realistic lighting emulation and high-speed video acquisition (overcomes the 15 FPS limit of prior light-stage-based video capture approaches) with radiometric compensation for real-world environment emulation
- **Hybrid training dataset**: combines real-captured and synthetic rendered portrait videos with known lighting, diverse subject appearances, facial motions, and head poses
- **Video diffusion relighting model**: conditioned on per-frame HDR environment maps and synthesized background images, leveraging pre-trained video generation priors for temporal consistency and identity preservation

## Method
The pipeline has two stages: (1) a Delight model that canonicalizes portrait lighting to a neutral reference, and (2) a Relight model that synthesizes target lighting given an HDR environment map. Both leverage pre-trained video diffusion model priors (temporal attention layers) for consistency. Lighting is controlled via per-frame environment map injection through cross-attention conditioning. A background image enables additional control over exposure and color tone. Long-video inference uses a sliding-window strategy. The Pixel Cube hardware uses LED panels arranged cubically with radiometric compensation to faithfully emulate arbitrary environment maps at high speed, enabling ground-truth video data capture.

## Why It Matters
Portrait video relighting has immediate practical impact for film production, AR/VR presence, virtual try-on, and content creation — currently these require expensive professional lighting setups. The Pixel Cube hardware + dataset contribution is particularly significant: it enables a new class of training data that was previously unavailable, and the insight about combining real-captured + synthetic data with known lighting is directly transferable to other inverse rendering tasks involving dynamic subjects.
