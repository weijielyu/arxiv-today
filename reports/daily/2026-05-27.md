### [PARE: Pruning and Adaptive Routing for Efficient Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27336) — Score: 88

**Authors:** Yutong Wang, Yunke Wang, Tianfan Xue, Yu Qiao

**TL;DR:** Jointly prunes video DiT width and depth using spatial-temporal-aware pruning and input-adaptive block routing, achieving ~52% parameter reduction with minimal quality loss and ~50× total speedup when combined with step distillation.

**Problem:** Video Diffusion Transformers (Wan2.1-scale, 14B parameters) are far too expensive for practical deployment. Prior compression methods compress width, depth, or steps independently and apply a fixed static architecture to every input regardless of complexity or denoising stage.

**Key Contributions:**
- Spatial-temporal head importance scoring: identifies that shallow attention heads process intra-frame content while deep heads handle cross-frame motion; protects temporal heads from pruning via a scaling factor (αtemp=1.5) that corrects their systematically lower activation magnitudes
- FFN dimension pruning with diversity-aware greedy selection to avoid redundant neuron pruning
- Content-adaptive block router conditioning on both denoising timestep and visual content (CLIP for I2V, latent statistics for T2V); allocates more blocks to high-noise steps and fewer to low-noise steps via a learned dynamic Top-K gate
- Progressive 3-stage training: width distillation, joint width-routing optimization, then step distillation

**Method:** PARE first prunes attention heads and FFN dimensions using calibration-guided WANDA scores with temporal protection. A lightweight router then learns to dynamically skip blocks per-input and per-timestep. Both are optimized jointly before a final step-distillation stage reduces 50 → 4 steps.

**Results:**
- Wan2.1-T2V-14B: 52% parameter reduction, VBench 77.10 vs. teacher 77.70 (−0.6%)
- With step distillation: 45% reduction at 4 steps, 77.00 VBench (vs. 77.70 teacher)
- Substantially beats baselines: NeoDragon (74.68), F3-Pruning (69.84)
- Wan2.1-I2V-14B: 52% reduction, 76.24 VBench (vs. 77.92 teacher)
- Projected ~50× total speedup from width+routing (~2×) plus step reduction (50→4, ~12.5×) plus CFG elimination (~2×)

**Why It Matters:** This is the most comprehensive compression study on Wan2.1-scale video DiTs. The spatial-temporal head specialization insight is new and directly actionable. The input-adaptive routing is exactly the kind of technique an agentic acceleration system should explore: it adapts to content rather than applying a fixed schedule.

### [VDE: Training-Free Accelerating Rectified Flow Model via Velocity Decomposition and Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23381) — Score: 86

**Authors:** Junwen Tan, Jinglin Liang, Hongyuan Chen, Shuangping Huang

**TL;DR:** Replaces static feature caching (which creates growing mismatch as inputs evolve) with velocity decomposition into a scalar-direction form, exploiting the temporal regularity of the components to estimate velocity at skipped steps without a forward pass.

**Problem:** All existing training-free acceleration methods for rectified flow models (FLUX, Wan, etc.) cache intermediate features from a previous timestep and reuse them unchanged. As the denoising input evolves, this creates a growing mismatch between stale cached features and the current model input, degrading quality especially at high acceleration ratios.

**Key Contributions:**
- Decompose model velocity into v_t = α_t·x_t + β_t·||x_t||·u_t (parallel and orthogonal components relative to input x_t)
- Empirical observation: scalar coefficients α, β evolve with strong local linearity across timesteps; orthogonal direction u_t remains highly stable (cosine similarity ≈0.99 between adjacent steps)
- Estimation: at "anchor" steps do full inference and decompose; at intermediate steps, linearly extrapolate α, β from recent anchors and reuse u_t; reconstruct velocity from the current input (input-adaptive, not stale)
- Unlike caching, the reconstructed velocity adapts to the current x_t at every step

**Method:** Periodic anchor steps perform full forward passes. Between anchors, VDE extrapolates the scalar coefficients via linear regression over recent anchor values and reuses the stable orthogonal direction. The reconstructed velocity is computed with the actual current input, eliminating the static-cache mismatch.

**Results:**
- FLUX.1 [dev]: 2.21× speedup with LPIPS 0.1243 (vs. 0.3197 for best cache baseline)
- FLUX.1 fast mode: 3.01× speedup with SSIM improvement (0.8267 vs. 0.7240 baseline)
- Wan2.1 video: 2.08-2.50× speedup, VBench scores near full baseline, LPIPS 0.0554-0.0754 (52% better than baselines)
- Training-free and plug-and-play across image, video, and 3D generation

**Why It Matters:** Directly applicable to the research project's action space: a new caching-type acceleration paradigm for rectified flow DiTs that outperforms all existing methods. The decomposition insight may generalize beyond this specific implementation and could inform the research agent's reasoning about why certain techniques work.

### [Timestep-Aware SVDQuant-GPTQ for W4A4 Quantization of Wan2.2-I2V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27003) — Score: 85

**Authors:** Junhao Wu, Dezhong Yao, Hai Jin

**TL;DR:** Post-training W4A4 quantization of Wan2.2-I2V that handles timestep-dependent activation distributions and MoE expert heterogeneity, achieving 59% memory reduction with only 0.9% VBench quality drop.

**Problem:** W4A4 (4-bit weights + 4-bit activations) quantization of video DiTs fails due to (1) sparse large-magnitude activation outliers that dominate quantization range and (2) strongly timestep-dependent activation distributions where early (high-noise) and late (low-noise) denoising steps have fundamentally different statistics. Wan2.2's two-expert MoE DiT design compounds this since each expert has distinct quantization sensitivities.

**Key Contributions:**
- SVDQuant baseline: channel-wise smoothing transfers outliers to weights; residual weights are decomposed into a high-precision low-rank branch plus a 4-bit residual, handled by GPTQ for reconstruction-aware quantization
- Timestep-bin-wise activation clipping: divides the denoising trajectory into coarse bins; for each (layer, bin) pair, searches the optimal activation clipping ratio by minimizing layer-wise reconstruction error
- Independent expert calibration: separate clipping search for high-noise and low-noise MoE experts
- Runtime overhead is negligible (simple lookup table indexed by current timestep)

**Method:** Post-training only — no training required. Calibration divides the trajectory into timestep bins and optimizes clipping per layer per bin per expert. At inference, the active expert and current timestep determine the clipping parameters via table lookup.

**Results:**
- Peak GPU memory: 26.2 GB vs. 64.4 GB BF16 baseline (59.3% reduction)
- Model size: 25.9 GB vs. 64.0 GB
- VBench average: 0.799 vs. 0.806 baseline (0.9% drop)
- Imaging Quality: 0.689 vs. 0.705 (2.3% drop)
- Latency currently increases (520→955s) due to lack of native FP4 hardware support; the current implementation is pseudo-quantization — the speedup benefit awaits hardware with native INT4/FP4 compute units

**Why It Matters:** Quantization is in the research project's action space. This paper provides the most detailed treatment of quantization challenges specific to the Wan model family (MoE DiT, timestep-dependent distributions) — exactly the target model class for the research project. The pseudo-quantization limitation is important: memory savings are real today, but latency benefits require FP4/INT4 hardware.
