### 1. Causal-rCM: A Unified Teacher-Forcing and Self-Forcing Open Recipe for Autoregressive Diffusion Distillation in Streaming Video Generation and Interactive World Models

**Score: 99/100** · [arXiv:2606.25473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25473) · [Project / Code](https://github.com/NVlabs/rcm)

**Kaiwen Zheng, Guande He, Min Zhao, Jintao Zhang, Huayu Chen, Jianfei Chen, Chen-Hsuan Lin, Ming-Yu Liu, Jun Zhu, Qianli Ma** (Tsinghua University, UT Austin, NVIDIA)

## TL;DR
Causal-rCM is a unified algorithm-infrastructure open recipe that distills autoregressive video diffusion models down to 1–2 sampling steps without sacrificing quality, achieving a VBench-T2V score of 84.63 with a 2-step Wan2.1-1.3B model and enabling real-time interactive world models via Cosmos 3.

## Problem
Autoregressive (AR) video diffusion with causal transformers is the leading paradigm for streaming video generation and interactive world models, but two training approaches have complementary weaknesses: teacher-forcing (offline/stable but prone to exposure bias at inference) and self-forcing with DMD (on-policy but sensitive to initialization and prone to mode collapse). No unified framework had reconciled them for the causal setting.

## Key Contributions
- Demonstrates empirically that teacher-forcing consistency models (TF-CM) are the optimal initialization strategy for self-forcing DMD, resolving mode collapse and initialization sensitivity.
- Presents the first implementation of continuous-time teacher-forcing consistency models (sCM/MeanFlow) for AR video diffusion, enabled by a novel custom-mask FlashAttention-2 JVP kernel — achieves 10× faster convergence than discrete-time CMs.
- Introduces Causal-rCM as an open, scalable algorithm-infrastructure recipe (covering parallelism, activation checkpointing, KV cache, and replayed back-propagation) applied to both streaming video generation and the Cosmos 3 interactive world model.

## Method
Causal-rCM extends the rCM framework — which unifies forward-divergence consistency distillation and reverse-divergence DMD — to the autoregressive causal setting. Teacher-forcing provides stable causal offline training for initialization; self-forcing with DMD then refines on-policy. The critical infrastructure contribution is a custom-mask FlashAttention-2 JVP (Jacobian-vector product) kernel that makes continuous-time causal distillation computationally feasible under FSDP2, Ulysses CP, and selective activation checkpointing.

## Why It Matters
This is the cleanest unification of efficient training paradigms for AR video diffusion to date, delivered as a fully open recipe. With 1-step inference at VBench-T2V 84.63, it makes real-time high-quality streaming video generation practical. Extending the same recipe to Cosmos 3 (omnimodal physical AI world model) shows immediate applicability to action-conditioned interactive world models — the bridge between video generation and embodied AI.

### 2. TryOnCrafter: Unleashing Camera Trajectories for Realistic Video Virtual Try-on via a Renderable 4D Try-on Proxy

**Score: 94/100** · [arXiv:2606.26092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26092) · [Project](https://sunhao242.github.io/TryOnCrafter_web.github.io/)

**Hao Sun, Hao Yan, Mengting Chen, Quanjian Song, Yu Li, Juan Cao, Jinsong Lan, Xiaoyong Zhu, Bo Zheng, Sheng Tang** (ICT-CAS, UCAS, Xiamen University, Alibaba Group)

## TL;DR
TryOnCrafter defines a new task — Camera-controllable Video Virtual Try-on (CaM-VVT) — and solves it by building a renderable 4D try-on proxy (clothed 3DGS avatar + SMPL-X animation + background point cloud) that anchors a Video DiT to synthesize photorealistic garment overlays under arbitrary, unconstrained camera trajectories.

## Problem
Existing video virtual try-on (VVT) methods are passively locked to the fixed camera trajectory of the input video, preventing users from viewing garments from novel viewpoints (side profiles, back appearance, 360° views). The new CaM-VVT task requires simultaneously synthesizing view-consistent garments AND maintaining strict geometric alignment between non-rigid human dynamics and background context under arbitrary camera motions — a requirement that sequential cascaded pipelines fail to meet cleanly.

## Key Contributions
- Defines Camera-controllable Video Virtual Try-on (CaM-VVT) as a new research frontier and releases CaM-VVTBench for evaluation.
- Renderable 4D Try-on Proxy: distills high-fidelity 2D image try-on priors into a clothed 3DGS-based avatar, animated via SMPL-X sequences and metric-aligned into a reconstructed background point cloud, providing a geometrically robust and texture-dense 4D scene representation.
- Proxy-Anchored Video DiT with a Cross-View Reference Adapter (CRA) and multi-modal semantic conditioning: leverages the rendered proxy as a primary geometric anchor to ensure synthesized videos are strictly constrained by prescribed trajectories and physically plausible deformations.

## Method
Two-stage pipeline unified end-to-end: (1) **4D Proxy Construction** — a 2D try-on model dresses the target garment on the human; this is lifted to a 3DGS avatar optimized via per-Gaussian deformation driven by SMPL-X motion sequences; the background is represented as a metric-aligned point cloud. (2) **Proxy-Anchored Video Generation** — the rendered proxy sequence is fed as a structural condition to a Video DiT alongside reference frames and garment features via a cross-view reference adapter, enabling photorealistic synthesis that follows the specified camera trajectory. The inherent editability of the 4D proxy further enables bullet-time effects, human relocalization, and 360° orbital viewing.

## Why It Matters
Opens a new and commercially significant research direction: users in digital fashion / e-commerce can inspect garments from arbitrary angles in realistic video, not just a frozen front-facing view. The 4D proxy paradigm elegantly bridges 3DGS, parametric body models (SMPL-X), and video diffusion transformers into a principled end-to-end framework applicable beyond try-on to any scene where explicit 4D geometry should anchor video generation.

### 3. MVTrack4Gen: Multi-View Point Tracking as Geometric Supervision for 4D Video Generation

**Score: 92/100** · [arXiv:2606.26087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26087) · [Project](https://cvlab-kaist.github.io/MVTrack4Gen/)

**JoungBin Lee, Jaewoo Jung, Jongmin Lee, Tongmin Kim, Hyunsung Kim, Takuya Narihira, Kazumi Fukuda, Jahyeok Koo, Jisang Han, Yuki Mitsufuji, Seungryong Kim** (KAIST AI, Sony AI, Sony Group)

## TL;DR
MVTrack4Gen reveals that 3D attention layers in camera-conditioned video diffusion models naturally encode cross-view geometric correspondences, then trains an auxiliary multi-view point tracking head on these features to explicitly strengthen them — yielding state-of-the-art geometric consistency in novel-view video generation without any explicit 3D reconstruction.

## Problem
Novel-view video generation from a monocular reference video demands geometric consistency, motion fidelity, accurate camera control, and photorealism simultaneously. Explicit 3D reconstruction pipelines fail on dynamic objects; camera-conditioning-only diffusion models are photorealistic but struggle with cross-view geometric and motion consistency because they lack explicit geometric grounding.

## Key Contributions
- Discovers that specific 3D attention layers encode strong cross-view correspondence cues: query features attend to key features at geometrically corresponding locations across views and over time — and that misalignment of these correspondences is the direct cause of motion inconsistency in generated videos.
- Multi-Scale 4D Correlation Volume + Multi-View Tracking Head: routes these correspondence-rich attention features into an auxiliary tracking head and jointly trains the diffusion model with a point-tracking objective, explicitly strengthening motion-aware correspondences.
- Achieves SOTA geometric consistency across diverse benchmarks (competitive camera accuracy) without introducing explicit 3D representations or reconstruction modules.

## Method
Starting from a camera-conditioning-only video diffusion transformer (3D attention over reference + target latent tokens), MVTrack4Gen adds two components: (1) improved Plücker ray camera encoding; (2) a multi-view point tracking head built on top of Multi-Scale Local 4D Correlation volumes extracted from the correspondence-rich attention features. Training jointly optimizes the diffusion loss and a tracking loss over pseudo ground-truth multi-view point tracks. The key insight — correspondences already exist in the attention maps and just need to be explicitly supervised — makes the approach architecturally lightweight.

## Why It Matters
Provides an elegant and theoretically grounded solution to the geometry-quality dilemma in novel-view video generation. The finding that attention layers encode geometric correspondences is fundamental and likely applicable across many video diffusion architectures. Achieving SOTA geometric consistency without explicit 3D reconstruction is a significant practical advance for camera-controlled generation systems.

### 4. Physics Question Scene Graph: Fine-grained Evaluation of Physical Plausibility in Text-to-Video Generation

**Score: 81/100** · [arXiv:2606.25306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25306) · [Code](https://github.com/atinpothiraj/pqsg)

**Atin Pothiraj, Jaemin Cho, Yue Zhang, Elias Stengel-Eskin, Mohit Bansal** (UNC Chapel Hill, AI2, Johns Hopkins, UT Austin)

## TL;DR
PQSG is a hierarchical VLM-driven evaluation framework for physical plausibility in text-to-video generation that decomposes scores across object / action / physics layers with logical dependency edges; evaluated on a new benchmark (FinePhyEval) covering Sora 2, Veo 3, and Wan 2.1, it achieves higher correlation with human judgments than all prior metrics.

## Problem
Current T2V evaluation metrics (VBench, FID) provide coarse aggregate scores that cannot localize or explain physical plausibility failures — making it impossible to distinguish object-presence errors, incorrect actions, and physically implausible dynamics. As models improve, subtler violations will require fine-grained diagnostics.

## Key Contributions
- Physics Question Scene Graph (PQSG): a two-stage evaluation pipeline where a VLM first generates a dependency graph of hierarchical questions (object → action → physics) from text prompts, then a VLM answers them against the generated video; logical dependencies prevent contextually invalid questions.
- FinePhyEval: a dataset of 195 human-annotated prompt-video pairs with fine-grained physics-violation labels, collected from Sora 2, Veo 3, and Wan 2.1 — enabling head-to-head comparison of frontier models on physical realism.
- Benchmark finding: proprietary models (Sora 2, Veo 3) outperform open-source (Wan 2.1, Cosmos-14B) on physical realism; all models struggle more on action and physics categories than on object presence.

## Method
**Question Generation (QG):** a VLM receives the text prompt and generates a scene graph of questions organized as object-level → action-level → physics-level with dependency edges; the graph enforces logical validity (e.g., physics of a fall is only evaluated if the object is present and falling). **Question Answering (QA):** a VLM receives the generated video plus the scene graph and answers each question; scores propagate up the dependency hierarchy. The whole pipeline is automated and model-agnostic — no human annotation needed at inference.

## Why It Matters
Fills a critical gap in the video generation evaluation toolkit: existing metrics are poor at physical plausibility, yet physical accuracy is essential for world models used in robotics and embodied AI. PQSG can provide targeted feedback on *which* physical laws a generated video violates, facilitating more principled model improvement.
