## Top Picks

### 1. Robot-Factored World Models via Robot Rendering
**Score: 85/100** | [arXiv:2607.22535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22535)

**Authors:** Byungjun Kim, Taeksoo Kim, Hyunsoo Cha, **Hanbyul Joo** (Seoul National University) | [Project Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22535)

## TL;DR
Robot-Factored World Models proposes a visual conditioning interface for action-conditioned video world models that explicitly moves two robot-specific factors—action realization and robot geometry—outside the learned model, rendering a deployment-available nominal trajectory through the robot's URDF as camera-aligned geometry and depth, enabling embodiment generalization and grounded video synthesis from human demonstrations.

## Problem
Action-conditioned video world models for robotics face a fundamental conditioning dilemma: conditioning directly on raw control signals forces the model to jointly learn action realization (how commands become motion) and scene response (how the environment reacts), coupling embodiment-specific knowledge inside the model weights. Conditioning on logged future robot states leaks the very interaction outcomes the model is meant to predict. Neither design cleanly separates what belongs in the model from what can be extracted from available robot descriptions.

## Key Contributions
- **Robot-factored visual world-model interface:** each action sequence is first rolled through the robot's controller and kinematics into a deployment-available *nominal trajectory* (the motion realized before any scene contact), then rendered through the robot URDF as camera-aligned mesh RGB and end-effector depth—factoring robot kinematics and appearance entirely out of the learned model.
- **Depth-aware conditioning:** end-effector depth is paired with scene depth to provide geometric cues for proximity, contact, and occlusion beyond what RGB image-plane overlap can encode, substantially improving contact-related prediction.
- **Embodiment generalization and human-to-robot transfer:** because the interface is defined in terms of rendered geometry rather than embodiment-specific control signals, the model generalizes zero-shot to unseen robot bodies at inference, and generates robot manipulation videos from retargeted human hand demonstrations.

## Method
Given an action sequence, the system numerically integrates it through the robot's forward kinematics and controller model to obtain a nominal trajectory—the anticipated motion prior to scene interaction. This trajectory is rendered into the camera frame using the robot's URDF model, producing RGB mesh overlays and an end-effector depth channel. Static scene context (RGB + scene depth) is provided as a camera-aware background. Together, these form the conditioning signal to a video generation backbone (Wan, adapted via LoRA-style tuning). The world model sees the robot only as explicit rendered geometry and is trained solely to predict how the surrounding scene responds to it—contact, object displacement, and occlusion—without learning any embodiment-specific motor dynamics.

## Why It Matters
This factoring principle cleanly separates concerns: embodiment-specific knowledge (kinematics, appearance) lives in the URDF renderer; scene physics lives in the learned model. The result is a world model that generalizes across robot embodiments out-of-the-box and bridges the gap between human demonstration data and robot learning—retargeting human hand motion to robot geometry and generating plausible robot-scene interaction videos without robot-specific demonstrations. For researchers in agentic AI and robot learning, this is a concrete architecture that makes learned world models more compositional and data-efficient.
