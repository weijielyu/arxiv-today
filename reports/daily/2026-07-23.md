### 1. Self Gradient Forcing: Native Long Video Extrapolation
**Score: 93/100** · [2607.20368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20368) · Project: https://zhuang2002.github.io/SelfGradientForcing

**Authors:** Junhao Zhuang, Shiyi Zhang, Yuxuan Bian, Yaowei Li, Yawen Luo, Yijun Liu, Weiyang Jin, Songchun Zhang, Xianglong He, Xuying Zhang, Haoran Li, Haoyang Huang, Zeyue Xue, Nan Duan (Joy Future Academy, JD)

## TL;DR
Self Gradient Forcing (SGF) fixes a fundamental training gap in autoregressive video diffusion by adding a second pass that lets future-frame losses supervise how earlier latents write into the KV context, enabling minute-scale video generation from only a 5-second training window.

## Problem
Autoregressive (AR) video diffusion with Self Forcing—training on self-generated rollout histories rather than ground-truth contexts—reduces exposure bias, but the historical KV cache is still used as frozen rollout state. Future frame losses therefore cannot guide how early latents encode into memory. This "historical context-gradient gap" causes degrading subject identity, layout consistency, and temporal stability as generation length grows.

## Key Contributions
- Identification of the **historical context-gradient gap**: the missing gradient path from future-frame losses to context KV encoding in autoregressive video diffusion, a principled explanation for why long-video quality degrades under Self Forcing
- **Self Gradient Forcing (SGF)**: a two-pass training strategy that restores this missing supervision without backpropagating through the full serial rollout
- Demonstrated extrapolation to **minutes-long video from a 5-second training window**, with superior subject identity, background/layout consistency, and temporal stability vs. Self Forcing baseline

## Method
SGF trains in two passes. **Pass 1**: a no-gradient autoregressive rollout (matching inference) records self-generated context and noisy future latents at a sampled denoising exit step. **Pass 2** (context-gradient reconstruction): uses the recorded context as stop-gradient clean-latent input while recomputing context KV representations and future-to-context causal attention—allowing future frame losses to flow back through context encoding. A streaming context policy and gradient boundary control memory usage and keep the reconstruction budget tractable.

## Why It Matters
Long-video generation is a core frontier and a direct priority interest. SGF closes a principled gap in the Self Forcing training paradigm without the memory cost of full backpropagation through time, yielding meaningful improvements in temporal coherence. The approach is model-agnostic and applies to any AR video generation system—making it a practical, theoretically motivated advance that will likely be widely adopted.

### 2. Vera: Identity-Faithful Human Subject-to-Video Generation
**Score: 85/100** · [2607.20247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20247)

**Authors:** Yulong Xu, Xinyue Liu, Shujuan Li, Huafeng Shi, Yan Zhou, Jiwen Liu, Xintao Wang, Pengfei Wan, Yu-Shen Liu, Huaibo Huang (Kuaishou Technology; CASIA; Tsinghua University)

## TL;DR
Vera introduces a unified human-centric subject-to-video framework with a million-pair identity-aligned dataset, spatially focused identity supervision (IFMS), and layer-selective attention regulation (RALA), achieving faithful identity preservation for single- and multi-person video generation including challenging multi-person identity binding.

## Problem
While video generation models produce photorealistic content, human facial identity drifts across frames under pose/viewpoint changes. In multi-person scenes, different identities get confused, attributes swap between subjects, or reference appearance cues get excessively copied. Generic subject consistency is structurally insufficient for human-centric video generation.

## Key Contributions
- A **million-pair identity-aligned human image-video dataset** built via person-level intra-clip identity clustering and cross-clip retrieval, providing explicit identity correspondence and diverse reference images at scale
- **Identity-Focal Masked Supervision (IFMS)**: spatially concentrated training loss on identity-critical face regions, reducing interference from irrelevant background artifacts while strengthening identity-aware feature learning
- **Reference-Aware Layer-wise Attention (RALA)**: regulates how video tokens interact with reference identity cues at each DiT transformer layer, using reference-anchor preserving masks and layer-selective identity residual reinforcement to maintain stable identity anchors across frames

## Method
Vera builds on a rectified-flow video DiT backbone. The data pipeline applies intra-clip identity clustering to pair diverse reference images with video clips. During training, IFMS applies a masked supervision loss focused on face bounding regions. RALA dynamically modulates cross-attention between video tokens and reference identity encodings at each transformer layer, with layer-selective gating that allows different layers to specialize in either identity anchoring or motion generation.

## Why It Matters
Human-centric video generation—personalized storytelling, digital humans, advertising, character-driven content—is a rapidly growing application space that intersects top-priority interests in both video generation (#2) and human video & face (#5). Vera directly addresses the identity consistency gap that currently prevents practical deployment in multi-person scenarios, and the million-pair dataset will benefit the broader community.

### 3. HeadCast: Casting Attention Heads for Efficient Autoregressive Video Generation
**Score: 84/100** · [2607.20125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20125) · Code: https://github.com/sjlgaga/HeadCast

**Authors:** Jinliang Shen, Lianghao Su, Zheming Li, Kang He, Ziliang Lai, Yanbing Jiang, Chengru Song (KlingAI Research)

## TL;DR
HeadCast classifies attention heads in pre-trained autoregressive video diffusion models into four stable archetypes (Sink, Dummy, Spatial, Global), routing each through a dedicated KV-cache pathway to achieve up to 1.95× inference speedup at 1080P without retraining or quality loss.

## Problem
Autoregressive video diffusion models synthesize video block-by-block using KV caching, but the cache grows without bound and attention over it dominates inference cost at high resolution (each frame contributes many tokens). Aggressive uniform cache eviction destroys long-range temporal consistency and causes inter-frame flickering; training-based sparse attention requires costly re-training.

## Key Contributions
- Discovery that attention heads in pre-trained AR video DiTs exhibit **four stable structural archetypes** (Sink, Dummy, Spatial, Global) that are consistent throughout generation and classifiable in a single early pass
- **HeadCast**: a training-free, plug-and-play framework performing one-time head classification at the maximum-noise step, then routing each head to a dedicated compact KV buffer
- Up to **1.62× speedup at 720P and 1.95× at 1080P** across state-of-the-art AR models while maintaining VBench quality on par with full attention and largely flicker-free output

## Method
After a brief full-context warm-up, HeadCast runs a one-time classification at the maximum-noise step (t=1000)—where attention reflects structural rather than content-specific preferences—sorting heads into: **Sink** (anchor on initial frame), **Dummy** (only most-recent block), **Spatial** (fixed-size local grid across history), and **Global** (full sliding window). The monolithic KV cache is split into compact head-specific buffers. Only Global heads retain the full window, preserving temporal coherence. The Spatial pathway's fixed-size grid ensures savings grow with resolution.

## Why It Matters
AR video generation is the leading paradigm for long and streaming video synthesis. Inference cost is a critical bottleneck in production settings, especially at high resolution. HeadCast demonstrates that pre-trained models already encode heterogeneous cache behaviors—making a principled, training-free 2× speedup achievable via structural routing alone, directly enabling higher-throughput video production at 1080P.

### 4. OSVE: One Step Video Editing with One Step Diffusion Models
**Score: 83/100** · [2607.19895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19895) · Code: https://github.com/KU-VGI/OSVE

**Authors:** Habin Lim, Gyeong-Moon Park (Korea University)

## TL;DR
OSVE adapts one-step text-to-image distilled diffusion models for video editing by combining a structure-aware learnable inversion encoder and unified-frame editing (cross-frame attention in a single generation step), achieving video editing quality comparable to multi-step methods at 155–171× faster speed.

## Problem
Text-guided video editing with diffusion models is impractically slow: both inversion (encoding the source video) and sampling (generating the edited video) require dozens of steps. One-step distilled models exist for images but have not been adapted for video editing due to challenges in inversion without iterative DDIM, maintaining editability vs. structural fidelity, and temporal consistency across frames.

## Key Contributions
- A **learnable frame-wise inversion encoder** that predicts initial noise for each frame in one forward pass (using a Structure-Aware Editing loss on structurally-aligned image pairs), bypassing slow iterative inversion
- **Unified-Frame Editing (UFE)**: concatenates all frame latents for cross-frame attention in a single generation step, enabling temporal coherence without multi-step video diffusion
- **155–171× speedup** over state-of-the-art multi-step methods with comparable editing quality (quantitative + user study), plus a sliding-window anchor strategy for long videos

## Method
OSVE leverages a pre-trained one-step T2I model (distilled from a multi-step diffusion model). An encoder is trained on structurally-aligned image pairs with SAE loss to predict per-frame noise that will reconstruct the source geometry in one denoising step. At edit time, UFE concatenates all frame latents into one tensor and runs cross-frame attention through a single generation step. For long videos, a sliding window with a fixed anchor frame maintains global consistency.

## Why It Matters
Real-time video editing is a high-demand downstream application of video generation technology. OSVE demonstrates that one-step diffusion models—previously useful only for images—can be adapted for video editing with practical speedups, opening the door to interactive video editing workflows. The core technique (structural inversion + UFE) is likely generalizable to other video-editing tasks.

### 5. Silent Failures in Multimodal Agentic Search: A Diagnostic Taxonomy and Cross-Judge Evaluation
**Score: 83/100** · [2607.19793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19793) · Project: https://github.com/DingWu1021/silent-failures-multimodal-agentic-search

**Authors:** Zhengxian Wu, Junjie Gao, Kai Yang (Ant Group)

## TL;DR
This work introduces a six-category taxonomy of silent failures in multimodal agentic search—cases where surface accuracy hides trajectory-level reasoning failures—showing that answer-level evaluation consistently overestimates true correctness across all four frontier models tested, with failures shifting rather than disappearing as model capability increases.

## Problem
Multimodal agentic search systems (agents using tool calls to answer visual knowledge questions via evidence retrieval) are evaluated almost entirely by final-answer accuracy. But intermediate reasoning—visual inspection, tool selection, evidence retrieval, evidence integration—can be systematically wrong while the final answer happens to be correct. These "silent failures" are invisible to standard benchmarks.

## Key Contributions
- A **six-category taxonomy** of silent failures for multimodal agentic search: modality shortcuts, phantom grounding, wrong-evidence-right-answer, over-retrieval laundering, cross-modal contradiction, and provenance hallucination—covering pre-retrieval, during-retrieval, and post-retrieval failure modes
- A **trajectory-level diagnostic pipeline** using a unified ReAct-style scaffold, LLM judge with structured evaluation rubric, and same-family/cross-family cross-judge validation to assess label reliability
- Empirical finding across **800 MMSearch-Plus trajectories** from 4 frontier models: surface accuracy consistently overestimates trajectory-grounded correctness; silent failures are capability-dependent and shift distribution (rather than disappearing) as model capability increases

## Method
Each frontier model runs under a unified ReAct framework with the same tool interfaces and logging format. An LLM judge evaluates each trajectory for both answer correctness and presence of each of the six silent failure categories. Reliability is assessed via blank-image stress tests, tool ablations, and cross-family judge validation to identify which findings are stable across judge models.

## Why It Matters
As agentic AI systems (#1 priority interest) are deployed for knowledge-intensive tasks, the reliability of reasoning trajectories—not just final answer accuracy—becomes critical for trust and safety. This work provides both a conceptual framework and a practical evaluation pipeline for diagnosing where current frontier multimodal agents systematically fail, directly guiding future development of more reliable agentic systems.
