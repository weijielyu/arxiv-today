### [2606.11289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11289) — i1: A Simple and Fully Open Recipe for Strong Text-to-Image Models
**Score: 88/100** | Image Generation

**Authors:** Boya Zeng, Tianze Luo, Shu Pu, Jucheng Shen, Taiming Lu, Gabriel Sarch, Zhuang Liu

## TL;DR
Princeton's i1 is the first fully open (weights, code, data) 3B-parameter text-to-image model that closes the gap with closed-source leaders — beating the previous best fully open model by 29.5 absolute percentage points — backed by 300+ controlled experiments that demystify which modeling and data choices actually drive T2I quality.

## Problem
Leading T2I diffusion models (FLUX, SeedDream, etc.) don't disclose their training details or data, making it impossible to disentangle what actually drives performance. The community needs a reproducible, fully open foundation for T2I research — yet existing fully open models still lag far behind commercial ones in quality.

## Key Contributions
- **300+ controlled experiments** totaling 700K+ TPU v6e hours, systematically ablating modeling and data choices and distilling 10 key empirical findings (e.g., equal weighting across curated datasets is a strong default; larger text encoder adapters improve performance with minimal added parameters).
- **i1-3B**: a 3B-parameter model using only publicly available data that is competitive with closed-source leaders on GenEval, DPG, PRISM, CVTG-2K, and LongText, outperforming the best prior fully open model by 29.5 pp average.
- **Complete open-source release**: model checkpoints, training + inference code, and data processing pipeline — the full recipe, not just weights.

## Method
i1 builds on a diffusion transformer backbone. Key findings guide design: combining text encoders from multiple families outperforms any single encoder; larger text encoder adapters (increased hidden dimension) improve alignment with minimal parameter overhead; removing AdaLN conditioning and adding long skip connections in the backbone both consistently improve quality. On the data side, equal-weight mixing of curated datasets (rather than hand-tuned ratios) is a surprisingly strong default, and synthetic VLM-based re-captioning plus prompt rewriting at inference substantially improve prompt following. Training proceeds at 256-resolution first, then scales to 1024.

## Why It Matters
Fully open T2I models are essential shared infrastructure — they let researchers isolate what matters, reproduce results, and build on each other's work without black-box baselines. The 29+ pp gap that previously existed between open and commercial models has been a real obstacle; i1 closes it while documenting exactly why, establishing a clean baseline from which the whole community can advance.

### [2606.12195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12195) — InternVideo3: Agentify Foundation Models with Multimodal Contextual Reasoning
**Score: 85/100** | Agentic AI + Video

**Authors:** Ziang Yan, Sheng Xia, Jiashuo Yu, Yue Wu, Tianxiang Jiang, Songze Li, Kanghui Tian, Yicheng Xu, Yinan He, Kai Chen, Limin Wang, Yu Qiao, Yi Wang

## TL;DR
InternVideo3 "agentifies" video foundation models via Multimodal Contextual Reasoning (MCR) — a closed-loop formulation that treats long-video understanding as iterative evidence accumulation, belief revision, and tool use — while M²LA attention compression makes this practical at scale, achieving strong results on Video-MME, MLVU, and EgoSchema.

## Problem
Agentic AI has advanced rapidly in text-dominant settings (coding agents, web agents, search) but lags severely on long-horizon multimodal tasks. Video MLLMs still struggle to sustain temporal reasoning across extended videos, localize evidence, and iteratively interact with tools or memory — the exact capabilities that real multimodal agents require. A single-pass question-answering step is fundamentally mismatched to these demands.

## Key Contributions
- **Multimodal Contextual Reasoning (MCR)**: a closed-loop formulation where observations, instructions, intermediate reasoning, tool actions, feedback, and memory all share one evolving unified context — treating video understanding as evidence accumulation and belief revision, not one-shot prediction.
- **M²LA (Multimodal Multi-head Latent Attention)**: a token-preserving attention reparameterization that compresses KV-cache states via RoPE-aware positional aggregation and low-rank latent factorization, enabling efficient long-horizon rollouts without discarding the full token stream.
- **Staged training recipe**: continued pretraining after M²LA conversion → short-to-long supervised fine-tuning for video → rule-based RL on verifiable tasks → on-policy distillation from stronger teacher models. Strong results on Video-MME, MLVU, and EgoSchema, with especially notable gains on long-horizon tasks.

## Method
MCR frames each task as a closed loop: the model receives multimodal observations and instructions, generates reasoning steps, selects and executes tool actions or memory reads, incorporates feedback, updates the shared context, and iterates until it has gathered sufficient evidence for a grounded answer or action. M²LA converts pretrained self-attention into a compressed-KV format via three steps: RoPE-aware positional aggregation (merging positions without losing rotational structure), low-rank content-channel factorization, and modality-aware latent adaptation. A video agent instantiation adds hierarchical memory, question routing, a tool set, and recursive verification, demonstrating how MCR composes with retrieval to support robust evidence-grounded behavior.

## Why It Matters
This paper directly bridges the two top interests in the researcher profile — agentic AI (#1) and video (#2) — by formalizing a practical abstraction (MCR) for what it means for a multimodal model to "reason like an agent" over time. The closed-loop, context-accumulating paradigm generalizes beyond video to any long-horizon task requiring iterative evidence gathering. M²LA is an immediately reusable efficiency component.

### [2606.11783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11783) — A Comprehensive Ecosystem for Open-Domain Customized Video Generation
**Score: 82/100** | Video Generation

**Authors:** Jingxu Zhang, Yuqian Hong, Daneul Kim, Kai Qiu, et al.

## TL;DR
PexelsCustom-1M — the first publicly available million-scale dataset of ⟨identity, text, video⟩ triplets across 8,000+ identity categories — combined with CustoMDiT (a parameter-efficient diffusion transformer requiring only 8% added parameters) and OpenCustom (a 1,000+-category benchmark), establishes the open-source foundation that identity-preserving video generation has been missing.

## Problem
Open-domain customized video generation (preserving a specific visual identity across diverse text-guided scenarios) is bottlenecked by the absence of large-scale annotated training data. Existing methods either cover narrow categorical domains or require per-identity fine-tuning at test time, neither of which scales to real-world applications spanning thousands of identity types.

## Key Contributions
- **PexelsCustom-1M**: 1M high-quality ⟨identity, text, video⟩ triplets across 8,000+ identity categories, curated from 400K HD Pexels videos via a dual-phase pipeline (VLM captioning + Grounded-SAM segmentation in preprocessing; aesthetic filtering, re-captioning, and augmentation in postprocessing).
- **CustoMDiT**: a Diffusion Transformer conditioned on identity-aware reference images via bias-injected RoPE embeddings + LoRA adaptation — only 8% additional learnable parameters on a pretrained text-to-video DiT — surpassing prior state-of-the-art including commercial APIs (Vidu).
- **OpenCustom benchmark**: 1,000+ categories (vs. 100 in DreamBooth) fused from ImageNet and MS-COCO, with a unified protocol for identity extraction, context-aware prompting, and multi-dimensional evaluation.

## Method
Data curation: the preprocessing stage uses a VLM to generate subject-centric captions for Pexels videos, extracts identities with GPT-4o, then runs Grounding-DINO + SAM to produce segmentation masks for each identity. Postprocessing applies aesthetic, bounding-box size, and overlap filters, then re-captions each identity with a contextual VLM caption and augments to reduce artifacts. CustoMDiT injections identity via bias-adjusted RoPE positional embeddings on reference image tokens, while LoRA layers in the transformer blocks handle efficient adaptation. Full 1M-triplet training is used for the final model.

## Why It Matters
The complete open-source release of the dataset, curation pipeline, benchmark, and model fills the infrastructure gap that has limited video customization research to narrow laboratory settings. With 8,000+ identity categories (80× more than prior benchmarks), researchers can now study identity preservation at realistic scale — and the parameter-efficient approach means adaptation to new video generation backbones is straightforward.

### [2606.12402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12402) — DIRECT: When and Where Should You Allocate Test-Time Compute in Embodied Planners?
**Score: 80/100** | Agentic AI

**Authors:** Jadelynn Dao, Milan Ganai, Yasmina Abukhadra, Ajay Sridhar, Mozhgan Nasr Azadani, Katie Luo, Clark Barrett, Jiajun Wu, Chelsea Finn, Marco Pavone

## TL;DR
DIRECT is a routing framework for hierarchical embodied planning that dynamically allocates test-time compute (chain-of-thought depth, model size, or memory context) per task from multimodal scene context — matching frontier VLM performance at up to 65% lower latency, validated on physical Franka hardware.

## Problem
Scaling test-time compute uniformly for embodied VLM planners wastes resources: frontier VLM latencies reach tens of seconds per step, and the three dominant scaling axes (deeper CoT reasoning, larger models, longer memory context) yield qualitatively different capability gains that are task-dependent. A 3.8× slower thinking model can have the same success rate as its non-thinking counterpart on simple tasks, yet be essential on others.

## Key Contributions
- **Diagnostic analysis** across three test-time compute axes showing they are complementary and non-interchangeable: CoT depth helps tasks with implicit spatial/semantic constraints; model size governs breadth of commandable skills; memory history helps on history-dependent tasks but can hurt elsewhere.
- **DIRECT router**: a lightweight multimodal model that conditions on scene context (image + instruction) and routes each task to the cheapest VLM configuration that matches the task's inferred cognitive demands — across all three scaling axes independently.
- **Physical validation** on a Franka arm (DROID setup) spanning zero-shot manipulation and long-horizon chaining: DIRECT matches the strongest model's success rate at up to 65% lower average latency.

## Method
For each of the three compute axes, the authors collect trajectory data on VLABench and RoboMME under multiple configurations, then train a lightweight router to predict which configuration (thinking/non-thinking, model size tier, memory window) will succeed on a given task from its multimodal context. The routers are modular and independently trained. Evaluation follows simulation results with physical Franka DROID validation.

## Why It Matters
The core insight — that different compute axes confer different cognitive capabilities, so matching compute type to task demand is more important than raw compute amount — is directly actionable for building efficient real-world agent systems. DIRECT is a practical blueprint for deploying frontier-level embodied agents under real latency constraints.
