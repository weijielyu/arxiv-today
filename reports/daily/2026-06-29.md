### 1. TempAct: Advancing Temporal Plausibility in Autoregressive Video Generation via Planner-Executor RL

**Score: 83/100** · [arXiv:2606.28016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28016)  
**Authors:** Jing Wang, Xiangxin Zhou, Jiajun Liang, Kaiqi Liu, Wanyun Pang, Zhenyu Xie, Tianyu Pang, Xiaodan Liang  
*(Tencent Hunyuan, Sun Yat-Sen University, Tsinghua University, Peking University, USTB)*  
Project page: https://jingw193.github.io/TempAct/ · Code: https://github.com/jingw193/TempAct

## TL;DR
TempAct proposes a Planner-Executor reinforcement learning framework for autoregressive (AR) video generation that jointly optimizes an LLM planner (temporal decomposition) and an AR diffusion executor (chunk-wise execution), directly training for temporally plausible event ordering across long-horizon multi-step video generation.

## Problem
AR video diffusion models generate chunk-by-chunk, but a single global prompt causes "temporal confusion" — events appear in the wrong order across chunks. Step-wise prompts partially fix this but create new failures at prompt transitions (delayed reactions, blended semantics, error propagation). SFT and rollout-based distillation cannot directly reward action ordering or prompt-transition correctness because they optimize low-level denoising or teacher-distribution matching rather than temporal plausibility.

## Key Contributions
1. **TempAct framework**: A planner–executor RL design where an LLM planner generates span-aware step prompts executable by the video model, and an AR diffusion executor learns to follow them under its own generated histories — jointly trained end-to-end with RL.
2. **Hierarchical group exploration**: Candidate plans form planning groups; each plan induces an execution group of multiple continuations from a shared visual context, enabling plan-level credit assignment for long-horizon temporal outcomes and executor-level credit assignment for prompt-switch behavior.
3. **Multi-level reward design**: Combines plan-quality and full-video temporal feedback for the planner with local transition-level step-following rewards, aesthetic regularization, and KL constraints for the executor.

## Method
TempAct decomposes temporally complex generation into two coupled problems: (1) the LLM planner explores candidate temporal decompositions (span-aware step prompts) for a given global instruction; (2) the AR diffusion executor (built on Self-Forcing/LongLive) generates video chunks conditioned on these step prompts under its own generated visual context. Hierarchical group exploration creates planning groups (multiple candidate plans) and execution groups (multiple rollouts per plan from the same visual context), enabling credit assignment at both the plan level (which temporal decomposition leads to correct event order?) and the executor level (which execution handles prompt transitions smoothly?). Hierarchical rewards supervise the planner on full-video temporal consistency and the executor on transition accuracy, visual aesthetics, and KL divergence from the base model. Experiments on Self-Forcing and LongLive demonstrate improved temporal consistency while preserving visual quality.

## Why It Matters
As AR video generation scales toward interactive world models, game engines, and embodied AI data generation, temporal instruction following over long horizons becomes a core requirement. TempAct is the first RL framework to jointly train the planning and execution components of AR video generation — directly optimizing for event ordering rather than optimizing a proxy. The hierarchical credit assignment mechanism is broadly applicable to any chunk-based AR video system and represents a principled path toward controllable, temporally coherent long-horizon video generation.

### 2. Directing the World: Fast Autoregressive Video Generation with Compositional Human-Camera Control

**Score: 80/100** · [arXiv:2606.27964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27964)  
**Authors:** Haoyuan Wang, Yabo Chen, Haibin Huang, Chi Zhang, Xuelong Li  
*(Institute of Artificial Intelligence, China Telecom TeleAI)*  
Project page: https://whydahuzi.github.io/Directing-the-World.github.io/

## TL;DR
"Directing the World" presents a fast autoregressive video generation framework with compositional control over both fine-grained human motion (single or multiple people) and camera trajectories simultaneously, using a Fast-Slow Memory training strategy to maintain temporal stability over long rollouts.

## Problem
Existing AR video world models either treat humans as passive elements or provide only coarse human control. Camera-control methods lack fine-grained articulated human dynamics. Long-horizon AR generation suffers from error accumulation, and combining heterogeneous controls (human motion + camera trajectory) makes the problem harder — different control signals can interfere with the pretrained video prior. No prior system unifies persistent world memory, precise multi-person motion control, and compositional camera-trajectory control.

## Key Contributions
1. **Fast-Slow Memory training strategy**: Stabilizes long-horizon AR rollout learning and accelerates convergence during controllable post-training by using different update frequencies for memory tokens across rollout lengths.
2. **t-guided Dynamic Projection + Motion-CFG**: Injects human pose conditions adaptively across denoising timesteps, improving motion alignment without sacrificing visual fidelity; naturally supports multi-person and individual-subject-among-crowd control.
3. **Two-stage compositional control**: Stage 1 trains a robust human-motion prior on motion-centric data; Stage 2 adds Causal-Aligned Camera Control on camera-centric data, enabling compositional control of both in a unified AR framework.
4. **Large-scale controllable world-model dataset**: Synchronized video, text, human-motion, and camera-trajectory annotations organized into motion-centric and camera-centric subsets for decoupled two-stage learning.

## Method
Built on an MMPL-based pretrained AR video diffusion model, the system decouples learning of human-motion and camera-trajectory controls to avoid cross-contamination. The t-guided Dynamic Projection injects pose conditions at specific denoising stages via a timestep-conditioned projection, combined with a refined Motion-CFG strategy that adjusts conditioning strength across denoising steps. For multi-person scenarios, each person's motion tokens are projected independently and composed in the AR visual context. Camera control is added in a second stage via a Causal-Aligned Camera Control module that encodes camera trajectories with causal attention masks, preserving the acquired human-motion prior. The Fast-Slow Memory strategy maintains different token update rates for recent and distant visual context during long rollouts, reducing error accumulation.

## Why It Matters
Interactive world models need to simulate human activities with full spatial control — both what people do and how the camera observes the scene. This work enables fine-grained compositional control over complex multi-agent scenarios in video form, directly applicable to embodied AI training data generation, interactive digital content creation, and virtual world simulation. The decoupled two-stage training approach provides a replicable recipe for adding heterogeneous control signals to pretrained AR video models without destabilizing the learned video prior.
