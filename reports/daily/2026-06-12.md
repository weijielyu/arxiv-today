### [2606.13679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13679) — InterleaveThinker: Reinforcing Agentic Interleaved Generation
**Score: 92/100** | Agentic AI + Image Generation

**Authors:** Dian Zheng, Harry Lee, Manyuan Zhang, Kaituo Feng, Zoey Guo, Ray Zhang, Hongsheng Li

## TL;DR
InterleaveThinker is the first multi-agent pipeline that endows any existing image generator with interleaved text-image generation capabilities — a planner agent orchestrates multi-step generation while a critic agent detects deviations and refines instructions via GRPO-trained RL, achieving GPT-5-comparable performance on interleaved generation benchmarks and delivering surprising gains on visual reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem
Current image generators are architecturally limited to single-image output; they cannot produce interleaved text-image sequences needed for visual narratives, step-by-step guidance, and embodied manipulation. Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) that can interleave outputs suffer from visual over-reliance (stopping at an intermediate state that looks like the goal) and step-wise error accumulation across long-horizon trajectories.

## Key Contributions
- **Multi-agent pipeline**: a planner agent organizes the image-text input sequence and instructs the underlying image generator at each step; a critic agent evaluates generator outputs, identifies deviations from the plan, and refines instructions for regeneration — decoupling interleaved generation capability from generator architecture.
- **Interleave-Critic-RL training with GRPO**: handles trajectories involving 25+ generator calls by training only single-step RL with accuracy reward and step-wise reward, making full-trajectory optimization computationally tractable.
- **Cross-benchmark generalization**: InterleaveThinker achieves performance comparable to Nano Banana and GPT-5 on interleaved generation benchmarks, and substantially boosts the base model on visual reasoning (WISE: 0.47 → 0.73; RISE: 13.3 → 28.9 on 4-step FLUX.2-klein).

## Method
A planner agent decomposes an interleaved generation request into a step-by-step plan and issues per-step instructions to an off-the-shelf image generator. After each generation, a critic agent evaluates the output against the plan, identifies deviations, and either accepts the result or refines the instruction for a regeneration attempt. Training uses SFT cold-starts (Interleave-Planner-SFT-80k, Interleave-Critic-SFT-112k) followed by GRPO RL on Interleave-Critic-RL-13k. The accuracy reward measures whether the final interleaved output fulfills the prompt; the step-wise reward penalizes individual steps that deviate from plan, allowing single-step RL to guide the entire multi-step trajectory without computing gradients through all generator calls.

## Why It Matters
By separating the "what to generate next" decision (agents) from the "how to generate it" capability (any image generator), InterleaveThinker is a modular wrapper that immediately benefits from improvements in base generators. The agentic paradigm — plan, generate, critique, refine — is a natural fit for the sequential, correctable workflows that visual narratives, embodied instructions, and multi-panel stories require.

### [2606.13432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13432) — OmniDirector: General Multi-Shot Camera Cloning without Cross-Paired Data
**Score: 88/100** | Video Generation

**Authors:** Jiwen Liu, Shujuan Li, Zhixue Fang, Xiaohan Li, Yan Zhou, Zijie Meng, Zhimin Zhang, Yawen Luo, Guoxin Zhang, Yu-Shen Liu, Pengfei Wan

## TL;DR
OmniDirector introduces the "camera grid" — a visual representation of camera parameters as motion videos — enabling general multi-shot camera cloning for video generation without needing scarce cross-paired data, trained on million-scale pairs with a hierarchical prompt expansion agent for director-level multimodal control.

## Problem
Cloning camera motion from reference videos is the most intuitive camera control interface for video generation, but existing methods either fail on multi-shot transitions (parametric representations) or rely on cross-paired data that is extremely scarce in real-world scenarios (implicit methods), resulting in poor performance on complicated camera motion.

## Key Contributions
- **Camera grid representation**: encodes camera parameters as grid motion videos — a visual, model-native representation that supports arbitrary trajectories and shot transitions, bridging the semantic gap between parametric camera data and image/video signals.
- **OmniDirector framework**: trained on million-scale camera grid-video pairs to coordinate characters, actions, and cameras within multimodal diffusion transformers, enabling director-level control over both camera and content.
- **Hierarchical prompt expansion agent**: autonomously harmonizes diverse control signals (camera motion, visual content, character actions) by systematically describing their relationships, combined with an adaptive classifier-free guidance strategy.

## Method
Camera motion from a reference video is encoded as a "camera grid" — a motion video that visualizes how the camera moves over the scene as a structured grid pattern. This representation handles multi-shot transitions by integrating diverse trajectory types. OmniDirector injects the camera grid via token concatenation into multimodal diffusion transformer architectures. A hierarchical prompt expansion agent processes input signals (text, reference video, camera grid) and produces unified textual descriptions of camera motion and visual content. Training uses million-scale camera grid-video pairs curated without requiring matched cross-paired source/target videos.

## Why It Matters
Decoupling camera representation from scene content via the camera grid enables training at internet scale without special cross-paired collections; the resulting model generalizes to complex multi-shot cinematography that no previous video generation system could clone from reference.

### [2606.13652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13652) — World Tracing: Generative Pixel-Aligned Geometry Beyond the Visible
**Score: 88/100** | 3D Reconstruction & Generation

**Authors:** Hao Zhang, Mohamed El Banani, Jen-Hao Cheng, Paul Zhang, Yi Hua, Ben Mildenhall, Christoph Lassner, Narendra Ahuja, Gengshan Yang

## TL;DR
World Tracing is a generative pixel-aligned geometry model that predicts an ordered stack of 3D surface points per input pixel — both the visible surface and all occluded surfaces behind it — using a flow-matching diffusion transformer, unifying faithful depth estimation and complete shape generation in a single model with HuggingFace demo available.

## Problem
Image-to-3D methods face a fundamental faithfulness-completeness tradeoff: depth estimators recover visible surfaces faithfully but stop there, while image-to-3D generators complete full shapes but lose pixel-level alignment with the input. Neither type can simultaneously be faithful to observed pixels and complete about occluded geometry.

## Key Contributions
- **World Tracing representation**: for each input pixel, an ordered stack of camera-space 3D points is predicted — the first layer is the visible surface, subsequent layers encode front-to-back intersections with occluded surfaces, providing complete and pixel-aligned geometry in one unified output.
- **WT-DiT architecture**: a diffusion transformer that treats multiple geometry layers as separate denoising tokens coupled through factorized (within-layer) and global (cross-layer) attention, trained with pixel-space flow matching and a mixed noise schedule balancing visible reconstruction and occluded-geometry generation.
- **Downstream versatility**: text-driven 3D scene editing, geometry-conditioned novel-view video synthesis, and training-free integration with textured-mesh generators, all enabled by the preserved 2D-to-3D correspondence.

## Method
WT-DiT takes an RGB image (or monocular video) and for each pixel independently denoises a stack of 3D points using flow matching. The mixed noise schedule applies lower noise to the first (visible) layer and higher noise to deeper (occluded) layers, guiding the model to prioritize faithful surface reconstruction while learning to hallucinate occluded geometry. Multi-layer tokens are coupled with both factorized attention (within each layer, spatially) and global attention (across all layers) to capture inter-layer dependencies and occlusion structure.

## Why It Matters
Layered pixel-aligned geometry is a powerful primitive: it simultaneously solves depth estimation and shape completion with a single model and forward pass, and its 2D-to-3D correspondence makes it directly usable for 3D editing, novel-view synthesis, and generation pipelines that need geometry-aware conditioning.

### [2606.13345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13345) — JointEdit3D: Feed-Forward 3D Scene Editing in a Unified Latent Space
**Score: 87/100** | 3D Generation

**Authors:** Xinnan Zhu, Ruijie Xu, Jiayu Ying, Daoguo Dong, Jiachen Xu, Yuan Xie, Xin Tan

## TL;DR
JointEdit3D is a feed-forward framework that edits 3D scenes by jointly synthesizing appearance and geometry in a unified RGB-geometry latent space — taking just one edited 2D reference image and producing consistent multi-view RGB outputs plus edited geometry without any per-scene optimization.

## Problem
Existing 3D scene editing requires either slow per-scene optimization over explicit 3D representations (NeRF, 3DGS) or cascaded pipelines that edit in 2D then reconstruct 3D separately, leading to structural inconsistencies, high test-time cost, and limited 3D-awareness during the editing step.

## Key Contributions
- **Asymmetric latent inpainting**: the edit branch observes only a single edited RGB reference latent, while the SceneAnchor Branch injects full source-scene structure — generating remaining multi-view RGB and edited geometry without direct copying from source.
- **SceneAnchor Branch**: an auxiliary branch that provides source-scene structure as a soft anchor, preventing the model from directly copying source appearance while still preserving unedited background geometry.
- **SceneEdit3D-15K dataset + SceneEdit3D-Bench**: 15K paired 3D editing samples with renderer-provided 3D annotations, and a curated 100-sample benchmark addressing the lack of standardized 3D scene editing evaluation.

## Method
JointEdit3D builds on a pretrained unified RGB-geometry reconstruction-generation latent space. At inference, only the edited 2D reference image is given to the edit branch (as a latent); the SceneAnchor Branch processes the full source scene to provide structural guidance through interleaved residual fusion. Edit-aware region decomposition separates edited and background regions during training, with within-region weighting and region-specific losses to balance fidelity in the edited zone and preservation of unedited content.

## Why It Matters
Feed-forward 3D editing without per-scene optimization is critical for interactive and scalable 3D content creation; by coupling appearance and geometry in one unified latent space, JointEdit3D ensures structural consistency that cascaded 2D-edit-then-reconstruct approaches cannot guarantee.

### [2606.13035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13035) — TetherCache: Stabilizing Autoregressive Long-Form Video Generation with Gated Recall and Trusted Alignment
**Score: 86/100** | Video Generation

**Authors:** Yu Meng, Xiangyang Luo, Letian Li, Wenyuan Jiang, Chen Gao

## TL;DR
TetherCache is a training-free, plug-and-play KV-cache management strategy for autoregressive video diffusion that prevents temporal drift in long-form generation — combining attention-diversity-balanced memory recall (GRAB) with statistics-aligned memory editing (TAME) — reducing quality drift from 7.84 to 1.33 on 240-second video generation on VBench-Long.

## Problem
Autoregressive video diffusion models (e.g., Self-Forcing) generate long videos by conditioning on previously generated frames, but two problems cause temporal drift: (1) limited KV-cache budget prevents retaining full history, forcing eviction of useful long-range frames; (2) conditioning on self-generated (potentially degraded) frames introduces a distribution shift that accumulates into visual artifacts and quality degradation over time.

## Key Contributions
- **GRAB (Gated Recall with Attention-Diversity Balancing)**: selects memory frames using a gated score combining attention-based relevance with temporal diversity, preserving informative yet temporally spread historical context under a fixed cache budget.
- **TAME (Trusted Alignment via Memory Editing)**: lightly edits recalled memory tokens by aligning their feature statistics to a trusted context distribution, neutralizing the distribution drift that accumulates in self-generated frames before they pollute the current generation step.
- **Plug-and-play integration**: training-free and backbone-agnostic; consistently improves VBench-Long across 30s, 60s, and 240s generation settings without modifying the base model.

## Method
TetherCache organizes the KV-cache into three regions: sink (fixed early context), memory (selected long-range frames), and recent (latest frames). GRAB computes a gated relevance score that combines attention-based similarity with a diversity penalty, ensuring the memory region captures both relevant and temporally spread history. TAME then normalizes recalled memory token statistics to match those of the trusted context (recent clean frames), preventing distribution mismatch from propagating through cross-attention to the newly generated content.

## Why It Matters
Minute-scale autoregressive video generation is a practical target for storytelling, simulation, and planning applications; TetherCache is the first method to separately address both the selection and the correction of long-range memory, achieving stable generation at 240 seconds — a 4× improvement in generation length over prior stable settings.

### [2606.13644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13644) — Surflo: Consistent 3D Surface Flow Model with Global State
**Score: 83/100** | 3D Reconstruction

**Authors:** Antoine Guédon, Shu Nakamura, Nicolas Dufour, Jiahui Lei, Ko Nishino, Angjoo Kanazawa

## TL;DR
Surflo encodes any number of unposed RGB views into a fixed-size global state (K latent tokens) and decodes arbitrarily many oriented surface points via flow matching — the first feed-forward 3D model to combine a global scene representation with resolution-free point decoding, orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based methods.

## Problem
Per-view feed-forward reconstruction models emit overlapping, unaligned point maps that grow linearly with input count; global-latent models commit to a fixed, low-resolution output token budget. Neither approach cleanly separates scene representation from output resolution.

## Key Contributions
- **Global state encoding**: a variable number of unposed RGB views are compressed into K fixed latent tokens regardless of input count, properly treating 3D geometry as invariant to viewpoint and avoiding redundant per-view encodings.
- **Resolution-free flow-matching decoder**: oriented 3D surface points are independently transported from noise to the surface via flow matching, allowing the same latent to decode from a few thousand to one million points in a single forward pass.
- **Inference-time photometric guidance**: a photometric gradient is injected during ODE integration to correlate nearby independently-decoded points, suppressing local inconsistencies without changing the model.

## Method
A transformer encoder compresses all input RGB views into K latent tokens, treating the collection as a redundant encoding of one global 3D state. A flow-matching decoder then transports query points from Gaussian noise to the surface conditioned on these K tokens. During ODE integration, a photometric guidance term — computed by rendering predicted points back to input views and comparing with observed pixels — provides a gradient that correlates nearby points to enforce local consistency.

## Why It Matters
Arbitrary-resolution output from a fixed-size latent is practically valuable: low-res latents can be decoded quickly for previewing, then the same latent can generate million-point surfaces for final output; the global state also enables principled fusion of many unposed views without accumulating per-view artifacts.

### [2606.13673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13673) — SpatialClaw: Rethinking Action Interface for Agentic Spatial Reasoning
**Score: 83/100** | Agentic AI

**Authors:** Seokju Cho, Ryo Hachiuma, Abhishek Badki, Hang Su, Byung-Kwan Lee, Chan Hee Song, Sifei Liu, Subhashree Radhakrishnan, Seungryong Kim, Yu-Chiang Frank Wang, Min-Hung Chen

## TL;DR
SpatialClaw is a training-free agentic spatial reasoning framework that uses code as the action interface — maintaining a stateful Python kernel where a VLM writes and executes one cell per step, observing all prior outputs — achieving 59.9% average accuracy across 20 spatial reasoning benchmarks, +11.2 points over the prior spatial agent, with no benchmark-specific training.

## Problem
Spatial reasoning agents face a design dilemma in their action interface: single-pass code execution commits to a full analysis plan before any intermediate result is observed (no mid-course correction), while structured tool-call interfaces limit compositional flexibility (tools can only be invoked in predefined ways). Both designs are fundamentally ill-suited to open-ended 3D/4D spatial reasoning that requires dynamic, adaptive analysis.

## Key Contributions
- **Code-as-action interface with stateful kernel**: maintains a persistent Python kernel pre-loaded with spatial reasoning libraries (3D reconstruction, SAM3, geometry tools, masking, etc.), so the VLM agent writes one executable cell per step and all state is preserved between steps.
- **Cell-by-cell adaptive reasoning loop**: each step conditions on all prior code, outputs (text and visual), and intermediate results — enabling dynamic tool composition, mid-course correction, and strategy adaptation based on what previous steps revealed.
- **Zero-shot generalization**: 59.9% average over 20 diverse spatial benchmarks (static and dynamic 3D/4D tasks), outperforming the prior spatial agent by +11.2 points, with consistent gains across 6 VLM backbones from 2 model families.

## Method
SpatialClaw provides the agent with a Python kernel pre-seeded with spatial primitives (vlm.locate, tools.Reconstruct for 3D reconstruction, tools.SAM3 for segmentation, tools.Geometry for measurements, tools.Time for temporal reasoning, etc.). A planning stage generates an analysis strategy from the question; code generation writes a Python cell that calls one or more tools; execution runs the cell in a sandbox; feedback assembles text and visual outputs. This loop repeats until the agent is ready to submit an answer. The stateful kernel means intermediate 3D structures, point clouds, or segmentation masks persist across steps.

## Why It Matters
The choice of action interface — not just the VLM backbone — is a primary driver of agentic spatial reasoning performance; code-as-action with a persistent stateful kernel provides the compositionality and adaptability that rigid tool-call interfaces lack, without requiring any additional training on spatial tasks.

### [2606.12886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12886) — Bridging Modal Isolation in Interleaved Thinking: Supervising Modality Transitions via Stepwise Reinforcement
**Score: 82/100** | Agentic AI + Image Generation

**Authors:** Tingyu Li, Le Zhou, Siyuan Li, Yujun Wu, Xinglong Xu, Jingxuan Wei, Conghui He, Cheng Tan

## TL;DR
MoTiF (Modality Transition Fidelity) identifies and fixes "Modal Isolation" — the failure mode where generated images diverge from textual reasoning plans and subsequent text ignores the visual evidence — by defining explicit modality transition losses and training with Reflective SFT followed by Flow-GRPO RL on transition-level rewards.

## Problem
In interleaved thinking (multimodal models alternating text reasoning and visual generation), long-chain scenarios exhibit Modal Isolation: generated images stop following textual plans while subsequent text ignores visual evidence, causing modalities to alternate without genuinely informing each other. This information loss compounds at each modality boundary and is not corrected by end-task training signals.

## Key Contributions
- **Modal Isolation diagnosis**: decomposes each reasoning cycle into atomic operations and defines modality transition loss — quantifying both cross-modal hallucination (text-to-image: generated image diverges from textual context) and visual utilization deficit (image-to-text: text reasoning ignores generated image evidence).
- **Reflective SFT**: trains the model to detect erroneous visual outputs (where the generated image doesn't follow the text plan) and regenerate with corrected conditioning, instilling self-monitoring at modality boundaries.
- **Flow-GRPO reinforcement learning**: improves image generation fidelity specifically at text-to-image transitions using RL rewards derived from transition-level fidelity rather than final task accuracy, improving cross-modal coherence without end-task overfitting.

## Method
MoTiF decomposes each reasoning cycle into text-to-image (T→I) and image-to-text (I→T) transitions and defines per-transition fidelity metrics. Stage 1 (Reflective SFT): supervised on trajectories where the model explicitly detects when a generated image diverges from its own textual plan and recovers with a revised generation. Stage 2 (Flow-GRPO): RL training where rewards come from T→I fidelity measurements at each boundary step, using group relative policy optimization on generation flows.

## Why It Matters
Modal Isolation is a previously unnamed but widespread failure mode in interleaved reasoning that neither end-task fine-tuning nor scaling alone addresses; MoTiF demonstrates that explicitly supervising transition quality improves both task accuracy and cross-modal coherence, pointing toward a principled training framework for multimodal chain-of-thought.

### [2606.13655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13655) — Flex4DHuman: Flexible Multi-view Video Diffusion for 4D Human Reconstruction
**Score: 81/100** | 3D/4D Reconstruction + Human Video

**Authors:** Jen-Hao Cheng, Yipeng Wang, Hao Zhang, Gengshan Yang, Jenq-Neng Hwang

## TL;DR
Flex4DHuman adapts the Wan 2.1 video diffusion model to generate synchronized dense multi-view videos from monocular or sparse input, conditioning only on relative camera-pose positional encoding — no skeleton, depth, or geometry priors needed — and feeds the output to off-the-shelf 4DGS for dynamic 4D Gaussian splat reconstruction of humans and animals.

## Problem
4D human reconstruction requires synchronized multi-view video, which is expensive to capture. Existing methods rely on explicit geometry priors (skeletons, depth maps, normals, rendered geometry) that limit generalization to novel subjects and diverse body types. Acquiring these priors for arbitrary monocular videos (e.g., phone-recorded footage) is impractical.

## Key Contributions
- **Prior-free multi-view video generation**: conditions generation entirely through relative camera-pose positional encoding (SE(3) relative geometry) without any explicit skeleton, depth, normal, or rendered-view conditioning.
- **Five-axis spatio-temporal RoPE**: extends the backbone's spatio-temporal RoPE with two additional axes for view index and continuous SE(3) relative camera geometry, seamlessly integrating multi-view structure into the existing temporal architecture.
- **Curriculum training + animal generalization**: three-stage curriculum (pose following → reference-to-target view generation → temporal rollout) and mixed human-animal training, demonstrating that the formulation generalizes to non-human subjects.

## Method
Built directly on the Wan 2.1 1.3B text-to-video model with minimal architectural changes. Camera pose information is injected through a five-axis positional encoding that appends view index and per-token SE(3) relative camera geometry to the existing spatio-temporal RoPE dimensions. During temporal rollout training, clean historical target-view tokens from previous frames are used as conditioning (avoiding autoregressive drift). Multi-view captions enable test-time text control over dynamic content. Generated dense multi-view videos are passed directly to an off-the-shelf 4D Gaussian Splatting pipeline.

## Why It Matters
Separating 4D reconstruction into a generation stage (Flex4DHuman: monocular → dense multi-view) and a reconstruction stage (off-the-shelf 4DGS) enables independent improvement of each component; the prior-free formulation means any monocular video of a person — or animal — becomes a viable input for 4D content creation targeting simulation, gaming, AR/VR, and video re-shooting.

### [2606.13558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13558) — Edit the Bits, Diff the Codes: Bitwise Residual Editing for Visual Autoregressive Models
**Score: 81/100** | Image Generation

**Authors:** Shengqiang Zhang, Ruotong Liao, Volker Tresp, Barbara Plank, Hinrich Schütze

## TL;DR
BitResEdit is a training-free text-guided image editor for bitwise-residual visual autoregressive generators (e.g., Infinity) that exploits two native model structures — the per-bit Bernoulli prediction head (BitEdit) and the additive multi-scale residual code field (ResEdit) — achieving the strongest CLIP text alignment among same-backbone VAR editors on PIE-Bench without any fine-tuning.

## Problem
Existing editors for visual autoregressive (VAR) models operate only on token streams, features, or flat next-token logits, ignoring the native bitwise structure of models like Infinity that use per-bit Bernoulli prediction and multi-scale additive residual codes. This mismatch leaves two powerful intervention points unused, limiting text alignment and precise spatial control during editing.

## Key Contributions
- **BitEdit**: tilts post-CFG per-bit log-odds along a source-target contrast computed on a shared edited prefix, then projects each bit update into a closed-form Bernoulli-KL trust region — providing decision-time guidance that respects the model's native Bernoulli sampling process.
- **ResEdit**: converts sampled bits into per-scale continuous-code residuals, gates them with a localization mask, and re-injects through the generator's native sum-of-scales — preserving masked-out regions exactly via code arithmetic while applying scale-aware edits inside the target region.
- **State-of-the-art CLIP alignment** on PIE-Bench with Infinity-2B (+1.07 over the strongest prior VAR editor), training-free, with complementary BitEdit/ResEdit roles verified by ablation.

## Method
BitResEdit couples two editing mechanisms at different stages of the generation pipeline. BitEdit operates at decision time: for each scale, it computes a source-target contrast in bit log-odds space from a shared prefix, scales the update, and clips it to a KL trust region around the clean CFG distribution. ResEdit operates at combination time: after bits are sampled, they are converted to per-scale code residuals; these residuals are spatially gated using a text-derived localization mask and re-injected through the generator's sum-of-scales reconstruction — leaving unmasked regions perfectly preserved by the inherent linearity of the residual code structure.

## Why It Matters
VAR models are emerging as competitive alternatives to diffusion for high-quality image generation; this paper demonstrates that their native discrete-Bernoulli and additive-residual structure enables a distinct class of training-free editing methods that can be cleaner and more controllable than diffusion-based editors for this model family.
