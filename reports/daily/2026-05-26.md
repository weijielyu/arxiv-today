### 1. Pantheon360: Taming Digital Twin Generation via 3D-Aware 360° Video Diffusion

**Score: 90/100** · [2605.25449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25449)

**Authors:** Ting-Hsuan Chen, Ying-Huan Chen, Tao Tu, Jie-Ying Lee, Cho-Ying Wu, Fangzhou Lin, Hengyuan Zhang, David Paz

## TL;DR
Pantheon360 proposes 3D-aware 360° video diffusion for complete digital twin generation, solving the limited field-of-view problem of perspective video generators via equirectangular representations with geometric conditioning and dual-anchor latent fusion.

## Problem
Generating complete digital twins from videos requires global scene coverage and strict spatial-temporal consistency — impossible with narrow-FoV perspective generators, which force long/multi-view trajectories and amplify cross-view inconsistency and temporal drift.

## Key Contributions
- First 3D-aware 360° video diffusion model for digital twin generation
- 3D Cache Reconstruction for geometric consistency across 360° frames
- Geometric latent conditioning (concatenation) + image feature conditioning (cross-attention)
- Dual-Anchor Latent Fusion for view interpolation between anchor frames
- Curated training pipeline combining real and synthetically annotated 360° video data

## Method
The model extends a video diffusion transformer to operate on equirectangular (360°) video. A 3D cache is reconstructed from the panoramic geometry and used to condition each frame via geometric latents concatenated with noisy video latents. Reference image features are injected via cross-attention. For interpolation between sparse anchor views, dual-anchor latent fusion blends latent representations from two anchors. Training uses a curated dataset with on-the-fly geometric annotation.

## Results
Outperforms perspective video generators on single/sparse-view 360° generation and 360° world model comparisons. Applications include 360° video stabilization and urban digital twin generation from sparse street-view data.

## Why It Matters
360° video is the natural representation for complete digital twins. By integrating 3D-aware geometry directly into the diffusion process, Pantheon360 enables scene-scale generation with global consistency — a key step toward immersive simulation and AR/VR digital twin pipelines.

### 2. Teaching Video Generators to Remember: Eliciting Dynamic Memory for Out-of-Sight State Evolution

**Score: 90/100** · [2605.25333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25333)

**Authors:** Tianshuo Xu, Yichen Xie, Depu Meng, Chensheng Peng, Quentin Herau, Bo Jiang, Yihan Hu, Wei Zhan

## TL;DR
ReMind elicits dynamic memory from pretrained video diffusion transformers by repurposing KV-cache mechanisms and projective memory positional embeddings, enabling world models to maintain and evolve hidden states for objects that leave the field of view.

## Problem
Video world models "freeze" when objects go out of sight — they cannot track evolving states of unobserved entities. Pretrained video diffusion transformers already have KV-cache mechanisms capable of non-local retrieval, but are never trained to use them as dynamic memory.

## Key Contributions
- ReMind: elicits dynamic memory from frozen video generation backbones without redesign
- Projective Memory Rotary Positional Embedding (PM-RoPE) for spatially-grounded memory retrieval
- Dynamic Memory with Streaming KV Cache for persistent state tracking
- Memory-oriented data construction pipeline with dynamic taxonomy and retrieval policy
- Training scheme combining dynamic auxiliary loss and masked reconstruction

## Method
ReMind repurposes the KV-cache of a pretrained video diffusion transformer. PM-RoPE spatially anchors memory tokens relative to their projected 3D positions. During training on specially constructed sequences (objects disappearing and reappearing with changed states), the model learns to retrieve and update memory tokens rather than hallucinating static appearances. A streaming KV cache stores memory states across autoregressive generation steps.

## Results
Strong improvements on out-of-sight state evolution benchmarks while maintaining general video generation quality. Attention map analysis confirms the model uses memory tokens during occlusion events.

## Why It Matters
World models need persistent memory to be useful for planning and simulation. ReMind shows that dynamic memory can be elicited from existing video generation architectures via targeted data and training — broad implications for building video-based world models for embodied AI.

### 5. Diff-Instruct with Diffused Reward: Towards Principled One-step Generator RL

**Score: 90/100** · [2605.24001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24001)

**Authors:** Junyi Wu, Weijian Luo, Haoyang Zheng, Runzhe Zhang, Guang Lin

## TL;DR
DIDR solves the terminal-reward domination problem in one-step T2I generator RL by "diffusing" the reward across the entire forward trajectory rather than evaluating only at the clean image — yielding principled, stable RL training with RLHF-target equivalence guarantees.

## Problem
One-step image generators trained with RL suffer from terminal reward domination: optimizing image-space rewards at the final denoising step destabilizes distribution matching and causes reward hacking and collapse.

## Key Contributions
- Formal analysis of terminal reward domination in one-step generator RL
- Reward-Tilted Trajectory Objective: propagates reward signal through the full noisy trajectory
- Diffused Reward Proxy: time-indexed reward computed at each noise level
- Teaching assistant training for practical implementation
- Theoretical guarantees: RLHF-target equivalence, score-based gradient derivations

## Method
DIDR extends Diff-Instruct by "diffusing" the reward: instead of evaluating only at the clean image, a diffused reward proxy evaluates the noised trajectory at each diffusion timestep. This time-indexed reward is consistent with the underlying forward process, eliminating terminal domination. A teaching assistant network stabilizes practical training.

## Results
Strong improvements on human preference alignment (PickScore, HPSv2) and text alignment (CLIP) over prior RL methods for one-step generators, including Flow-GRPO and DDPO baselines.

## Why It Matters
One-step image generation is increasingly important for real-time applications. DIDR provides a principled theoretical foundation for applying RL to flow-based generative models, and the diffused-reward principle generalizes to video and other modalities.

### 14. World Models as Group Actions

**Score: 85/100** · [2605.24578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24578)

**Authors:** Zijie Wang, Wei Zhang, Weiming Zhang, Fanqi Zhang, Xiao Tan, Yipeng Qin, Guanbin Li

## TL;DR
Formalizes action faithfulness in video world models via group theory: actions compose as group elements (SE(2) for navigation), and group-action training constraints enforce that generated world states respect compositional action structure.

## Problem
Visually realistic video world models don't guarantee genuine action faithfulness — they may look correct locally but fail compositionally (a sequence of turns doesn't produce the right viewpoint). Existing metrics don't test this.

## Key Contributions
- Formal framework: action-conditioned world model as group action (SE(2) for navigation)
- Group-Action Consistency (GAC) and Group-Action Robustness (GAR) evaluation metrics
- Group-action training constraints on free-running latent rollouts
- Demonstrated across multiple navigation datasets

## Method
Actions are treated as elements of a group, and the world model's transition function is trained to respect group composition: applying a sequence of actions equals applying the composed action. Training constraints enforce this on free-running latent rollouts.

## Results
World models trained with group-action constraints outperform baselines on GAC and GAR metrics, with improved qualitative behavior in multi-step rollouts.

## Why It Matters
The group-action formalism provides a principled mathematical framework for a fundamental world model requirement: action consistency. Relevant beyond navigation to any structured action space.

### 20. COSY: Compositional 3DGS Synthesis for Disentangled Human Head Editing

**Score: 80/100** · [2605.24114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24114)

**Authors:** Florian Barthel, Shalini De Mello, Koki Nagano, Wieland Morgenstern, Anna Hilsmann, Peter Eisert (Fraunhofer HHI / NVIDIA)

## TL;DR
COSY achieves disentangled 3DGS human head editing by synthesizing semantic components (hair, skin, glasses, torso) independently via a compositional generator trained with only sparse color supervision — enabling targeted edits without segmentation masks or geometric priors.

## Problem
3DGS GANs entangle semantic attributes in the latent space, making targeted edits cause unintended changes. Post-hoc disentanglement requires pre-trained classifiers and remains fragile.

## Key Contributions
- Compositional generator: each semantic component synthesized independently as its own 3DGS
- Sparse color supervision only (hair/skin color labels) — no segmentation or geometric priors
- EMA geometry loss for shape/lighting consistency at component boundaries
- Latent mixing for smooth attribute interpolation

## Method
A set of component-specific generators each synthesize their own 3D Gaussian primitives, composited in 3D. Sparse color labels supervise individual components. The EMA geometry loss aligns component shapes and lighting at composition boundaries.

## Results
Outperforms baselines on disentangled editing: changing hair color, glasses, or skin tone without affecting other attributes. Real-time rendering maintained.

## Why It Matters
Controllable, disentangled 3D head editing is essential for digital humans in film, games, and telepresence. COSY's sparse-supervision compositional approach is elegant and scalable.

### 21. AdvantageFlow: Advantage-Weighted Least Squares for RL in Flow Models

**Score: 80/100** · [2605.26013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26013)

**Authors:** Branislav Kveton, Anup Rao, Subhojyoti Mukherjee, Krishna Kumar Singh, Viet Dac Lai

## TL;DR
AdvantageFlow introduces advantage-weighted RL for rectified flow generation models via forward-process optimization, stabilized by rollout policy regularization — a principled and stable alternative to Flow-GRPO for human preference alignment of flow-based generators.

## Problem
RL for rectified flow models is unstable: Flow-GRPO optimizes the reverse process; forward-process advantage weighting becomes non-convex for negative advantages.

## Key Contributions
- Advantage-weighted least-squares RL for rectified flow models via forward process
- Rollout policy regularization stabilizing negative-advantage non-convexity
- Variance reduction via rollout-policy constraint
- Applicable to both image and video rectified flow generators

## Method
Optimizes an advantage-weighted forward-process prediction loss. When advantages are negative, the optimization landscape becomes non-convex; rollout policy regularization constrains policy deviation from its own rollout distribution, reducing variance and restoring stability.

## Results
Consistent improvements in human preference and text-image alignment over Flow-GRPO and other RL baselines, with better training stability.

## Why It Matters
RL alignment of flow-based models (the dominant generative architecture) is a key open problem. AdvantageFlow's forward-process approach provides a theoretically grounded, stable alternative.
