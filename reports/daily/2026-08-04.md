### 1. VC-Tooler: Learning Compositional and Adaptive Visual Tool Use
**Score: 85/100** · [arXiv:2608.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02217)  
**Authors:** Yizheng Wu, Jiashen Hua, Bing Deng, Jieping Ye  
**Project page:** https://w1zheng.github.io/VC-Tooler

## TL;DR
VC-Tooler teaches vision-language models to use visual tools compositionally and adaptively through hierarchical trajectory synthesis and two-stage training (supervised + RL), reaching 95.8% on V* and 35.3% on VTC-Bench — SOTA among open-source models on agentic visual benchmarks.

## Problem
VLMs trained on fixed tool sets memorize rigid invocation patterns instead of learning transferable tool-use skills: they fail to compose multiple tools across steps or adapt when the tool interface changes at inference time. The root cause is a data bottleneck — visual tool trajectories spanning diverse tools and multi-step interactions are scarce because they require executable environments and image-state transitions.

## Key Contributions
1. **Hierarchical trajectory bank** covering three capability levels: single-tool grounding, multi-tool composition, and diverse tool contexts/interfaces (hundreds of visual tool schemas and contextual variants), providing scalable supervision without hand-labeling.
2. **Two-stage training**: supervised cold start that establishes grounding + composition + adaptation; followed by reinforcement learning that rewards accurate, efficient, and context-aware tool use — encouraging the model to leverage tool-returned observations rather than ignoring them.
3. **Transfer to richer tool settings**: VC-Tooler generalizes to new tool interfaces unseen during training, demonstrating that the learned capability is compositional rather than schema-specific.

## Method
VC-Tooler treats visual tool use as a capability rather than a fixed protocol. A pipeline generates hierarchical trajectories: Level 1 trains single-tool grounding (selecting and parameterizing one tool from visual context); Level 2 trains multi-tool composition (chaining tools across steps); Level 3 trains adaptation (recognizing when tool outputs change the reasoning path). The two training stages are: (1) supervised fine-tuning on the trajectory bank establishes capability; (2) RL with process-level rewards on accuracy, efficiency, and observation utilization refines quality. At inference, VC-Tooler dynamically selects tools, executes them, and revises its plan based on returned observations.

## Why It Matters
Building agents that can flexibly acquire and refine visual evidence through tools is a core unsolved challenge for agentic AI. VC-Tooler addresses the compositional + adaptive gap that has held back prior work. The RL stage aligns the model toward cost-efficient tool use, a property critical for deployment. The approach is model-agnostic and generalizes beyond the training tool distribution.

### 2. UniMoCa: Unifying Motion and Camera Controls as Visual Proxies for Faithful Human Video Generation
**Score: 84/100** · [arXiv:2608.01944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01944)  
**Authors:** Liming Tan, Ye Chen, Hao Zhang, Lirong Qian, Feifei Li  
**Project page:** https://tanliming-daniel.github.io/UniMoCa/

## TL;DR
UniMoCa introduces the Motion-Camera Visual Proxy (MCVP) — a unified visual representation that converts 3D human motion and camera trajectories into a single visual space, resolving the incompatibility between pixel-aligned motion controls and parametric camera embeddings, yielding substantial gains in human video generation faithfulness.

## Problem
Current human video generation pipelines expose motion and camera through incompatible control signals: motion is given as pixel-aligned skeleton/pose maps while camera is injected as Plücker coordinate embeddings. This heterogeneous interface creates two problems: (1) the model must reconcile visual and non-visual cues to attribute changes to subject vs. camera; (2) errors in camera estimation propagate as raw perturbations in parametric space, making the model sensitive to estimation noise.

## Key Contributions
1. **Motion-Camera Visual Proxy (MCVP)**: converts 3D human motion and camera trajectories extracted from driving videos into identity-neutral rendered geometry under the recovered camera trajectory, augmented with explicit camera trajectory markers — making both controls distinguishable visual cues in the same space.
2. **MCVP-Video dataset**: curated corpus covering complex human actions, multi-person interactions, and diverse camera trajectories — fills the gap in training data for joint motion-camera control.
3. **Robustness to estimation errors**: because MCVP represents camera as visual proxy frames, moderate errors appear as mild deviations in scale/position rather than corrupting a parametric embedding, which the video diffusion model can absorb naturally.

## Method
From driving videos, 3D human pose and camera trajectory are estimated. The human geometry is rendered under the recovered camera view with temporal smoothing, producing MCVP frames. Camera trajectory markers are overlaid as additional visual cues, distinguishing camera motion from body motion. During training (built on Wan2.2 I2V), MCVP frames are fed as visual conditioning, replacing heterogeneous visual+parametric inputs. The identity-neutral rendering avoids contaminating the target appearance with estimation errors. Sample filtering suppresses high-frequency jitter.

## Why It Matters
Joint motion-camera control for multi-person scenes with occlusions and dynamic cameras is one of the hardest open problems in human video generation. UniMoCa's key insight — putting both control signals into the same visual space — is elegant and practical: it eliminates the architectural complexity of reconciling incompatible inputs while also making the system more robust. The approach generalizes to arbitrary driving videos.

### 3. Token Radius Attention for Efficient Video Generation
**Score: 84/100** · [arXiv:2608.02504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02504)  
**Authors:** Jiayu Chen, Zhikun Jiang, Maoliang Li, Jiayi Luo, Jiawei Yang, Zihao Zheng, Hengyi Zhang, Guojie Luo, Xiang Chen  
**Code:** https://github.com/IF-LAB-PKU/Token-Radius-Attention

## TL;DR
Token Radius Attention (TRA) is a training-free sparse attention framework for video diffusion transformers that assigns each query token its own entropy-derived attention budget and converts it into a spatiotemporal radius mask — retaining only 9–19% of attention interactions while achieving 1.56–2.05× speedup with competitive generation quality on Wan2.1/2.2 and HunyuanVideo.

## Problem
Dense 3D self-attention in video diffusion transformers (VDiTs) grows quadratically with token count, making it the primary inference bottleneck. Existing sparse methods (head-level or block-level) share a single computation budget across all queries within a group, ignoring that attention demand varies by orders of magnitude per token — wasting compute on concentrated queries and starving diffuse ones.

## Key Contributions
1. **Entropy-budget mapping (Insight I)**: retained attention density (fraction of top-k keys needed to preserve a target attention mass) correlates log-linearly with attention entropy, enabling token-specific budget estimation without per-query key ranking.
2. **Token radius pattern (Insight II)**: dominant keys form query-centered spatiotemporal circular neighborhoods with exponentially decaying attention probability, so a budget maps analytically to a spatial radius without explicit key sorting.
3. **Entropy-based warm-up reuse**: entropy is measured during early dense warm-up steps and reused across subsequent sparse steps, amortizing measurement cost.

## Method
During warm-up denoising steps, TRA computes attention entropy per token. Using the log-linear entropy–budget relationship, each token's entropy is mapped to a token-specific key budget. A temporally decayed radius model converts the budget to a spatiotemporal radius, forming block-sparse mask patterns. Fused CUDA kernels handle entropy extraction and radius mask construction efficiently. This pipeline runs without modifying model weights or retraining.

## Why It Matters
Efficient video generation is essential for scaling to longer, higher-resolution videos. TRA's per-token approach is principled and generalizes across VDiT architectures and tasks (T2V and I2V on three different models). The 1.5–2× speedup with ≤10% attention interactions computed is a strong result that directly enables practical deployment.

### 4. InfiniSplat: Implicit Gaussian Decoding for Large-Baseline Monocular View Synthesis
**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2608.02437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02437)  
**Authors:** Jiawei Wang, Hao Yu, Yongzhen Hu, Xinyi Yang, Tao Ni, Xin Zhan, Junbo Chen, Xiaowei Zhou, Ruizhen Hu, Sida Peng  
**Project page:** https://zju3dv.github.io/InfiniSplat

## TL;DR
InfiniSplat is a feed-forward single-image 3DGS framework that moves from pixel-aligned to surface-aligned Gaussian representation by coupling geometry-guided support sampling with query-conditioned implicit decoding, achieving SOTA novel view synthesis under large viewpoint shifts and zero-shot generalization to open-world scenes.

## Problem
Single-image feed-forward 3DGS methods predict Gaussians from fixed image-grid locations (pixel-aligned representation). While this produces plausible nearby-view renderings, it fails under large baseline viewpoint changes: the Gaussians behave as locally expanded splats rather than a coherent 3D surface, causing tearing, breaks, and severe geometric distortion when the camera moves far from the source view.

## Key Contributions
1. **Geometry-guided support sampling**: uses pretrained monocular depth to place 2D supports according to depth-induced local surface structure, breaking free from the regular image grid to produce surface-aligned scaffolding.
2. **Query-conditioned implicit Gaussian decoder**: a shared MLP queries fused DINO semantic + CNN texture features at each support coordinate and predicts Gaussian parameter updates — decoupling attribute prediction from fixed pixel centers.
3. **Zero-shot generalization**: trained on indoor synthetic (Hypersim), InfiniSplat generalizes to complex open-world scenes without fine-tuning, demonstrating that surface alignment is a domain-agnostic advantage.

## Method
Two-stage coupled core: (1) Geometry-guided sampling back-projects the predicted depth map to identify per-patch 3D surface areas, distributes supports proportionally to area, and initializes base Gaussians at each support. (2) Implicit Gaussian decoding queries a dual-branch encoder (DINO + CNN) at support coordinates, fuses via a gated mechanism, and predicts bounded offsets to all Gaussian parameters. The surface-aligned supports provide the geometric scaffold that learnable queries without geometric anchors cannot discover from single-view ambiguity alone.

## Why It Matters
Moving from pixel-aligned to surface-aligned 3D Gaussians is a fundamental advance for practical single-image 3D reconstruction. Applications include interactive spatial photo browsing, AR/VR head motion, and embodied AI that needs coherent 3D scene representations from monocular input. The zero-shot generalization validates the approach without dataset-specific tuning.

### 5. EchoCache: Energy-Guided Cross-Modal Caching for Efficient Audio-Driven Video Generation
**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2608.02474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02474)  
**Authors:** Jiayu Chen, Xiaoyu Wu, Rongshan Gao, Maoliang Li, Zihao Zheng, Xinhao Sun, Hailong Zou, Guojie Luo, Xiang Chen  
**Code:** https://github.com/IF-LAB-PKU/EchoCache  
*(MM '26)*

## TL;DR
EchoCache uses audio time-frequency energy as a cross-modal saliency anchor to identify which video latent clips need fresh computation vs. cached reuse, achieving 2.46× speedup on Wan2.2-S2V with best performance on the EMTD benchmark — a principled acceleration method specific to audio-driven video generation.

## Problem
Audio-driven video generation (A2V) inference is bottlenecked by iterative diffusion denoising. Existing caching methods exploit only visual temporal redundancy, but A2V is inherently a cross-modal alignment process where audio drives video generation non-uniformly: critical clips carrying local motion changes (high audio energy events) need fresh computation, while smooth/silent segments can safely reuse cached features.

## Key Contributions
1. **Cross-Modal Saliency Anchors**: models audio time-frequency energy distribution as a proxy for visual generation importance — high-energy audio segments (speech onset, rhythm changes) signal that corresponding video latent clips require full computation updates.
2. **Latent-level dynamic scheduling**: rather than making reuse decisions at the denoising timestep level, EchoCache operates at the latent clip granularity, allowing fine-grained allocation of compute to truly critical temporal positions.
3. **Quantized cache management**: reduces memory footprint of cached features through quantization, enabling joint efficiency and memory optimization.

## Method
Audio signals are transformed into time-frequency representations; energy is computed per temporal segment and mapped to cross-modal saliency scores. At each denoising step, clips with saliency above a threshold trigger fresh computation; others reuse cached latent features. A dynamic timestep-latent mechanism adapts reuse decisions jointly over denoising progress and temporal position. Quantized cache storage reduces memory overhead. The entire system is plug-and-play — no model retraining required.

## Why It Matters
A2V has critical applications in digital humans, virtual avatars, and cinematic content creation, but slow inference limits real-time deployment. EchoCache's key insight is that the audio signal itself carries the information needed to predict where visual compute matters most — a more principled and effective approach than purely visual-similarity-based caching. Generalizes across mainstream A2V models.
