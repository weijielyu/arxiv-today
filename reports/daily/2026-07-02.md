## Top Picks (Score ≥ 80)

### 88/100 — [2607.01131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01131) — Autonomous Scientific Discovery via Iterative Meta-Reflection

**Bingchen Zhao, Sara Beery, Oisin Mac Aodha**

*(University of Edinburgh; MIT)*

## TL;DR
DiscoPER is an LLM-powered autonomous scientific discovery framework that conducts truly open-ended research—starting from raw multimodal data without any pre-specified question—by formulating hypotheses as executable statistical tests and periodically reflecting on accumulated findings to redirect exploration toward uncharted regions of the hypothesis space.

## Problem
Current AI scientist systems either require predefined research questions/seed hypotheses or are restricted to pairwise-edge causal structures, and critically lack any mechanism to synthesize their accumulated discoveries and reason about what they collectively imply—the cumulative, reflective dimension central to how human researchers work. The result: systems that iterate hypotheses but never ask "what does everything we've found so far suggest we should try next?"

## Key Contributions
1. **DiscoPER framework** — formalizes autonomous discovery as a generalized **Propose→Evaluate→Reflect** loop operating over fully open executable hypothesis spaces (arbitrary Python statistical tests), subsuming prior systems (classical causal discovery, AI Scientist, ExperiGen) as special restricted cases.
2. **Meta-reflection (Reflect module)** — a second-order reasoning step that periodically analyzes the entire accumulated claim store (both accepted and rejected), identifies confounds, epistemic gaps, and under-explored variable combinations, and generates structured guidance that steers the next Propose round—without access to ground truth.
3. **iNatDisco benchmark** — a new multimodal ecological knowledge benchmark sourced from citizen science iNaturalist data, with expert-verified, peer-reviewed ground-truth patterns, enabling evaluation of fully open-ended discovery (no pre-specified research question, answer recoverable from raw data).

## Method
At each iteration, an LLM agent receives the dataset schema, sample rows, current accumulated claims (C_{t-1}), and guidance (G_{t-1}), and outputs K hypotheses—each consisting of a natural-language statement and accompanying Python code that implements a statistical test. **Evaluate** runs each hypothesis on a training split, then re-runs exactly once on a held-out validation split; a claim is accepted only if both effect size and p-value thresholds pass on both splits (dual-validation explicitly prevents p-hacking). Every T iterations, **Reflect** analyzes all accepted and rejected claims to produce structured guidance G_t—identifying which variables are under-explored, which confounds recur across accepted claims, and which compound hypotheses are suggested by the collective evidence—which then conditions the next Propose call. Tool use extends hypothesis exploration to multimodal sources (images), enabling discovery beyond tabular metadata. The system operates with P=∅: no prior guidance, no seed hypothesis, no pre-specified question.

## Why It Matters
Meta-reflection addresses a fundamental missing capability in automated discovery: the ability to reason at a higher level about what the accumulation of evidence implies, rather than treating each hypothesis step independently. On iNatDisco, DiscoPER recovers 8 of 9 expert-verified ecological patterns with a 72.7% hypothesis support rate, outperforming both classical causal discovery algorithms and LLM-guided baselines. Ablations confirm that reflection is the key driver of improvement (not just more compute) and that performance scales with more data. For researchers building AI scientist systems, this work provides a concrete, generalizable framework for autonomous open-ended discovery from raw multimodal data—and a new benchmark for measuring progress beyond task-specified question answering.
