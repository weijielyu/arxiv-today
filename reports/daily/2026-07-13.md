### 1. Video Generation Models are General-Purpose Vision Learners
**Score: 88/100** · [arXiv:2607.09024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09024)
**Authors:** Letian Wang, Chuhan Zhang, Rishabh Kabra, Jasper Uijlings, Steven Waslander, Andrew Zisserman, Joao Carreira, Kaiming He, Misha Andriluka, Eduard Gabriel Bazavan, Andrei Zanfir, Cristian Sminchisescu (Google DeepMind)
**Project:** https://genception.github.io

## TL;DR
GenCeption repurposes a large-scale text-to-video diffusion backbone as a general-purpose perception model via multi-task post-training on predominantly synthetic data, matching or surpassing specialized models like DepthAnything3, SAM3, and VGGT-Ω across depth estimation, surface normals, camera pose, segmentation, and 3D keypoints — with 7–500× less finetuning data.

## Problem
Computer vision remains in a "specialized model" era: separate architectures are engineered per task (depth, segmentation, pose, etc.), unlike NLP where next-token prediction collapsed thousands of tasks into a single generalist foundation model. The field lacks a unifying pretraining paradigm that provides spatiotemporal world priors, vision-language alignment, and scalability simultaneously.

## Key Contributions
- Establishes text-to-video generation as the visual analog of next-token prediction — uniquely satisfying all three imperatives for generalist vision intelligence: spatiotemporal world priors, native vision-language alignment, and data/compute scalability.
- Introduces GenCeption, a feed-forward perception model built on a pretrained video diffusion backbone, unified across diverse tasks through data format design and text instructions rather than task-specific architectural modifications.
- Achieves state-of-the-art or competitive results across depth estimation, surface normals, camera pose, expression-referring segmentation, and 3D keypoint prediction — outperforming specialized models with 7–500× less finetuning data.
- Reveals emergent behaviors: a model trained exclusively on synthetic human videos generalizes zero-shot to real-world footage and out-of-distribution object categories (animals, robots), without explicit supervision for either domain.

## Method
GenCeption treats a pretrained text-to-video diffusion backbone as a frozen feature prior and adapts it to feed-forward perception through multi-task post-training. The iterative diffusion process is converted to a deterministic single-forward-pass inference; tasks are specified through text instructions and output format design, enabling the same backbone to handle pixel-level tasks (depth, segmentation) and structural tasks (3D keypoints, camera pose) within a unified architecture. Training relies predominantly on scalable synthetic data generation, bypassing manual annotation bottlenecks. The video generative backbone outperforms the largest variants of alternative pretraining paradigms (V-JEPA, VideoMAE V2) under comparable settings, and exhibits preliminary data and model scaling properties.

## Why It Matters
This work provides compelling evidence that video generation is not merely a synthesis tool but the foundational pretraining paradigm for general-purpose vision. The 7–500× data efficiency advantage and emergent generalization suggest that scaling video generative models could collapse the fragmented landscape of specialized vision models into a unified foundation — reframing how the field should think about visual pretraining and model architecture for the next generation of computer vision systems.

### 2. Wan-Dancer: A Hierarchical Framework for Minute-scale Coherent Music-to-Dance Generation
**Score: 81/100** · [arXiv:2607.09581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09581)
**Authors:** Mingyang Huang, Peng Zhang, Li Hu, Guangyuan Wang, Bang Zhang (Tongyi Lab, Alibaba Group)

## TL;DR
Wan-Dancer breaks the ~20-second duration barrier in music-to-dance video generation through a hierarchical two-stage framework — global keyframe planning using full-track audio context followed by local temporal refinement — producing stable 720p/30fps dance videos exceeding one minute with rhythmic coherence across five dance genres.

## Problem
Diffusion-based video generation models fail beyond ~20 seconds due to quadratic attention complexity and compounding temporal coherence degradation. Music-to-dance generation specifically requires sustained long-range rhythmic alignment, identity consistency, and choreographic coherence — challenges that neither skeleton-based pipelines nor end-to-end video diffusion approaches have overcome at minute-scale durations.

## Key Contributions
- Hierarchical two-stage design that decouples global motion planning (full-track musical context → sparse keyframes) from local temporal refinement (keyframe-to-keyframe densification), enabling minute-scale coherence without the context window limitations of standard diffusion models.
- Dynamic frame rate adaptation via time-mapped RoPE embeddings for precise music-video temporal alignment across varying musical tempos and song durations.
- Optical-flow-based loss function combined with motion-speed control, maintaining high visual fidelity during rapid dance movements in the local refinement phase.

## Method
The global phase generates sparse keyframes conditioned on the entire audio track and text prompts (dance genre, style), providing long-horizon rhythmic anchors. The local phase fills frames between keyframes using temporally constrained diffusion guided by optical flow loss to enforce motion continuity. Built on the Wan video diffusion backbone. Supports LoRA-based customization for individual dance styles across five genres. Inference pipeline: global keyframe generation → temporal segmentation → local frame generation → final assembly. Training proceeds in two stages: low-resolution pre-training followed by high-resolution fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters
Wan-Dancer establishes hierarchical planning as a principled solution to long-form video generation — decomposing the hard global coherence problem from the local quality problem. The minute-scale capability at 720p/30fps opens practical doors for music video production and content creation, and the hierarchical framework could generalize to other long-duration human motion and video generation tasks where maintaining global structure over extended temporal horizons is essential.
