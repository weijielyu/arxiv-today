### 1. Orca: The World is in Your Mind
**Score: 83/100** | [arXiv: 2606.30534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30534)
**Authors:** Orca Team (Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence)

## TL;DR
Orca is a general world foundation model that learns a unified "world latent space" from multimodal signals via two complementary paradigms—unconscious learning from raw video and conscious learning from language-described events—then exposes this latent through lightweight, task-specific decoders for text generation, image prediction, and embodied action generation without retraining the backbone.

## Problem
Existing AI models are modality-siloed: LLMs do next-token prediction, video models do next-frame prediction, and robot policies do next-action prediction. None develop a general internal representation of world state capable of supporting multiple modalities and tasks. This fundamentally limits transfer, generalization, and the emergence of general intelligence from a unified world model.

## Key Contributions
- Proposes **Next-State-Prediction** as a unified modeling objective spanning text, vision, and action—subsuming next-token, next-frame, and next-action prediction into one state-transition framework.
- Introduces two complementary pre-training paradigms: **unconscious learning** (predicting next-frame latents from continuous video with no language supervision) and **conscious learning** (language-conditioned prediction of meaningful state transitions).
- Pre-trains on a large-scale inventory of **125K hours of video and 160M event annotations** covering egocentric interaction, robot execution, and event-level transitions.
- Demonstrates scalability: larger model / more data → lower pre-training loss → consistently stronger readout performance; frozen backbone outperforms similar-sized specialized baselines on text, image, and action benchmarks.

## Method
Orca uses an encoder–decoder architecture built on a pre-trained VLM (Qwen3-based). The **Encoder** learns world state through two objectives: (1) unconscious learning—MLP heads predict the latent of the next adjacent frame from the current frame's VLM output, with no semantic label; (2) conscious learning—given a language event description as condition, the model predicts the latent of the corresponding future (or past) state. Pre-training merges these two signals on the large-scale inventory. After pre-training, the encoder is frozen and **lightweight, modality-specific decoders** (for text, image, action) are independently fine-tuned as probes to evaluate latent quality. The frozen encoder is never updated during decoder training—the downstream performance improvement comes entirely from world latent quality, not decoder capacity.

## Why It Matters
Orca is arguably the most ambitious unified world modeling paper of the year: it trains a single model to understand, predict, and act across vision, language, and embodied action by treating all three as latent state-transition readouts. The frozen-encoder protocol provides clean evidence that world latent quality drives downstream capability. For this researcher's work on agentic AI systems, Orca's architecture is a direct reference design: it shows how a world model pre-trained on raw experience can serve as a zero-shot prior for autonomous agents without task-specific retraining.

### 2. Self-Evolving Agentic Image Restoration via Deliberate Planning and Intuitive Execution
**Score: 80/100** | [arXiv: 2606.28971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28971)
**Authors:** Shuang Cui, Fan Ji, Guanglong Sun, Yufei Guo, Xiongxin Tang, Jiangmeng Li, Fanjiang Xu (Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences)

## TL;DR
SEAR reformulates image restoration as long-horizon sequential decision-making and solves it with a dual-process architecture—a slow Deliberate Planner using Pruning-Aware Monte Carlo Tree Search plus an MLLM-based perceptual tournament, paired with a fast Intuitive Executor that reuses high-reward tool trajectories from a self-evolving episodic memory, overcoming the greedy myopia and episodic amnesia of prior agentic restoration systems.

## Problem
Real-world images suffer from complex, coupled degradations (blur + noise + compression artifacts simultaneously). LLM-driven agentic restoration systems that sequence repair tools are crippled by two structural failures: (1) **greedy myopia**—step-wise reward maximization leads to irreversible errors (e.g., aggressive denoising destroys structure needed for later super-resolution); (2) **episodic amnesia**—expensive MCTS search trajectories are discarded after each image, forcing identical cold-start exploration on similar inputs.

## Key Contributions
- **Deliberate Planner**: hierarchical reasoning with LLM for macro-level task scheduling (agenda-driven action space pruning) and Pruning-Aware MCTS for micro-level tool coordination—explicitly balances exploration and exploitation over long restoration trajectories.
- **Intuitive Executor**: maintains a self-evolving episodic memory indexed by degradation-aware state fingerprints; retrieves and replays previously discovered high-reward trajectories instead of replanning from scratch, amortizing cold-start cost progressively across all images processed.
- **MLLM-based perceptual tournament** as a no-reference reward, preventing adversarial metric exploitation (e.g., over-smoothed outputs that score well on PSNR but look bad).
- Dual-system synergy: fast System-1 intuitive execution when memory has a match; slow System-2 planning when not—with successful searches feeding back into memory.

## Method
SEAR is inspired by cognitive dual-process theory. The **Deliberate Planner** first parses the input via an MLLM to determine macro-level degradation structure and prune the tool-use search space. It then runs P-MCTS, which selects, expands, and backpropagates tool sequences evaluated by a hybrid no-reference reward (quality metrics + MLLM perceptual tournament). The **Intuitive Executor** computes a degradation-aware fingerprint from the current image state; if a similar fingerprint exists in episodic memory, it retrieves the corresponding tool trajectory and executes it directly. After execution, results are gated by reliability checks before being written into memory. Memory grows over a deployment lifetime, making subsequent restorations progressively faster and better.

## Why It Matters
SEAR advances the agentic AI paradigm by combining principled tree search with persistent episodic memory—two architectural choices applicable to any agentic pipeline where tool sequences must be optimized over long horizons (scientific experiment design, automated code repair, multi-step data analysis). The dual-process framing and self-evolution mechanism are particularly relevant to building AI scientist systems where expensive prior searches should accumulate as reusable expertise rather than being discarded.
