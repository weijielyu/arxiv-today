### 1. FR3D: Future Dynamic 3D Reconstruction — A 3D World Model with Disentangled Ego-Motion

**Score: 83/100** · [arXiv:2606.18250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18250)  
**Authors:** Nils Morbitzer, Jonathan Evers, Artem Savkin, Thomas Stauner, Nassir Navab, Federico Tombari, Stefano Gasperini  
Project page: https://fr3d-wm.github.io

## TL;DR
FR3D is a 3D world model that forecasts future dynamic 3D scene representations from monocular video by explicitly disentangling ego-camera motion from world-object motion, achieving geometrically consistent rollouts up to 2 seconds ahead with strong zero-shot generalization.

## Problem
Existing generative world models operate in 2D image space and entangle ego-motion with scene dynamics. This causes geometric inconsistencies — morphing or vanishing objects — especially over long horizons, making them unsuitable for agents that must reason about 3D space (autonomous driving, robotics).

## Key Contributions
1. **FR3D**: a 3D latent world model that predicts persistent 3D scene representations for future dynamic reconstruction, operating in latent 3D space rather than image space.
2. **Ego-motion disentanglement**: explicitly decouples the agent's camera trajectory from 3D scene evolution, treating inferred ego-motion as a latent action proxy — eliminating the self-motion vs. world-motion ambiguity.
3. **Teacher-student distillation**: leverages off-the-shelf 3D foundation models to inject spatial "common sense," enabling robust zero-shot generalization across multiple driving datasets without per-dataset fine-tuning.

## Method
FR3D represents the scene as a persistent 3D latent map and predicts future states using a masked transformer with autoregressive token prediction. The model factorizes scene dynamics into two streams: an ego-trajectory stream (predicted from camera poses) and a world-motion stream (inferred from scene content). Training uses a distillation loss that aligns FR3D's 3D latents with outputs from pretrained foundation models, providing strong 3D supervision without requiring dense ground-truth annotations. At inference, FR3D rolls out future 3D states conditioned on a history of monocular frames and an ego-motion prior.

## Why It Matters
Geometric integrity in world model rollouts is essential for planning agents — a world model that morphs objects cannot support reliable downstream reasoning. FR3D's disentanglement approach and zero-shot generalization represent a principled advance toward physically consistent 3D world models for autonomous agents and robotics, directly bridging interests in 3D reconstruction/generation (#4) and agentic AI (#1).

### 2. ActWorld: From Explorable to Interactive World Model via Action-Aware Memory

**Score: 83/100** · [arXiv:2606.17730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17730)  
**Authors:** Zhexiao Xiong, Yizhi Song, Hao Kang, Qing Yan, Liming Jiang, Jenson Yang, Zhoujie Fu, Stathi Fotiadis, Angtian Wang, Zichuan Liu, Bo Liu, Yiding Yang, Xin Lu, Nathan Jacobs *(ByteDance)*  
Project page: https://interactwm.github.io/ActWorld

## TL;DR
ActWorld extends video world models from navigation-only (walk/turn/look) to full object interaction (pick up, open doors, trigger physical responses) by introducing a hierarchical action-aware memory that prevents "action-forgetting," supported by a 100K annotated human-object interaction dataset.

## Problem
Existing interactive world models support only locomotion/viewpoint actions (WASD + camera), producing worlds that are "visually explorable but not truly actionable." Object-level interactions — picking up items, opening doors, manipulating tools — are absent or domain-restricted, blocking use as general training environments for embodied agents.

## Key Contributions
1. **100K interaction video dataset** with per-chunk chain-of-thought captions covering rich object interactions across diverse environments — addresses the data bottleneck.
2. **Hierarchical action-aware memory**: routes history compression by interaction importance, prioritizing event-transition frames that causally determine subsequent object states over redundant navigation frames — eliminates the action-forgetting pathology.
3. **Persistent memory bank**: maintains event-update and object-identity tokens across long rollouts, enabling consistent object state tracking beyond the local context window.

## Method
ActWorld builds on a chunk-autoregressive video generation backbone conditioned on keyboard/mouse inputs (Plücker rays + FiLM modulation). The hierarchical memory module distinguishes interaction-critical frames from navigation-only frames using a learned importance router, retaining high-importance frames in the persistent memory bank. DINOv3 object anchors ground object identity across frames. Training uses multi-resolution flow matching (stage 2) followed by backward simulation with teacher forcing (stage 3) for distillation.

## Why It Matters
True interactive world models that support object-level interaction are a key missing component for training embodied agents at scale. ActWorld bridges the gap between "game engine" (fully programmed interactions) and "neural world model" (flexible, learned interactions), enabling simulation of diverse manipulation scenarios without hard-coded physics. Directly relevant to video generation (#2) and embodied agentic AI (#1).

### 3. MoonSplat: Monocular Online Gaussian Splatting with Sim(3) Global Optimization

**Score: 81/100** · [arXiv:2606.17935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17935)  
**Authors:** Guo Pu, Yixuan Han, Haofeng Li, Yao Zhang, Hui Zhou, Zhouhui Lian *(Peking University; Beijing Hydrogen Intelligent Tech)*  
Code: https://github.com/TrickyGo/MoonSplat · **SIGGRAPH 2026**

## TL;DR
MoonSplat is an online monocular 3DGS system with Sim(3) global loop closure that achieves state-of-the-art camera tracking and rendering quality in real time, demonstrated via a deployed UAV active reconstruction system.

## Problem
Online 3D Gaussian Splatting from monocular video suffers from fragile camera pose estimation (no global optimization → scale drift and loop closure failures) and poor scalability in large-scale or long-sequence scenarios, limiting deployment in robotics and AR/VR.

## Key Contributions
1. **Sim(3) global optimization**: full scale-aware loop closure for both camera poses and the voxelized 3DGS map, eliminating scale drift that plagues monocular systems.
2. **Color residual learning**: initializes per-Gaussian colors from a base estimate and learns residuals, substantially accelerating voxel Gaussian convergence while improving rendering quality.
3. **UAV active reconstruction system**: real-world deployment of MoonSplat on a UAV that plans its own trajectory while reconstructing scenes in real time, validating robustness and generalizability.

## Method
MoonSplat organizes 3D Gaussians in a voxel grid for efficient local optimization. Camera tracking uses sequential PnP followed by a global Sim(3) pose graph optimization triggered on detected loop closures — this handles both rotational and scale drift from monocular cameras. When a loop is closed, both camera poses and Gaussian voxel blocks are globally re-aligned. Color residual learning treats Gaussian color initialization as a prior and adds a lightweight residual decoder, enabling faster per-step optimization without sacrificing expressiveness.

## Why It Matters
Accepted at SIGGRAPH 2026, MoonSplat addresses the core robustness bottleneck for real-world 3DGS deployment — drift-free monocular tracking at real-time rates. The UAV demo validates practical applicability for robotics and autonomous mapping. Directly relevant to 3D reconstruction (#4) with strong implications for embodied agents needing online scene understanding.

### 4. TivTok: Broadcasting Time-Invariant Tokens for Scalable Video Tokenization

**Score: 81/100** · [arXiv:2606.17590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17590)  
**Authors:** Weiliang Chen, Yuanhui Huang, Xuebo Wang *(Kuaishou)*, Yueqi Duan *(Tsinghua University)*

## TL;DR
TivTok separates video tokens into time-invariant (shared static content) and time-variant (per-frame residuals) components, achieving 2.91× better compression for 128-frame videos and rFVD of 12.65 on 16×256×256 while using only 1.1% of the tokens of standard tokenizers.

## Problem
Existing video tokenizers redundantly re-encode persistent content — static backgrounds, consistent object appearances — at every frame, wasting tokens and limiting the length and quality of video generation achievable within a fixed compute budget.

## Key Contributions
1. **Scope-Induced Factorization (SIF)**: assigns full-clip attention scope to TIV tokens (capturing shared content across all frames) and frame-local scope to TV tokens (encoding per-frame residuals), achieving disentanglement without explicit supervision.
2. **Invariant Broadcasting (IB)**: reuses the same TIV tokens across all frames and chunks for parallel reconstruction, enabling long-video tokenization without growing the TIV budget.
3. **2.91× compression efficiency** for 128-frame videos over baselines with competitive FVD (12.65 on 16×256×256); token count scales sub-linearly with video length.

## Method
TivTok uses a two-stream encoder: TIV tokens attend to the full clip via global self-attention; TV tokens attend only to their local frame together with TIV tokens. This design encourages TIV to capture temporally persistent content and TV to specialize in dynamic residuals. The decoder broadcasts TIV tokens to every frame position, then combines TIV + TV per-frame to reconstruct each frame in parallel. The SIF design allows training with standard video autoencoder losses without explicit disentanglement supervision.

## Why It Matters
Token efficiency is the central bottleneck for long video generation — more tokens directly mean shorter videos, higher cost, or lower quality. By broadcasting persistent content rather than re-encoding it, TivTok enables significantly longer, higher-quality videos at lower cost. This is a fundamental infrastructure improvement for scalable video generation (#2) and has natural extension to world models.

### 5. UniAR: Unified Multimodal Autoregressive Modeling with Shared Context-Visual Tokenizer is Key to Unification

**Score: 80/100** · [arXiv:2606.18249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18249)  
**Authors:** Wujian Peng, Lingchen Meng, Yuxuan Cai, Xianwei Zhuang, Yuhuan Yang, Rongyao Fang, Chenfei Wu, Junyang Lin, Zuxuan Wu, Shuai Bai *(Fudan University; Alibaba)*  
Project page: https://sharelab-sii.github.io/uniar-web

## TL;DR
UniAR unifies image understanding and generation in a single autoregressive model using one shared discrete visual tokenizer, enabling the model to directly interpret its own generated tokens without re-encoding — achieving state-of-the-art image generation and editing while remaining competitive on multimodal understanding benchmarks.

## Problem
Existing unified multimodal models use two separate visual tokenizers (one continuous for understanding, one discrete for generation), fragmenting the representation space and preventing the model from reading its own generated outputs — forcing expensive re-encoding at the understanding/generation boundary.

## Key Contributions
1. **Shared discrete visual tokenizer** with multi-level feature fusion and lookup-free bitwise quantization — preserves both high-level semantics (for understanding) and fine-grained pixel details (for generation quality) in a single quantized representation.
2. **Parallel-bitwise-prediction**: the autoregressive model jointly predicts spatially grouped, multi-level visual codes in parallel, substantially reducing sequence length and accelerating generation.
3. **State-of-the-art on image generation and editing** via a three-stage training pipeline (large-scale pretraining → supervised fine-tuning → RL alignment), competitive on multimodal understanding benchmarks.

## Method
UniAR adapts a pretrained vision encoder with multi-level feature fusion into a discrete quantizer using lookup-free bitwise quantization, scaling the effective visual vocabulary at minimal parameter cost. The unified autoregressive model uses parallel-bitwise-prediction to produce spatially grouped, multi-level codes in one forward pass per spatial group. A diffusion-based visual decoder maps discrete tokens to high-fidelity images. Training follows: (1) large-scale VLM pretraining on image-text pairs; (2) supervised fine-tuning on understanding + generation tasks; (3) RL alignment for generation quality and instruction-following.

## Why It Matters
The shared tokenizer eliminates the re-encoding bottleneck that limits unified multimodal systems, enabling seamless integration of understanding and generation within a single model. This architectural advance is directly relevant to image generation (#3) and has broader implications for unified generation-understanding systems central to the researcher's interests.
