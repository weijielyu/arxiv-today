## Top Picks

### 1. GS-Agent: Creating 4D Physical Worlds With Generative Simulation
**Score: 86/100** | [arXiv:2607.21522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21522)

**Authors:** Hongxin Zhang, Chunru Lin, Junyan Li, Zhou Xian, Tsun-Hsuan Wang, Chuang Gan (UMass Amherst, Genesis AI)

## TL;DR
GS-Agent is a multi-agent framework that generates physically realistic, controllable 4D dynamic worlds from natural language by integrating a physics engine in the loop, decomposing the task across a Manager Agent, Entity Agent, and Render Agent that iteratively collaborate to produce plausible interactions among liquids, deformable objects, and rigid bodies.

## Problem
Creating dynamic, physically plausible 4D worlds from text requires enormous manual effort—curating 3D assets, tuning material parameters, orchestrating object motion, setting up lighting and camera trajectories. Existing generative models either lack physical plausibility or require manual intervention at every step, and learning directly from data fails to ensure controllability or physical consistency.

## Key Contributions
- A multi-agent framework (GS-Agent) with three specialized agents (Manager, Entity, Render) that decompose 4D world creation into entity management and rendering configuration, coordinated through natural language and code.
- A physics-engine-in-the-loop design: agents interact with a physics simulator via code, receive multimodal feedback (RGB, depth, surface normals, dynamics), and iteratively self-correct—enabling autonomous error detection and recovery.
- Cinematic camera and lighting control achieved without human intervention, and emergent capabilities such as fine-grained compositional manipulation of liquids, deformable objects, and rigid bodies from a single text prompt.

## Method
GS-Agent instantiates three LLM-based agents with specialized tool sets over a physics engine (Genesis). The Manager Agent plans the scene decomposition from the input text. The Entity Agent writes simulation code to curate 3D assets from generative models, tune materials, place objects, and define motion trajectories. The Render Agent configures cameras and lighting. Each agent produces code that runs in the physics engine; the resulting simulation output is reviewed by the agents, which diagnose failures and revise their code until the result matches the description. No handcrafted templates or fixed scene graphs are required.

## Why It Matters
GS-Agent presents a compelling alternative to end-to-end neural 4D generation: instead of hallucinating geometry and physics, it grounds generation in simulation. The generated multi-modal output (depth, normals, dynamics) directly supports downstream embodied AI training and sim-to-real pipelines. For researchers in agentic AI, this is a concrete example of a multi-agent creative pipeline that achieves controllable physical plausibility—a capability still largely absent from purely neural generative systems.

### 2. SANA-Video 2.0: Hybrid Linear Attention with Attention Residuals for Efficient Video Generation
**Score: 84/100** | [arXiv:2607.21553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21553)

**Authors:** Junsong Chen, Jincheng Yu, Yitong Li, Shuchen Xue, Haozhe Liu, Jingyu Xin, Yuyang Zhao, Tian Ye, Zhangjie Wu, Zian Wang, Daquan Zhou, Ping Luo, Song Han, Enze Xie (NVIDIA)

## TL;DR
SANA-Video 2.0 is a hybrid video diffusion transformer (5B and 14B) that combines O(N) gated linear attention with periodic softmax anchor layers and cross-layer Block Attention Residuals, matching full-softmax video DiT quality at up to 120× lower latency on a single H100 at 720p.

## Problem
High-resolution long video generation with standard softmax attention is O(N²) in sequence length—punishing for video where a single 1080p clip spans tens of thousands of latent tokens. Pure linear attention is O(N)-efficient but loses the full-rank token interactions needed for fine spatiotemporal correspondence and detail. Existing approaches either sacrifice quality (pure linear) or speed (pure softmax).

## Key Contributions
- Hybrid Linear–Softmax Attention at a 3:1 ratio: gated linear attention for most layers with periodic gated-softmax "anchor" layers that restore full-rank token interactions without incurring quadratic cost globally.
- Block Attention Residuals (AttnRes): routes completed block summaries from softmax anchor layers into later linear layers via a learned router, boosting deep-layer effective rank by ~12% and enabling cross-depth feature reuse.
- From-scratch training (not linearization of pretrained weights) with content-aware flow-shift, online RL post-training, and full-stack Sol-Engine deployment (kernel fusion + caching + sparse attention)—achieving VBench 84.30 and 120× speedup over Wan 2.2-A14B at 720p/5s on one H100.

## Method
The 5B/14B SANA-Video 2.0 backbone uses a gated linear attention stack with softmax anchors inserted at a fixed 3:1 depth ratio. AttnRes modules route summarized block outputs from anchor positions to later linear layers via selective cross-layer routing queries. Training uses a quality/motion data funnel, timestep-stratified validation, self-flow distillation (weight EMA), and two post-training stages (DPO + online RL for preference alignment). Deployment further uses Sol-Engine kernel fusion, quantization-aware training (QAT), and compiled attention kernels.

## Why It Matters
SANA-Video 2.0 resolves the core tension in open video generation: being fast enough to run on commodity hardware while matching the quality of large closed models. Its 120× speedup over Wan 2.2-A14B at comparable quality, combined with open availability, directly lowers the barrier for video generation research and application. The hybrid attention principle and AttnRes mechanism are likely to influence next-generation video and multimodal architectures.

### 3. Inference-Time Scaling of Diffusion Models via Progressive Seed Pruning
**Score: 80/100** | [arXiv:2607.21591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21591)

**Authors:** Rogério Guimarães, Pietro Perona (California Institute of Technology) | [Project](https://www.vision.caltech.edu/psp) | [Code](https://github.com/rogerioagjr/psp)

## TL;DR
Progressive Seed Pruning (PSP) is a training-free inference-time scaling method for diffusion models that starts with a large pool of noise seeds, evaluates intermediate denoised estimates, and aggressively prunes poor trajectories—concentrating a fixed compute budget on the most promising candidates and consistently outperforming best-of-N, importance sampling, and tree search at matched FLOPs.

## Problem
Inference-time scaling for diffusion models is underdeveloped compared to LLMs. While seed quality strongly influences output quality, existing methods either fully denoise all seeds (best-of-N—wasteful), or apply importance sampling / tree search that maintain constant memory throughout, missing the opportunity to prune unpromising trajectories early.

## Key Contributions
- Progressive Seed Pruning (PSP): start with a large candidate pool, score intermediate predicted-clean images at multiple checkpoints, and prune candidates on a tunable schedule—keeping total model evaluations (FLOPs) fixed while front-loading exploration.
- A new inference-time scaling axis: relaxing the constant-memory constraint enables strictly better quality-compute tradeoffs than BoN, importance sampling (FK-Steering), or tree search at matched compute.
- Strong empirical results across diffusion (SDXL, SD3.5) and flow-matching backbones, with improved GenEval scores and human-evaluated prompt alignment; no backbone finetuning required.

## Method
PSP maintains a pool of noise seeds through denoising steps. At each pruning interval, it computes a reward estimate from the current predicted clean image x̂₀ using a black-box reward model (e.g., CLIP, aesthetic scorer). The bottom fraction of candidates is dropped on a predetermined schedule, and the surviving candidates receive the remaining denoising budget. The pruning schedule (intervals and survival fractions) is tuned via a small grid search on a held-out set. The method is fully plug-and-play—it wraps any denoising backbone without retraining.

## Why It Matters
As inference-time compute scaling becomes central to LLM deployment (chain-of-thought, tree-of-thought, best-of-N), PSP opens a parallel direction for image and video generation. It demonstrates that the analogy holds: more careful compute allocation at inference time yields quality gains without touching weights. For any application requiring quality-critical generation (image synthesis, video frames, 3D rendering), PSP offers a practical, backbone-agnostic upgrade path.
