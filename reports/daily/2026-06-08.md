### 1. Streaming Video Generation with Streaming Force Control

**Score: 86/100** · [arXiv:2606.07508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07508) · [Project](https://neu-vi.github.io/StreamForce/)

**Authors:** Hanhui Wang, Yiming Xie, Haiwen Feng, Zhaoyang Lv, Shenlong Wang, Huaizu Jiang

## TL;DR
StreamForce is a causal autoregressive video generation model that takes continuous force inputs (both local contact forces and global wind-like forces) as control signals, enabling users to steer generated video dynamics in real time — at 16.6 FPS on a single GPU — by modifying forces while watching the video unfold.

## Problem
Existing physically-grounded video generation falls into two failure modes: (1) methods like Force-Prompting train separate models for global and local forces, use only fixed forces during training, and require offline full-sequence generation — preventing interactive force adjustment; (2) causal autoregressive streaming models support real-time generation but are limited to kinematic trajectory control, not force-based physical interaction. No prior work simultaneously achieves unified force types, temporally varying forces, and causal streaming generation.

## Key Contributions
- **Unified force representation**: a shared pixel-aligned masked force map encodes both local contact forces (acting on a spatial region) and global environmental forces (whole-scene, e.g. wind) within a single formulation, eliminating the need for separate models per force type
- **Force-conditioned bidirectional teacher**: a high-capacity bidirectional diffusion model trained on Blender-rendered synthetic data with temporally varying force sequences, establishing strong force-to-motion correspondence including dynamic force transitions
- **Force-aware causal distillation**: a two-phase distillation pipeline (ODE initialization + Self-Forcing DMD distillation) that transfers the teacher's force-motion correspondence into a causal autoregressive student — with the key finding that naive distillation loses force responsiveness, requiring explicit force conditioning throughout the entire distillation process
- **Real-time streaming**: 16.6 FPS at 832×480 on a single H200 GPU with 0.6s latency, achieving state-of-the-art on force adherence and motion realism benchmarks including Physics-IQ

## Method
StreamForce follows a control-before-causality paradigm. Stage 1 trains a bidirectional teacher on synthetic force-conditioned clips; forces are parameterized as (magnitude, direction, spatial mask) and stacked into a 4-channel pixel-aligned tensor that handles both global and local types uniformly. Stage 2 distills this into a causal autoregressive student: the teacher generates ODE solution pairs from both synthetic force-conditioned data and diverse real-world images annotated with forces (dual-source initialization), then Self-Forcing DMD distillation fine-tunes the student with force conditioning applied at every stage. Users interact by specifying force vectors in real time; the causal model generates frames sequentially, incorporating updated force signals immediately.

## Why It Matters
Force control is a more natural and physics-faithful interface than trajectory specification: users express causes (push here with this strength) rather than effects (move there). StreamForce's real-time streaming capability turns video generation into an interactive physical simulation tool — directly relevant for video generation research (priority 2), with implications for world models, interactive content creation, and embodied simulation. The distillation paradigm for preserving controllability through causal conversion is a broadly applicable technique.
