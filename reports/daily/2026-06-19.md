### 1. Agentic AutoResearch for Space Autonomy: An Auditable, LLM-Driven Research Agent for Aerospace Control Problems
**Score: 83/100** · [arXiv:2606.20394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20394)  
**Authors:** Amit Jain, Richard Linares

## TL;DR
AutoResearch is an LLM-driven agentic framework that autonomously closes the hypothesis→experiment→analysis loop for aerospace control problems, paired with a built-in credibility layer that certifies each reported result against measured seed noise, reseeded verification, and leave-one-out edit pruning — producing auditable policies that outperform undirected search by statistically significant margins.

## Problem
Developing learned control policies for spacecraft guidance and navigation is a slow, labor-intensive research loop prone to over-claiming, since a single favorable run is easily mistaken for a robust result. Existing LLM-driven research agents (AI Scientist, FunSearch) can speed up experimentation but lack built-in statistical discipline to distinguish genuine improvements from seed-to-seed noise — making their results untrustworthy without external post-hoc auditing.

## Key Contributions
- **Reusable agentic experimentation framework**: a "family contract" (plain-language problem description + editable training script + single metric + append-only run log) that lets one LLM agent loop apply unchanged across aerospace problems with vastly different physics
- **In-loop credibility layer**: (1) per-problem seed noise measurement, (2) reseeded verification of the best configuration, and (3) leave-one-out pruning of individual edits — all run inside the agent loop, not bolted on afterward
- **Audited demonstrations** on CW relative rendezvous and safety-constrained collision-avoidance docking: the audited policy clears measured seed noise by many standard deviations; undirected parameter search yields no feasible policy on the harder docking task while the agent's policy holds the hard safety constraint on every seed

## Method
At each iteration the agent reads a natural-language problem description and the full append-only run history, proposes a single edit to the training script, executes it, and logs the outcome. No result is credited until passing three checks in sequence: the measured per-problem seed noise baseline, reseeded verification of the best configuration, and leave-one-out pruning confirming which specific edits carry the result. The LLM operates strictly offline as the research agent; the trained policy it produces is deployed onboard (the model itself never controls the spacecraft).

## Why It Matters
One of the first rigorous demonstrations of an LLM autonomously closing a real engineering research loop with statistical accountability built in. The credibility layer design — measure noise first, verify inside the loop, isolate contributing edits — is a directly exportable template for any LLM-driven research agent that wants to avoid the reproducibility failures plaguing manual ML research. The "family contract" abstraction is domain-agnostic; any problem expressible as a plain-language description + editable script + single metric can plug into the same loop. Directly and squarely relevant to the top priority area: agentic AI research and automated scientific discovery.

### 2. Through the PRISM: Preference Representation in Intermediate States of Video Diffusion Models
**Score: 80/100** · [arXiv:2606.20310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20310)  
**Authors:** Haoxuan Wu, Lai Man Po, Mengyang Liu, Kun Li, Hongzheng Yang, Wei Liu

## TL;DR
PRISM repurposes a frozen video diffusion backbone as a noise-aware video preference evaluator by adding a lightweight Query-based Aggregation head, achieving state-of-the-art preference accuracy from noisy latents without any VAE decoding — and enabling early-stage Best-of-N sampling that filters bad candidates at the very start of denoising.

## Problem
Current video reward models are external VLM-based evaluators that operate in clean pixel space, creating a fundamental architectural mismatch with video diffusion models: they cannot evaluate intermediate noisy latent states, force expensive VAE decoding of noisy signals (degrading evaluation quality), and cannot scale with the backbone generator since they are structurally separate. This blocks step-level RL and efficient inference-time scaling for video generation.

## Key Contributions
- **PRISM**: a latent video reward model that freezes the pre-trained video diffusion backbone and attaches a lightweight Query-based Aggregation head to decode preference signals directly from intermediate noisy latents — no VAE decoding required
- **First systematic evidence** that a video diffusion backbone's generative capability correlates strongly with its inherent evaluative power, suggesting a self-improving cycle: better generators → better built-in reward models
- **Early-stage Best-of-N sampling**: PRISM's noise-robustness enables filtering suboptimal candidates at the very beginning of denoising (high noise level), drastically reducing computation while boosting final video quality

## Method
PRISM takes the noisy latent z_t, text prompt c, and timestep t as inputs — matching the standard diffusion model interface exactly. A frozen pre-trained video diffusion backbone processes these inputs, producing intermediate spatial-temporal features. A Query-based Aggregation head extracts and decodes preference signals from the high-dimensional noise-corrupted features. Training uses preference ranking and alignment objectives. At inference no full denoising or VAE decoding is needed; rewards are computed directly in latent space at any noise level.

## Why It Matters
The core architectural insight — that a video generator already encodes a rich "blueprint of the natural video manifold" and can evaluate quality from noisy states — has broad implications for video generation alignment. It enables step-level RL, efficient early rejection in Best-of-N sampling, and self-improving feedback loops where better generators produce better reward models. As video generation scales and alignment becomes a bottleneck, eliminating the expensive and architecturally-mismatched external VRM is a meaningful practical advance. Directly relevant to video generation research.
