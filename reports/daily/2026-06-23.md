### Score: 88/100 — [VideoAgent: All-in-One Framework for Video Understanding and Editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23327)

**Authors:** Hengji Zhou, Lingxuan Huang, Jian Wang, Bing Zhou, Si Wu, Lianghao Xia, Chao Huang

## TL;DR
VideoAgent is a multi-agent orchestration framework that unifies video understanding and editing — spanning shot planning, cross-modal retrieval, and 30+ specialized editing agents — achieving 87–95% task success while reducing API costs by 60% and producing content rated only 4% below human-created videos.

## Problem
Existing automated video editing systems are limited to short-segment tasks and domain-specific workflows. They lack (1) the ability to handle diverse comprehension and editing operations in a single system, and (2) the long-video understanding needed to create coherent narratives across multiple shots. Professional video creation demands hierarchical planning, diverse tool orchestration, and multimodal coordination across extended content.

## Key Contributions
- **All-in-one agentic video framework** that unifies understanding and editing across diverse genres (news, commentary, music video, stand-up) into a single end-to-end pipeline — the first system to do so at production quality.
- **Shot Planning Agent with cross-modal retrieval**: a top-down narrative planner that decomposes goals into shot-level specifications and retrieves aligned visual/audio content from large libraries using global semantic and temporal understanding.
- **Multi-Agent Graph Orchestration with textual-gradient optimization**: 30+ specialized editing agents (visual processing, audio, knowledge analysis) are dynamically assembled into non-linear workflow graphs; intent parsing selects relevant tools and textual-gradient optimization adapts the graph structure without manual pipeline specification.

## Method
VideoAgent operates in two stages. First, a Shot Planning Agent generates coherent narrative structures from high-level goals, dividing them into shot plans and retrieving semantically aligned clips via cross-modal retrieval with global content awareness. Second, these plans are passed to a Multi-Agent Orchestration layer where an intent analyzer selects relevant agents from a library of 30+ specialized tools (video editors, face swappers, audio processors, content generators). A textual-gradient graph optimization procedure assembles and refines the workflow graph, propagating feedback through the pipeline to fix errors and adapt to task-specific constraints. The full system is trained/prompted via LLM backbones and evaluated on a newly proposed VideoEdit benchmark plus standard public datasets.

## Why It Matters
VideoAgent directly addresses the researcher's priority interest in agentic AI frameworks: it demonstrates that a multi-agent graph with 30+ tools and LLM-based orchestration can perform real-world, long-horizon video creation tasks that single-model approaches cannot handle. The textual-gradient graph optimization is a broadly applicable technique for adapting multi-agent pipelines. The near-human-level performance on diverse video genres, combined with the 60% API cost reduction, makes this a strong existence proof that agentic orchestration is production-viable for creative video workflows.

**Code:** https://github.com/HKUDS/VideoAgent

### Score: 82/100 — [World Action Models: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20781)

**Authors:** Qiuhong Shen, Shihua Zhang, Yue Liao, Qi Li, Zhenxiong Tan, Shizun Wang, Shuicheng Yan, Xinchao Wang

## TL;DR
A comprehensive taxonomic survey of World Action Models (WAMs) — embodied predictive-action models that couple future prediction with action — organizing the field across predictive substrate, backbone architecture, action coupling, and deployment regime, and identifying the emergent principle: "dream less, act more."

## Problem
The boundary between world models, video generation models, action-grounded video world models, VLA policies, and WAMs has blurred as the field expanded rapidly. Works from video-generation, robot-learning, and language-model communities use the "World Action Model" label inconsistently, making it difficult to compare methods, identify design trade-offs, or situate new work. A principled organizing framework is missing.

## Key Contributions
- **Two-level taxonomy**: (1) a design-philosophy taxonomy (Render-and-Decode / Latent-Only / Video-Generation-Free) capturing where action is decoded along the inference path, and (2) a component-level anatomy decomposing each WAM into four axes — predictive substrate, architectural backbone, action coupling, and deployment regime — with a unified 4-tuple notation.
- **Five core properties framework** (Interactability, Causality, Persistence, Physical Plausibility, Generalization) that ground abstract design choices in concrete embodied-AI requirements.
- **Open-challenge synthesis**: identifies seven open problems — including the dream-less-act-more design tension, data sourcing at each training stage, memory scalability, generalization across embodiments, and evaluation blind spots — providing a research roadmap for the next generation of WAMs.

## Method
The survey conducts a census of WAMs, organized into Tables tracking video-generation-based families (Render-and-Decode, Latent-Only) and Video-Generation-Free methods separately. Each paper is analyzed along the four anatomy axes and placed in the philosophy taxonomy. The survey homepage at https://world-action-models.github.io/ maintains a live version of the tables. Data, evaluation practices, and benchmark coverage are critically assessed, identifying what current metrics miss (long-horizon coherence, physical plausibility beyond visual fidelity).

## Why It Matters
This survey is essential reading for anyone working at the intersection of video generation and agentic AI — both of the researcher's top interests. It consolidates a rapidly growing space (dozens of papers in 18 months) into a navigable map, clarifies the design trade-offs between rich pixel-level futures and efficient latent-space foresight, and identifies the data and evaluation gaps that limit current systems. The "dream less, act more" principle is a directly actionable design heuristic for building efficient agentic world models.

**Survey Homepage:** https://world-action-models.github.io/

### Score: 80/100 — [Go-with-the-Track: Video Compositing and Motion Control with Point Tracking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20891)

**Authors:** Koichi Namekata, Yash Kant, Zhizheng Liu, Ryan Burgert, Yuancheng Xu, Kuan Heng Lin, Emmett Steven, Julien Philip, Li Ma, Andrea Vedaldi, Paul Debevec, Ning Yu (Netflix / Eyeline Labs / University of Oxford / UCLA / Columbia)

## TL;DR
Go-with-the-Track unifies video compositing and motion control in a single video diffusion model by extending conventional point-tracks to multi-reference-anchored tracks, enabling multiple reference images to be composited into generated video with precise spatiotemporal control throughout all frames — not just the first.

## Problem
Filmmaking requires two things that existing video generation methods handle separately: (1) precise motion control over how elements move across frames, and (2) compositing of reference images (characters, props) that may enter or exit at arbitrary points in the video. Point-track-conditioned models fix all controllable elements to the first frame; multi-reference models lack explicit motion control. Neither can handle the full creative workflow.

## Key Contributions
- **Multi-reference anchored point-tracks**: extends the definition of point-tracks beyond in-video 2D flow to explicitly link each track to coordinates on multiple reference images, enabling compositing + motion control to operate jointly for elements that aren't in the first frame.
- **Spatially-aware point-track embeddings**: a coordinate-wise MLP followed by temporal pooling that encodes full point-track sequences such that embedding similarity correlates with spatial proximity — enabling the model to associate up to 15,000 conditioning tracks without the slow convergence and poor generalization of random embedding identifiers.
- **Hybrid training strategy** across dynamic, static, and synthetic video datasets that enables a single model to handle motion-preserved restylization, mesh/keypoint-driven compositing, and camera motion control for both static and dynamic scenes.

## Method
Reference images and multi-reference-anchored point-tracks (tracks linking both video frames and reference image coordinates) are fed jointly to a video diffusion transformer. Spatially-aware embeddings are injected via a lightweight adapter that resolves the pixel-to-patch resolution mismatch. Training combines dynamic video datasets (for motion diversity), static scenes (for camera control), and synthetic point-track data. At inference, a user provides reference images and point-tracks; the model composites each reference at the specified location and time according to the track, maintaining appearance consistency throughout. Published at SIGGRAPH 2026.

## Why It Matters
Video compositing with motion control is a core enabler for video generation workflows that produce narratively complex content — enabling references to enter, move, interact, and exit precisely as intended. By solving the motion/compositing unification problem in a single diffusion model, this work removes a critical barrier to professional-quality automated video production, directly relevant to the researcher's interest in video generation. The spatially-aware track embedding technique is a broadly useful primitive for any point-track-conditioned video model.

**Project Page:** https://eyeline-labs.github.io/Go-with-the-Track/
