### 1. Wonder: Video World Model Done Better
**Score: 93/100** | [arXiv:2607.26037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26037) | [Project](https://wonder-world-model.github.io/)

**Authors:** Jiacong Xu, Hanwen Jiang, **Zhixin Shu**, Kalyan Sunkavalli, Vishal M. Patel, Yiqun Mei
*(Adobe Research, Johns Hopkins University)*

Notable: **Zhixin Shu** is an author.

## TL;DR
Wonder is a general-purpose video world model that enables real-time, camera-controllable world exploration from a single image or video, synthesizing minute-scale videos at 16 FPS with coherent geometry and persistent long-term scene memory — while simultaneously achieving accurate camera following, open-ended exploration, and stable latency.

## Problem
Existing video world models fail to simultaneously achieve all three necessary properties for interactive use: (1) accurate camera control, (2) long-horizon coherent scene memory, and (3) real-time low-latency inference. Prior work addresses each property in isolation, but the three impose conflicting demands on model architecture and training. Systems that densely attend to historical KV states grow in latency with rollout length; compressed or sliding-window memory loses scene fidelity; abstract camera embeddings drift after distillation.

## Key Contributions
- **Pixel-space coordinate field for camera control**: Renders a synthetic 3D scaffold along the target camera trajectory, converting camera pose into dense per-frame visual evidence (translation, rotation, parallax as rendered pixel cues) the model can interpret directly — more stable across few-step distillation than abstract Plücker embeddings or pose-vector conditioning.
- **Sparse full-fidelity memory**: Retains the full historical KV cache in high resolution but uses lightweight pooled query-key summaries to select a constant-size set of relevant memory chunks at each rollout step — enabling faithful scene revisit and persistent geometry without linearly-growing active attention cost.
- **Diversity-preserving distillation**: A timestep-wise mixture-of-students (increasing effective student capacity without changing the streaming interface) and a camera-aware GAN regularizer suppress diversity collapse and camera drift during bidirectional-to-causal few-step distillation.

## Method
Wonder uses a two-stage teacher-student pipeline: (1) a camera-conditioned bidirectional video diffusion teacher trained on a curated dataset of real-world navigation videos and large-scale synthetic UE/Blender renderings, annotated with Depth Anything 3 (DA3) camera poses; (2) a few-step causal autoregressive streaming student distilled via sparse context forcing — a co-designed training strategy that stably activates the sparse-memory capability by conditioning the student on the same retrieved-memory chunks it faces at inference. Both image-to-world and video-to-world (re-shooting existing dynamic scenes along new camera trajectories) modes are unified in a single model. The key departure from prior work is treating control, memory, and distillation as a coupled system rather than three separate components.

## Why It Matters
Wonder directly addresses the central unsolved challenge in video world modeling: achieving camera accuracy, long-term memory, and real-time latency simultaneously. The system generates minute-scale explorable worlds at 16 FPS with stable per-step latency as history grows — a concrete milestone for interactive AI, game creation, and virtual production applications. The project comes from Adobe Research with collaborator Zhixin Shu, signaling strong industrial production potential.

### 2. Parallel Decoding Distillation for Fast Image and Video Generation
**Score: 88/100** | [arXiv:2607.26004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26004) | [Project](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/genair/pdd)

**Authors:** Neta Shaul, Chao Liu, Arash Vahdat, Julius Berner
*(NVIDIA, Weizmann Institute of Science)*

## TL;DR
Parallel Decoding Distillation (PDD) is a clean trajectory-based distillation method that predicts multiple diffusion/flow denoising steps per network evaluation via a parallel decoder, achieving state-of-the-art quality at 4–8 NFEs on Wan 14B text-to-video, Qwen-Image 20B text-to-image, and LTX-2.3 text-to-video/audio — while substantially improving video diversity compared to VSD/GAN-based baselines.

## Problem
SOTA acceleration methods for video generation rely on variational score distillation (VSD) and adversarial losses. Despite achieving high visual quality, these methods are notoriously hard to optimize, suffer from mode collapse, and produce videos with reduced temporal diversity and lack of motion — limiting their practical utility for content creation. Prior trajectory-based methods avoid these issues but have not scaled to video models. The computational bottleneck of many-step inference remains a key blocker for real-time video generation and interactive world models.

## Key Contributions
- **Parallel decoder architecture**: Reuses the pre-trained model's backbone unchanged, but appends N learnable final linear heads (one per time-grid step) instead of one. A single forward pass at state X_n predicts mean velocities for all L intervals in the current block simultaneously — enabling L× fewer sampling steps without multi-stage training or time-conditioning changes.
- **Simple single-objective MSE training**: Regression onto Runge-Kutta approximations of the teacher's per-interval mean velocities, with on-policy evaluation via stop-gradient. Avoids Jacobian-vector products, finite differences, adversarial losses, and alternating objectives — all sources of instability in prior methods.
- **Layer fusion at inference**: The N linear heads are pre-fused into a single weighted-average head for each block size L before deployment, adding zero inference overhead compared to the original model structure.

## Method
PDD discretizes the flow/diffusion time domain into N intervals grouped into blocks of L. For a current state X_n, the parallel decoder predicts mean velocities for all intervals in the block via one forward pass; the block-step rule advances the state by L intervals using the sum of those predictions. Variable block sizes (L_min to L_max) are trained jointly by sampling k uniformly within each block, giving a single model that supports different NFE counts at generation time without re-training. The method is architecture-agnostic: validated on class-conditional ImageNet-256, Qwen-Image (text-to-image), Wan2.1 1.3B/14B (text-to-video), and LTX-2.3 (text-to-video/audio).

## Why It Matters
Distribution-based distillation (VSD+GAN) currently dominates video generation but produces mode-collapsed, motion-poor outputs. PDD provides a practically viable trajectory-based alternative — simpler to implement, more stable to train, and producing demonstrably more diverse videos — directly enabling real-time interactive video generation and more efficient content pipelines. Coming from NVIDIA Research (Arash Vahdat leads generative AI research there), this is likely to become an influential reference method for video diffusion acceleration.
