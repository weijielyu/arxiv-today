### [2608.03979] Video-DeepResearch: Towards the Next-Generation Multimodal Deepresearch Agent

**Score: 87/100** · [arXiv:2608.03979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03979) · [GitHub](https://github.com/Osilly/Vision-DeepResearch)

**Authors:** Zhen Fang, Yu Zeng, Wenxuan Huang, Yiming Zhao, Shiting Huang, Tianfei Ren, Qi Lu, Qingnan Ren, Qisheng Su, Lionel Z. Wang, Qingyu Yin, Shuang Chen, Zehui Chen, Lin Chen, Zhenfei Yin, Yao Hu, Shaohui Lin, Wanli Ouyang, Shaosheng Cao, Feng Zhao

## TL;DR
The first framework for autonomous video deep research—extending multimodal research agents from static images to continuous video streams—that compels exhaustive visual grounding before web search and trains with GRPO to exceed the imitation-learning ceiling, achieving 64.0% on VideoDR-Bench and outperforming Claude-4.5-Sonnet (59.0%) and GPT-5 (52.5%).

## Problem
Existing multimodal deep research agents fail in the video setting due to two systematic bottlenecks: (1) modality bias—agents default to textual search while bypassing visual perception tools; and (2) parametric knowledge leakage—models generate answers from internal memory rather than executing genuine tool-augmented retrieval. No training pipeline or evaluation benchmark existed for this complex video-grounded research setting.

## Key Contributions
- A decoupled perception-exploration pipeline with stage-wise tool unlocking that enforces exhaustive cross-frame visual grounding before web retrieval is permitted
- A two-stage training recipe (SFT → GRPO) that teaches agents to adaptively route across video grounding, web search, and answer synthesis, breaking through the imitation-learning performance ceiling
- VideoDR-Bench: a human-AI collaborative benchmark of 200 complex multi-hop VQA instances over continuous video streams, requiring temporal localization, cross-modal reasoning, and open-web evidence synthesis

## Method
The pipeline enforces a strict unlocking order during inference: visual perception tools (cross-frame grounding, temporal localization) are exhausted first; only afterward can web retrieval tools be accessed. During training, the SFT phase establishes strong baseline behavior on curated VQA trajectories. The RL phase then fine-tunes with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), rewarding both answer accuracy and appropriate tool routing—enabling the agent to discover search strategies that supervised data cannot capture. Benchmark construction pairs human-annotated multi-hop questions with AI-assisted answer verification, ensuring genuine spatiotemporal reasoning is required.

## Why It Matters
Video-DR-35B-A3B achieves 64.0% average accuracy on VideoDR-Bench, setting a new SOTA that surpasses Claude-4.5-Sonnet by 5 points and GPT-5 by 11.5 points. This is the first end-to-end system that treats video archives as a primary research substrate for agentic AI, directly advancing the frontier of autonomous systems that can reason, retrieve, and synthesize across temporal visual data. For a researcher whose top priority is agentic AI systems, this defines a new benchmark category and a concrete architecture to build upon.

### [2608.03974] JoyAI-Video-Edit: Real-Time Open-Ended Video Editing with Autoregressive Diffusion

**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2608.03974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03974) · [GitHub](https://github.com/jd-opensource/JoyAI-Video-Edit)

**Authors:** Yicheng Xiao, Wenxun Dai, Xinran Qin, Lin Song, Maoquan Zhang, Hang Xu, Yukang Chen, Yitong Li, Guohui Zhang, Yuan Zhang, Xuying Zhang, Tommy Zhang, Jianlong Yuan, Peihao Li, Shuai Lu, Siming Fu, Chuyang Zhao, Xin Han, Jie Huang, Wenbo Li, Guoqing Ma, Wei Huang, Xiaojuan Qi, Haoyang Huang, Nan Duan (Joy Future Academy, JD)

## TL;DR
A 16B-parameter autoregressive diffusion framework for real-time video editing at 720p/30 FPS on a single GPU, achieving streaming video editing without future frame access or predefined video length, using distillation techniques to prevent temporal drift over unbounded streams.

## Problem
Real-time video editing requires causal generation—processing frames as they arrive without seeing future content—while maintaining bounded memory and producing stable output over arbitrarily long video streams. Offline editors rely on bidirectional temporal attention and grow in memory with video length; simply causalizing them introduces a train-inference mismatch where the model conditions on clean history during training but its own noisy predictions at inference, causing reconstruction errors to accumulate into visible long-horizon drift.

## Key Contributions
- Chunk-wise autoregressive adaptation of a pretrained video generation model for causal streaming editing, enabling open-ended video editing without bounded duration or future frame access
- Source-Anchored Distribution Matching Distillation (SA-DMD), which preserves source fidelity during two-step distilled generation by anchoring the student's distribution to the source signal rather than the multi-step teacher
- Long-Horizon Autoregressive Distillation that trains the model on longer sequences than standard chunk-size, bridging the gap between training and deployment conditions on unbounded video streams

## Method
The 16B model processes video in fixed-length chunks, conditioning each chunk on the source video segment and the bounded edited history via a causal attention mask. SA-DMD aligns the distilled model's output distribution to the source video while matching the quality of the full multi-step teacher—preventing the two-step approximation from drifting away from the source content. Long-Horizon Distillation uses extended training sequences so the model experiences its own prediction errors during training, building robustness to accumulating drift. Deployment requires only bounded KV-cache across the rolling window.

## Why It Matters
JoyAI-Video-Edit achieves 720p video editing at approximately 30 FPS on a single Nvidia B200 GPU—the first streaming video editor competitive with offline systems on both short and long video benchmarks. This makes real-time video editing practical for live broadcasting, interactive entertainment, and content creation. For video generation research, this work closes the gap between offline generation quality and the real-time deployment requirements that limit practical use.

### [2608.02711] Hunyuan3D-Buffalo 1.0: A Unified Multimodal Model for Scalable 3D Generation, Understanding, and Editing

**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2608.02711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02711) · [Project Page](https://tencent-hunyuan.github.io/Hunyuan3D-Buffalo1.0/)

**Authors:** Junliang Ye, Kenkun Liu, Guocun Wang, Yang Li, Yansong Qu, Chunshi Wang, Jingwei Xu, Yunhan Yang, Zibo Zhao, Jiachen Xu, Jiaao Yu, Lifu Wang, Zhihao Liang, Zhuo Chen, Chunchao Guo (Tencent Hunyuan)

## TL;DR
Tencent's first unified 3D multimodal model integrating understanding, text-to-3D generation, instruction-guided editing, and part generation in a single architecture trained on an 87M-sample 3D corpus—demonstrating that all four tasks mutually reinforce each other.

## Problem
While unified 2D multimodal models (GPT-4o, FLUX, Seedream) have shown compounding benefits from combining understanding, generation, and editing, unified 3D modeling has stalled because of two data bottlenecks: the scarcity of large-scale paired 3D data and the near-absence of geometrically consistent 3D editing data, preventing the construction of training corpora at the scale needed for unified learning.

## Key Contributions
- A single unified architecture supporting four 3D tasks: semantic/spatial understanding, text-to-3D generation, instruction-guided editing, and text-grounded part generation
- An 87M-sample 3D multimodal corpus: 25M understanding samples, 50M text-to-3D pairs, and 12M editing pairs auto-generated through a Nano3D-v2 pipeline with hierarchical prompt synthesis and multi-tier captioning
- A two-component architecture: Hunyuan3D-VLM for semantic understanding and condition generation, paired with Hunyuan3D DiT for high-fidelity 3D synthesis and editing

## Method
Hunyuan3D-VLM processes text instructions and provides semantic hidden states as conditioning signals for the 3D DiT; for editing and part generation tasks, the DiT additionally conditions on the source object's latent representation to preserve unedited regions. Corpus construction involves five automated stages per data type—for editing data: anchor view selection, instruction planning, voxel editing, fine-grained geometry/texture editing, then annotation and filtering. Training progresses through four stages: 3D-VLM training → T2-3D pretraining → omni-pretraining (all four tasks jointly) → continued pre-training, with each stage building stronger cross-task representations.

## Why It Matters
Hunyuan3D-Buffalo achieves state-of-the-art on T2-3D generation and 3D editing benchmarks, and ablations confirm that joint training across all four tasks improves each one—the generation capability particularly benefits editing accuracy. The 87M-scale 3D corpus is the largest reported for this purpose and demonstrates an automated pipeline for generating diverse, geometrically consistent 3D training data at scale. This is the most comprehensive 3D generative modeling system to date and directly advances the frontier of unified 3D generation that the researcher follows.

### [2608.03779] AgenticVAU: Multi-Agent Explore-Verify Reasoning for Video Anomaly Understanding

**Score: 81/100** · [arXiv:2608.03779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03779)

**Authors:** Yuxiang Duan, Huining Li, Ao Li, Shuai Feng, Lanju Kong, Ning Liu, Jian Zhang, Xingdong Sheng, Yuntao Du

## TL;DR
A training-free multi-agent framework for video anomaly understanding that decomposes reasoning into four specialized agents—visual-rule construction, search planning, video observation, and decision—coordinated through a shared anchor registry, outperforming RL-based baselines on VAU-Bench without any fine-tuning.

## Problem
Video anomaly understanding (VAU) requires not just detecting an anomaly but also localizing evidence and explaining the cause—a multi-faceted task that current methods address poorly. Learning-based methods depend on specialized training and struggle with unseen anomaly types. Single-agent systems integrate exploration, observation, and decision-making in one pass, preventing role specialization and enabling evidence from different temporal regions to conflict without structured resolution.

## Key Contributions
- A training-free multi-agent framework that separates video anomaly understanding into distinct roles: visual-rule construction, temporal search planning, multi-granularity video observation, and final evidence-aggregated decision
- An anchor registry—a shared evidence memory structure that binds each observation to spatial-temporal anchors, enabling structured cross-agent coordination between exploration and verification
- An explore-verify loop with principled termination: broad temporal exploration first, then dense local verification and cross-interval comparison, continuing until sufficient evidence accumulates

## Method
The system begins with an Initial Analysis agent that produces visual rules (criteria for what counts as anomalous) from a brief first pass, and a Prior Scan agent that generates a temporal search plan. The Explore-Verify loop then runs iteratively: a Search Planning agent dispatches observation agents to targeted time intervals, multi-granularity Video Observation agents collect visual evidence, and that evidence is stored in the anchor registry with spatial-temporal bindings. A Loop Control mechanism terminates when evidence crosses a sufficiency threshold. The final Decision agent aggregates all registry entries to produce the output. All agents are frozen VLM instantiations with no task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters
AgenticVAU outperforms both zero-shot inference baselines and RL-trained approaches on three VAU-Bench subsets (ECVA, UCF-Crime, MSAD), demonstrating that principled multi-agent role specialization adds measurable value over monolithic inference—without any task-specific training. This is directly relevant to the researcher's top priority on agentic AI research: the explore-verify decomposition and anchor registry pattern offer a generalizable template for how to structure multi-agent video understanding systems beyond anomaly detection.
