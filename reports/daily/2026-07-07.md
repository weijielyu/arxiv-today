### 1. VideoSearcher: Empowering Video Deep Research with Multi-Tool Agentic Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning
**Score: 88/100** · [arXiv:2607.02927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02927) · [Project Page](https://stephen-gzk.github.io/VideoSearcher-website/) · [GitHub](https://github.com/Stephen-gzk/VideoSearcher)

**Authors:** Zhenkun Gao, Yicheng Bao, Jinlong Peng, Xueheng Li, Theo Huang, Bangwei Liu

## TL;DR
VideoSearcher is a closed-loop agentic framework that combines temporal localization, spatial focusing, and multimodal web search in a single reasoning trajectory, trained with a novel Bi-branch RL algorithm to jointly optimize tool use and answer accuracy — defining and then tackling "Video Deep Research" as the canonical next frontier in open-world video understanding.

## Problem
Current video understanding assumes a closed-context setup: the answer must be found within the provided video clip. But real user questions often require information that is not visible in the video — who is this person, where is this place, what happened before? Existing multimodal search agents are image-centric and the prior Video Deep Research (VDR) benchmark relies on text-only retrieval, discarding visual evidence. This leaves a major open problem: how does a VLM dynamically seek visual and textual evidence from the open web to answer knowledge-intensive video questions?

## Key Contributions
- **VideoSearcher framework**: unifies temporal localization (identifying when), spatial focusing (identifying where/what), and multimodal web search (retrieving external evidence) in a single agentic reasoning trajectory, enabling progressive evidence grounding.
- **Bi-branch Sequence Policy Optimization (BiSPO)**: a novel RL algorithm that decouples the tool-invocation signal (did the agent call the right tool, at the right time?) from the answer-accuracy signal, providing stable gradients for both distinct learning objectives simultaneously.
- **VideoSearch-QA benchmark**: the first benchmark designed to evaluate open-world video information grounding, requiring models to retrieve multimodal evidence from the web rather than reason purely over in-context frames.
- Significantly outperforms open-source agentic baselines across search-oriented and multimodal video understanding benchmarks; BiSPO's decoupled signal design is key to stable RL convergence.

## Method
VideoSearcher is built on a VLM backbone extended with three tools: a temporal localizer (returns relevant frame segments), a spatial focuser (crops and zooms into regions of interest), and a multimodal web search engine (retrieves image+text evidence from the web). The reasoning loop operates as a closed-loop: the model formulates a reasoning step, selects and invokes a tool, receives evidence, and continues — until it synthesizes a final answer. Training uses a two-phase approach: cold-start SFT on curated search trajectories, then BiSPO RL. BiSPO assigns tool-invocation rewards (based on whether retrieved content was relevant) separately from outcome rewards (based on final answer correctness), allowing independent optimization of the two orthogonal skills. VideoSearch-QA was constructed with a video-centric data pipeline that pairs questions with pre-verified answers requiring web evidence not deducible from the video alone.

## Why It Matters
Video Deep Research formalizes a crucial capability gap — VLMs that can only reason over a closed clip will always hit a knowledge wall. VideoSearcher shows that RL can teach a model to learn when to trust its own memory vs. when to search, making this the first principled agentic approach to open-world video understanding. The BiSPO design principle (decouple tool policy from answer policy) is directly applicable to any agentic task where tool use and downstream reasoning are distinct skills. For automated research agents, the closed-loop "search → ground → reason" pattern is exactly what document/evidence search in scientific pipelines requires.

### 2. Search Beyond What Can Be Taught: Evolving the Knowledge Boundary in Agentic Visual Generation
**Score: 85/100** · [arXiv:2607.05382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05382)

**Authors:** Haozhe Wang, Weijia Feng, Jinpeng Yu, Che Liu, Ping Nie, Fangzhen Lin, Jiaming Liu, Ruihua Huang, Jimmy Lin, Wenhu Chen, Cong Wei

## TL;DR
Visual generators confidently fabricate entities they have never seen; this paper formalizes the world-knowledge bottleneck in agentic visual generation, builds SearchGen-20K/Bench (20K prompts, 12 failure categories), and introduces a co-training recipe where a generator's own knowledge boundary is discovered to decide when to trust internal knowledge vs. when to invoke external search — yielding monotonic, selective improvement.

## Problem
Text-to-image generators fail on long-tail, post-training-cutoff, or trending content: new characters, real-world entities, recent events. Frontier open generators score only 21–28 out of 100 on SearchGen-Bench, a 40-point collapse relative to their in-distribution performance. Naive retrieval augmentation (inject search results into the prompt) makes things worse: it adds noise for prompts the generator already handles well. The root cause is that each generator has a model-specific, evolving "knowledge boundary" — the divide between what it has internalized and what must come from external context.

## Key Contributions
- **SearchGen-20K and SearchGen-Bench**: 20,839 prompts spanning 12 failure categories and 22 domains, paired with a 1M-item multimodal SearchGen-Corpus for offline, reproducible research; exposes a blind spot invisible to standard T2I benchmarks.
- **Knowledge boundary discovery**: rather than pre-specifying what a generator knows, a noise-resistant agentic reasoner identifies the boundary empirically during co-training — the model learns to self-classify which requests it can handle internally.
- **Teach-then-Search co-training**: Phase 0 trains a SFT gating/filtering/integration pipeline; Phase 1 fine-tunes the generator with DPO on preference data; Phase 2 refines the reasoner via rejection finetuning — together driving monotonic improvement on SearchGen-Bench.
- Dataset, code, and SearchGen-Corpus released as a replayable harness for world-knowledge-grounded visual generation research.

## Method
The co-training framework has three stages executed jointly over a VLM reasoner and a diffusion generator. The reasoner is a gating model that classifies each incoming prompt into three categories: (A) "I know this — generate directly," (B) "I need filtered search results," (C) "I need full integration of search results." For stage A, the generator uses internal knowledge; for B and C, a noise-resistant search pipeline retrieves relevant reference images and integrates them into the generation conditioning. The knowledge boundary (the decision surface separating A from B/C) is itself learned through SFT and then refined by rejection finetuning on the generator's own failure signals. DPO preference data is constructed by comparing images with and without correct world-knowledge grounding. Iterative co-training cycles gradually push the boundary to cover more of the long-tail.

## Why It Matters
The world-knowledge bottleneck in visual generation is structural and cannot be solved by more training data alone — the visual world is open-ended and continuously evolving. This paper provides the first principled framework for teaching a generator to know what it doesn't know and to seek help selectively. The benchmark fills a critical measurement gap: SearchGen-Bench is now the standard way to evaluate whether a visual generator can handle real-world, open-ended, knowledge-grounded requests. The "teach-then-search" pattern is a broadly applicable recipe for any generative AI system that must remain current without full retraining.

### 3. Homer: Understanding Long-form Videos with Hierarchical Memory and Agentic Reasoning
**Score: 85/100** · [arXiv:2607.02588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02588) · [GitHub](https://github.com/Dereck0602/Homer)

**Authors:** Yixin Ji, Fanghua Ye, Juntao Li, Bo Zhao, Zexuan Qiu, Zhaopeng Tu, Liefeng Bo, Min Zhang

## TL;DR
Homer introduces a three-level hierarchical memory (perceptual buffer → entity episodic graph → event causal graph) and an agentic reasoner that explores memory the way humans do — locating the scene, looking up entity details, composing multi-hop answers — outperforming the best prior agent method by +5.5 to +10.8 points on long-video benchmarks.

## Problem
Hour-long videos require reasoning over thousands of frames that cannot all be held in a VLM's context window. Existing online methods either (a) compress frames into compact visual representations that lose semantic structure, or (b) build higher-level memory organized by temporal proximity rather than explicit causal and entity relationships. This means every query must re-derive complex multi-hop narrative reasoning from scratch, and models systematically fail at questions that require tracking entities across discontinuous segments or reasoning about causal event chains.

## Key Contributions
- **Three-level hierarchical memory**: a perceptual buffer (key frame crops), an EntityGraph (recurring entities with episodic and semantic attributes), and an EventGraph (events connected by explicit temporal and causal relation edges) — together mirroring the multi-scale structure of long videos.
- **Agentic reasoner**: explores the memory hierarchy through multi-round retrieval — locating relevant events, looking up entity details, and composing answers with verification and self-correction at each step.
- **Model-agnostic**: consistently improves three different LLM backbones (Gemma, Qwen, LLaMA), indicating the hierarchical memory structure is the source of the gain rather than backbone-specific tuning.
- Sets new state-of-the-art on M3-Bench-robot (+5.5), M3-Bench-web (+10.8), and Video-MME-Long (+4.4) over the best prior agent method.

## Method
Homer processes video frames in an online streaming fashion. At the lowest level, a key-frame extractor populates a perceptual buffer with visual crops. An entity tracker identifies recurring objects and people, building an EntityGraph where nodes carry appearance crops, role descriptions, and episodic timestamps. Above this, an event detector links temporal segments and identifies causal dependencies between events (e.g., "Person A's action at t=120s caused outcome B at t=300s"), forming an EventGraph with typed directed edges. At query time, an agentic reasoner operates as a multi-round retrieval loop: it decomposes the question into sub-questions, issues graph queries against the EntityGraph and EventGraph, retrieves evidence, and verifies each retrieved fact before composing the final answer. A harness module flags self-inconsistencies and triggers corrective re-retrieval.

## Why It Matters
Long-form video understanding is the key bottleneck for real-world AI systems — surveillance, training data curation, procedural task assistance, and embodied intelligence all require processing hour-long streams. Homer demonstrates that the right architectural inductive bias (explicit entity+event graphs rather than flat temporal memory) provides large, consistent gains across diverse benchmarks and backbones. The agentic reasoner pattern (decompose → retrieve → verify → compose) is directly applicable to scientific literature review agents where multi-hop reasoning over structured knowledge graphs is the core challenge.

### 4. MV-Forcing: Long Multi-View Video Generation via 4D-Grounded Spatio-Temporal Self-Forcing
**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2607.05376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05376) · [Project Page](https://galfiebelman.github.io/mv-forcing/)

**Authors:** Gal Fiebelman, Hadar Averbuch-Elor, Sagie Benaim

## TL;DR
MV-Forcing generates long, geometrically consistent multi-view videos of dynamic scenes at arbitrary viewpoint counts and temporal lengths by using an autoregressive 3D reconstruction model as a 4D geometric bridge between sequentially generated video views, eliminating the fundamental tension between temporal length and multi-view consistency.

## Problem
Existing video diffusion models can either generate long single-view videos (via temporal autoregression) or short multi-view clips (via bidirectional attention), but not both simultaneously. Dynamic scene video generation across multiple consistent viewpoints for arbitrary duration is entirely unsolved — the 3D geometric constraints needed for view consistency and the temporal autoregression needed for length are architecturally incompatible in current approaches.

## Key Contributions
- **4D geometric bridge**: after generating a source-view video segment, an autoregressive 3D reconstruction model (used inference-only) reconstructs the 3D structure and renders a geometric prior for the next viewpoint — grounding the diffusion model's generation in explicit 4D geometry rather than implicit learned priors.
- **Joint denoising regime**: both the source and target view slots are initialized from noise during training, decoupling the model from the teacher's fixed temporal window and enabling temporally unbounded generation at inference.
- **Spatio-Temporal Self-Forcing via Distribution Matching Distillation**: closes the train-inference exposure bias gap simultaneously for both temporal and view-sequential autoregression — a critical challenge since exposure bias accumulates in two dimensions.
- Evaluated on synthetic and real-world data; produces coherent, geometrically consistent multi-view videos with a single few-step student model.

## Method
MV-Forcing frames long multi-view video generation as interleaved spatio-temporal autoregression within a single diffusion model. Given a text prompt and a sequence of camera trajectories, the model generates the first video segment for view 0. An off-the-shelf 3D reconstruction model then lifts this clip into 3D and renders a depth/geometry image for view 1's camera — this "geometric prior" is fed as conditioning into the diffusion model for the next view. This process alternates between generating video segments and computing geometric bridges, enabling an arbitrary number of views and frames. Distribution Matching Distillation with Spatio-Temporal Self-Forcing is applied to distill the multi-step process into a fast few-step student, while the joint denoising training regime prevents exposure bias from compounding across the long generation chain.

## Why It Matters
Multi-view consistent video generation of dynamic scenes is the missing capability connecting video generation with 3D/4D content creation. The 4D geometric bridge idea is elegant: rather than forcing the diffusion model to learn 3D consistency implicitly, it explicitly supplies geometric priors from a proven reconstruction system, separating concerns cleanly. This work directly enables applications in virtual production, 3D-aware world model training, and dynamic scene reconstruction — all critical for the researcher's interest areas of video generation (priority 2) and 3D reconstruction & generation (priority 4).

### 5. Flex-Forcing: Towards a Unified Autoregressive and Bidirectional Video Diffusion Model
**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2607.03509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03509) · [Project Page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/genair/flex-forcing/)

**Authors:** Xinyin Ma, Julius Berner, Chao Liu, Arash Vahdat, Weili Nie, Xinchao Wang (NVIDIA)

## TL;DR
Flex-Forcing unifies bidirectional and autoregressive video diffusion through a flexible chunking mechanism over both temporal frames and denoising timesteps — enabling a single model to trade off speed vs. quality dynamically, generate videos of arbitrary length with stable quality, and perform any-order any-timestep video editing; accepted at ICML.

## Problem
Video diffusion models are locked into a fixed inference paradigm at training time: bidirectional (all frames attend to all frames) gives high quality but slow inference and no streaming capability, while autoregressive (each frame conditions only on past frames) enables streaming but suffers from long-range inconsistency and exposure bias. No prior approach lets a single model operate in both regimes flexibly based on compute budget or use case.

## Key Contributions
- **Flexible chunking over frames**: partitions a video into chunks of configurable size; within each chunk, frames are generated bidirectionally (for local coherence), while chunks are stitched autoregressively (for unbounded length and efficient streaming).
- **Flexible chunking over denoising timesteps**: within a chunk, earlier denoising steps can use bidirectional attention (global structure planning) while later steps use autoregressive attention (fine-grained synthesis) — composing two regimes within a single denoising trajectory.
- **Any-order, any-timestep autoregressive editing**: since the causal constraint is relaxed by the flexible chunking design, the model can generate or edit frames in arbitrary temporal order, enabling novel editing applications impossible in strictly causal models.
- Outperforms strong baselines on standard video generation benchmarks for both 5s and 30s videos, with faster inference and better long-video stability.

## Method
The core innovation is a training objective where the model simultaneously sees multiple chunking strategies: sometimes the full video is bidirectional (teacher-like), sometimes each frame autoregressively conditions on all prior frames, and sometimes an intermediate chunk size is used. A K-projection mechanism aligns the noise levels across causal and non-causal attention paths within the same training example, preventing gradient conflicts from the mixed supervision signal. At inference, the user selects a chunking configuration matching their device budget — large chunks for quality, smaller chunks for memory efficiency and streaming. For video editing, the any-timestep variant allows arbitrary frames to be denoised at any noise level, enabling targeted editing without full regeneration.

## Why It Matters
The bidirectional-vs-autoregressive tension is the central architectural tradeoff in video generation today. Flex-Forcing resolves it elegantly by treating the generation paradigm as a hyperparameter rather than a hard design choice, enabling a single trained model to serve diverse deployment scenarios — from high-quality offline generation to real-time streaming — without retraining. The any-order editing capability is practically valuable for interactive video production. The NVIDIA/ICML venue validates the technical depth of the contribution.

### 6. Vidu S1: A Real-Time Interactive Video Generation Model
**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2607.03118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03118) · [Demo](https://vidu.com/vidu-stream)

**Authors:** Jintao Zhang, Kai Jiang, Jintao Chen, Xu Wang, Yang Luo, Yuji Wang

## TL;DR
Vidu S1 achieves real-time interactive video generation at up to 42 FPS on consumer GPUs by combining TurboDiffusion (for fast sampling) and TurboServe (for efficient serving), supporting infinite-length generation with voice-driven character control, custom image uploads, and best-in-class quality metrics.

## Problem
Existing video diffusion models are fundamentally offline: they generate a fixed-length clip, which the user plays back passively. Real-time interactive generation — where the user can issue control commands mid-generation and see the video respond immediately — requires latency and throughput far below what current DiT-based systems achieve. Prior attempts sacrifice quality, resolution, or length to meet latency constraints.

## Key Contributions
- **TurboDiffusion**: a fast sampling method enabling high-quality video generation in significantly fewer steps than standard diffusion, operating in 540p without quality regression.
- **TurboServe**: an inference serving optimization that pipelines generation stages for consumer GPU deployment, achieving up to 42 FPS real-time output.
- **Voice-controlled character interaction**: users can issue natural language voice commands at any moment during generation to redirect character behavior, creating a genuinely interactive experience.
- **Infinite-length generation**: avoids the temporal drift and visual distortion that limits prior streaming approaches — Vidu S1 maintains visual coherence indefinitely.
- Accepts custom image uploads of real people, anime, or pets; achieves best performance across all reported quality metrics.

## Method
Vidu S1 builds on a DiT-based video generation backbone optimized by TurboDiffusion, which combines distillation and adversarial training to reduce the required denoising steps. TurboServe implements a production serving stack that overlaps diffusion step computation with frame decoding and display, achieving sub-frame latency per generated frame on consumer GPUs. The interactive control loop operates via a voice recognition module that transcribes user commands and injects updated conditioning signals mid-generation using an asynchronous conditioning mechanism that does not interrupt the denoising pipeline. Custom images are encoded by a fine-grained identity encoder compatible with the DiT conditioning architecture.

## Why It Matters
Real-time interactive video generation is the logical endpoint of video generation research — moving from passive clip synthesis to genuine user-directed interactive worlds. Vidu S1 is the first deployed system to demonstrate this at consumer GPU latencies with high visual quality. The combination of real-time generation, infinite length, and multi-modal control (voice + image upload) represents a step function in interactivity relative to prior systems. This directly advances the frontier of both video generation (priority 2) and interactive agentic systems.

### 7. Track the Noise, Move the World: 3D-Grounded Motion-Consistent Noise for Controllable Video Generation
**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2607.02798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02798)

**Authors:** Long Vu, Tan Ngo, Animesh Karnewar, Amir Habibian, Binh-Son Hua, Hung Bui, Minh Hoai Nguyen, Phong Nguyen-Ha (Qualcomm AI Research)

## TL;DR
UniCaMo achieves simultaneous, precise control over both object motion and camera movement in video generation by directly constructing the diffusion model's input noise in 3D — warping noise along object point tracks and sampling globally-consistent noise from a virtual sphere for camera motion — requiring only lightweight LoRA fine-tuning on Wan 2.1 (14B).

## Problem
Current video generation models control either object motion (via text, bounding boxes, or point tracks) or camera motion (via trajectory descriptions), but not both jointly and precisely in a single generation process. The two control signals interact in 3D space — an object moving right while the camera pans left requires understanding both in a shared 3D reference frame — but existing methods operate in 2D pixel space where this coupling is intractable.

## Key Contributions
- **Track-the-Noise (object motion)**: constructs a 3D-consistent noise tensor by warping the reference frame's Gaussian noise along desired 3D object trajectories (defined by sparse 3D point tracks), preserving noise statistics while encoding the intended motion direction at the diffusion model's input level.
- **Move-the-World (camera motion)**: represents the entire scene as a virtual sphere in 3D space; noise values for newly revealed scene regions under camera movement are sampled from this sphere's surface, ensuring global consistency in the camera direction.
- **Unified 3D noise space**: the two mechanisms operate in the same 3D representation, enabling combined object + camera control without conflict — a key architectural advantage over prior modular approaches.
- Works as a plug-in to any pretrained video diffusion model (demonstrated with Wan 2.1 14B); achieves state-of-the-art on MoveBench for both video quality and motion controllability.

## Method
Given a reference image and desired trajectories (2D object tracks + camera path), UniCaMo first lifts the reference frame to 3D using a monocular depth estimator, creating a 3D point cloud. For object motion control, 3D point tracks are used to warp patches of the reference frame's sampled Gaussian noise along the desired trajectories (patch copy-paste along 3D trajectories). For camera motion control, a virtual sphere centered at the scene is constructed; for each novel view introduced by camera movement, ray-sphere intersection identifies the sphere-UV coordinates, and the globally-consistent sphere noise at those coordinates is sampled and downsampled to the video latent resolution. The combined noise tensor is fed as the initial noisy input to the video diffusion model; lightweight LoRA layers adapt the model to respect the structured noise conditioning.

## Why It Matters
Precise joint control over object and camera motion is the essential missing ingredient between video generation as "creative tool" and video generation as "3D-aware world modeling." UniCaMo's insight — that the diffusion model's initial noise tensor encodes spatial structure, and that 3D-grounded noise construction can directly impose motion geometry — bypasses the need for complex architectural modifications or additional control encoders. The result (applied to Wan 2.1 14B) is immediately practical for video production workflows where precise motion choreography is required.

### 8. Incentivizing Vision Language Models to Search for Long Video Question Answering (VSeek)
**Score: 82/100** · [arXiv:2607.02959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02959) · [Project Page](https://utaustin-swarmlab.github.io/VSeek)

**Authors:** Harsh Goel, S P Sharan, Sahil Shah, Minkyu Choi, Joungbin An, Kristen Grauman

## TL;DR
VSeek transforms long-video QA from a single-pass perception task into an agentic multi-turn retrieval process, trained with RL using neuro-symbolic rewards derived from formal temporal logic specifications — improving Pass@1 by up to 8% and Pass@4 by 15% on long-video benchmarks.

## Problem
Long-video QA is fundamentally different from short-clip QA: the answer-relevant content is sparsely distributed across a potentially hour-long video, and single-pass models either process all frames (computationally intractable) or subsample heavily (missing relevant content). RL-based reasoning has transformed math and code tasks, but its application to long-video understanding is hindered by the absence of verifiable dense rewards — outcome-only rewards (correct/wrong answer) provide too sparse a training signal for the multi-step retrieval behavior that long-video QA requires.

## Key Contributions
- **VSeek agentic framework**: iteratively formulates natural-language search queries targeting specific temporal segments within the video, retrieves relevant clips, and reasons over the retrieved evidence — transforming a passive perception task into an active evidence-seeking loop.
- **Neuro-symbolic reward design**: complex natural language questions are compiled into formal temporal logic specifications that enumerate atomic visual primitives (key objects, activities, orderings); these provide dense, verifiable RL rewards based on whether retrieved context grounds the required primitives.
- **Formal temporal logic for reward**: bridges open-ended NL questions and discrete visual verification — the formal spec acts as a "ground truth checklist" enabling dense RL feedback that is model-agnostic and does not rely on learned preference models.
- Improves Pass@1 by up to 8% and Pass@4 by 15% over base VLM models on long-video understanding benchmarks; open-sourced.

## Method
VSeek augments a base VLM with a search action space: at each reasoning step, the model can either emit an answer or issue a temporal search query ("find segments showing X occurring before Y"). A video retrieval module converts the NL query into a temporal segment retrieval, returning the most relevant clips. The model then conditions its next reasoning step on the retrieved clips. RL training uses GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with rewards derived from temporal logic verification: user questions are first parsed into a set of temporal logic constraints (e.g., "object A appears before event B"), and the retrieved clips are programmatically checked against each constraint, providing a binary, dense reward for each retrieved segment rather than a single outcome reward at the end of the trajectory.

## Why It Matters
VSeek advances two distinct fronts simultaneously: (1) it provides a practical recipe for turning any long-video QA model into an evidence-seeking agent without architectural changes, and (2) it solves the key open problem of providing verifiable dense rewards for RL in video settings by leveraging formal temporal logic. The neuro-symbolic reward approach is broadly applicable to any multi-step visual reasoning task where formal specifications can be derived from natural language. For researchers working on agentic AI and video understanding simultaneously, this is a direct existence proof that RL can train agents to actively gather evidence from long video streams.

### 9. Reward Lightning: Fast Video Generation via Homologous Preference Distillation
**Score: 80/100** · [arXiv:2607.03960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03960) · [Project Page](https://reward-lightning.github.io)

**Authors:** Jiaxiang Cheng, Bing Ma, Xuhua Ren, Kai Yu, Peng Zhang, Tianxiang Zheng (Tencent Hunyuan)

## TL;DR
Reward Lightning jointly solves preference alignment and distillation acceleration in video diffusion by ensuring both objectives operate on identical latent features ("homology"), eliminating the gradient conflicts that cause existing methods to compromise one goal for the other — generating high-fidelity aligned videos in 1–4 steps.

## Problem
Video diffusion models need both preference alignment (producing what users actually prefer) and distillation (generating in far fewer denoising steps than the full diffusion chain). Existing approaches address these separately or jointly but over different representation spaces — the preference model scores decoded pixel-space videos while distillation loss operates on latent features, creating gradient conflicts where improving alignment degrades distilled quality and vice versa.

## Key Contributions
- **Latent Reward Model (LRM)**: a reward model that scores video quality directly in the diffusion latent space (without pixel decoding), trained on a multi-margin preference dataset augmented with few-step distillation samples; surpasses pixel-level and prior latent-level reward baselines by 11.0% and 14.7% in preference accuracy.
- **Homologous Preference Distillation (HPD)**: reuses the LRM backbone for both preference scoring and adversarial distillation in a single unified network — because both objectives see identical latent representations, gradient conflicts are eliminated by construction.
- **Time-Modulated Attention Head**: a specialized reward head that conditions on diffusion timestep, enabling the LRM to correctly evaluate partially-denoised latents at any step in the diffusion chain.
- Generates videos in 1–4 steps with a 2.1% improvement on VBench average score; leads on text alignment, motion quality, and visual quality sub-metrics.

## Method
The LRM is trained on a preference dataset constructed by augmenting standard video preference pairs with few-step distillation variants, creating a multi-margin ranking objective that covers the distribution of both distilled and full-step videos. The LRM architecture uses a time-modulated attention head so it can score latents at any denoising step. HPD then frames both distillation and preference alignment as losses computed on the shared LRM backbone: an adversarial distillation loss (matching the student's few-step latent distribution to the teacher's full-step distribution) and a preference alignment loss (maximizing LRM reward for student samples relative to less-preferred alternatives) are jointly optimized through the same feature routing, ensuring gradient directions remain compatible. Adaptive preference weighting dynamically balances the two losses during training.

## Why It Matters
The homology principle — ensuring that all learning objectives operate on identical representations — resolves a fundamental tension that has limited joint optimization in video generation since diffusion distillation began. The resulting Reward Lightning generators are both fast (1–4 steps) and well-aligned with human preferences, which is the practical combination needed for real-world deployment. The Tencent Hunyuan affiliation suggests this will be integrated into production video generation infrastructure.

### 10. Aura: Consistent Multi-Subject Video Generation via VLM-Grounded Semantic Alignment
**Score: 80/100** · [arXiv:2607.04311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04311) · [Project Page](https://aura-project-page.github.io/)

**Authors:** Zixiang Zhou, Zhentao Yu, Yifeng Ma, Hongmei Wang, Wenqing Yu, Cong Wang, Zilin Yang, Rui Chen, Jiarong Ou, Yezhou Liu, Yuan Zhou, Qinglin Lu (Tencent Hunyuan)

## TL;DR
Aura is a unified framework for high-fidelity, identity-consistent multi-subject video generation that uses VLM-extracted multimodal semantic features and a two-stage VLM-to-DiT alignment strategy to preserve fine-grained appearance across all subjects simultaneously, achieving state-of-the-art on both single- and multi-subject generation.

## Problem
Subject-consistent video generation — generating a video where a specific person, character, or object maintains its exact visual identity throughout — is already challenging for a single subject. Multi-subject generation (multiple specific characters, objects, and backgrounds all simultaneously consistent with their reference images) remains largely unsolved: models tend to either blend identities, fall into copy-paste artifacts, or fail to model complex inter-subject interactions and relationships.

## Key Contributions
- **AI Director-Level Captions (MTSS)**: dense, structured multi-element descriptions that capture both per-subject appearance attributes and global inter-subject relationships and scene dynamics — providing richer conditioning than standard VLM captions.
- **Dual-Stream VLM conditioning**: a vision-language model with learnable queries extracts both global semantic features and fine-grained visual cues from reference images; a two-stage alignment strategy (text-space alignment then DiT feature-space alignment) bridges the representational gap.
- **Subject-aware RoPE-Shift**: modifies the rotary position encoding to distinguish different subject types (human, object, scene), reducing cross-subject identity confusion and copy-paste artifacts.
- **Progressive-APG (Adaptive Prompt Guidance)**: inference-time strategy that gradually increases guidance strength on semantic alignment, alleviating oversaturation while improving fidelity to the multi-subject reference.
- Achieves state-of-the-art on OpenS2V-Eval and human preference studies for both single-subject and multi-element video generation.

## Method
Reference images for each subject are encoded by a shared VLM (with learnable subject-type-specific queries) that outputs both global text-space semantic embeddings and fine-grained visual feature embeddings. The two-stage alignment first maps VLM text-space embeddings to T5-compatible text tokens via a lightweight adapter, then adapts DiT cross-attention layers to additionally attend to the fine-grained visual feature embeddings via token concatenation. The subject-aware RoPE-Shift applies subject-type-conditioned offsets to the positional encoding, preventing the DiT from conflating positional features across subjects of different categories. A high-quality training dataset is built via a dedicated video-subject curation pipeline that extracts temporally consistent subject appearances from in-the-wild video. Progressive-APG scales guidance strength over the inference trajectory to balance identity consistency with generation diversity.

## Why It Matters
Multi-subject video generation is the practical frontier for commercial video production: advertisement videos require specific products and brand characters to appear together; filmmakers need specific actors or props to maintain identity across shots. Aura solves the multi-subject alignment problem with a principled VLM-grounded approach that doesn't degrade to copy-paste, demonstrating that the key ingredients are structured semantic conditioning + subject-type-aware positional encoding.
