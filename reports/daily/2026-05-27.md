### 1. E³C: Video Generation with 3D Environmental Memory and Ego-Exo Human Pose Control

**Score: 91/100** · [arXiv:2605.26316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26316)  
**Authors:** Qiao Gu, Lingni Ma, Adam W Harley, Richard Newcombe, Florian Shkurti, Julian Straub

## TL;DR
E³C is a controllable egocentric video diffusion framework that conditions generation on a semi-dense point cloud-based 3D environmental memory augmented with appearance features, plus explicit ego and exo human pose controls, enabling geometrically grounded and human-motion-adherent egocentric video synthesis.

## Problem
Generating controllable egocentric video is exceptionally difficult because the camera is tightly coupled to the actor, causing rapid viewpoint changes and frequent self-occlusions of the wearer's own body. Existing video diffusion models lack persistent 3D spatial memory to maintain scene consistency across large camera motions, and do not jointly handle both the ego wearer's articulated body (often partially out of view) and surrounding exocentric people. Standard pose-on-frame conditioning degrades when body parts are occluded or truncated.

## Key Contributions
- A semi-dense point cloud-based 3D environmental memory built from context frames, where each point is augmented with video-VAE appearance descriptors and rendered into the target camera view as a persistent structural condition.
- An ego pose encoder that converts 3D body joints and 6DoF wrist poses into persistent cross-attention tokens (rather than drawing poses onto frames), preserving ego-motion control under self-occlusion and out-of-view configurations.
- Exo human pose control via rendered 2D skeleton overlays from YOLO-X/ViTPose detections, jointly conditioned alongside the 3D memory.
- Demonstration that explicit, disentangled conditioning enables intuitive scene editing (removing objects from 3D memory, changing surrounding people's motion) without retraining.

## Method
E³C is initialized from Wan2.1-VACE-1.3B (a video latent diffusion model) and LoRA fine-tuned on the Nymeria egocentric dataset. From 200 context frames, it constructs a semi-dense point cloud using Project Aria MPS, then augments each point with per-point appearance features extracted from the video-VAE encoder and renders these into target camera views as spatial conditioning. Exo human skeletons are overlaid on rendered point-cloud frames and injected via a context adapter, while ego body and wrist poses are encoded into persistent cross-attention tokens through a temporal transformer with gated fusion, which then cross-attend into the main video DiT. At inference time, 3D memory anchors scene structure, pose tokens steer human motion, and the diffusion prior fills missing appearance and dynamics.

## Results
On the Nymeria dataset, E³C achieves state-of-the-art results across all evaluated axes: FVD 249 vs. next-best 328 (VACE pts+exo FT), PSNR 18.6 vs. 17.3, TErr 2.40 cm vs. 3.25 cm, and Hand-F1 41.44 vs. 34.24, outperforming fine-tuned VACE variants with similar conditioning. Compared to dedicated egocentric generators, E³C achieves LPIPS 17.3 vs. 24.3 (EgoControl) and dramatically reduces camera error (TErr 2.40 cm vs. EgoTwin's 67.0 cm).

## Why It Matters
E³C advances the state of the art in controllable human video generation for embodied settings, directly enabling embodied agents to simulate and reason about how actions change the world from a first-person perspective. The disentanglement of persistent scene structure from human-driven dynamics, and the novel pose tokenization approach for handling self-occlusion, offer transferable design patterns for any video generation system that must jointly control camera trajectories and articulated human motion. The explicit conditioning also unlocks practical scene-editing capabilities without retraining, making the framework useful for data augmentation and simulation in robotics and AR/VR research.

### 2. LongCat-Video-Avatar 1.5 Technical Report

**Score: 87/100** · [arXiv:2605.26486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26486)  
**Authors:** Meituan LongCat Team, Xunliang Cai, Meng Cheng, Feng Gao, Zhe Kong, Jiamu Li, Le Li, Weiheng Li, Hongyu Liu, Shuai Tan, Xiaoming Wei, Tianyu Yang, Yong Zhang

## TL;DR
LongCat-Video-Avatar 1.5 is an open-source audio-driven human avatar video generation framework from Meituan that achieves commercial-grade quality through rigorous data engineering, per-frame RLHF training, and inference distillation to 8 NFEs, matching or surpassing closed-source systems like HeyGen and OmniHuman 1.5.

## Problem
Existing audio-driven avatar generation methods that perform well on curated benchmarks fail to meet production requirements: they lack long-horizon temporal stability, identity consistency, robust lip synchronization across diverse speaking styles, and the ability to handle real-world complexity such as multi-person interactions, stylized characters, and object handling. A scalable open-source framework meeting all these requirements simultaneously does not yet exist, while systems with genuine commercial-grade quality remain proprietary.

## Key Contributions
- Upgraded audio encoder from Wav2Vec2 (94M params) to Whisper-large (1.5B params, trained on 680k hours), yielding substantially tighter lip synchronization and more natural mouth dynamics.
- Per-frame Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) extending video-level RLHF to temporally fine-grained credit assignment, improving physical rationality and reducing hand/face distortion.
- Parameter-efficient DMD2 distillation using a single DiT backbone with interchangeable Generator and Fake Score LoRA adapters, compressing inference from 150 NFEs to 8 NFEs with minimal quality loss.
- Specialized data curation for multi-person, silent, and emotional content, including a silent audio track mechanism to prevent background characters from exhibiting speech-driven lip motion.

## Method
The system builds on a DiT-based 3D video diffusion architecture with 3D VAE, 3D self-attention, text cross-attention, and an additional audio cross-attention layer per block, conditioned on Whisper-large audio embeddings resampled to video frame rate and temporally compressed to match latent sequence length. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) base model training via flow matching from low-resolution to 720p with multi-person dialogue data; (2) GRPO post-training with per-frame reward signals and a multi-clip rollout strategy (up to 5 sequential clips); (3) DMD2 acceleration distillation to 8 NFEs using a shared backbone with dynamically mounted LoRA adapters. Multi-person generation uses L-RoPE to bind each speaker region to its audio condition, with dedicated silent audio tracks assigned to background characters via bounding-box-guided attention separation.

## Results
On a benchmark of 508 diverse image-audio pairs evaluated by 770 crowdsourced raters and 10 domain experts, LC-Video-Avatar 1.5 (8 NFE) achieves leading performance in temporal stability, identity consistency, and physical rationality, winning pairwise A/B preference tests against HeyGen, OmniHuman 1.5, and Kling Avatar 2.0. The accelerated 8-NFE model shows lower rationality issue rates (32.4%) than the 50-step base (51.5%), attributed to DMD2 suppressing hand distortion and over-exaggerated facial expressions, while the base retains an advantage in human-likeness (3.389 vs. 3.336 single-person).

## Why It Matters
This work demonstrates that a carefully engineered open-source system can match or exceed proprietary commercial avatars without architectural novelty, making production-quality audio-driven video generation broadly accessible. The per-frame GRPO formulation and parameter-efficient DMD2 distillation strategy are generalizable techniques applicable to other long-video diffusion systems. The release narrows the gap between academic research prototypes and deployable digital human applications relevant to broadcasting, e-commerce, education, and entertainment.

### 3. MRT: Masked Region Transformer for Layered Image Generation and Editing at Scale

**Score: 86/100** · [arXiv:2605.27235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27235)  
**Authors:** Zhicong Tang, Zhao Zhang, Jingye Chen, Mohan Zhou, Yifan Pu, Yuchi Liu, Yalong Bai, Ethan Smith, Yuhui Yuan

## TL;DR
MRT is a 20B-parameter masked region diffusion model for multi-layer transparent image generation and editing, trained on 10M multilingual design samples, that unifies text-to-layers, image-to-layers, and layers-to-layers tasks in a single framework with dramatically improved efficiency over prior art.

## Problem
Layered image generation and editing — producing multiple independent, reusable RGBA transparent layers from text or image inputs — is a fundamental but underexplored capability at scale. Existing approaches either lack overflow layer support, require linearly scaling compute with layer count, or fail to unify generation and editing tasks. No prior work has studied layered image editing (layers-to-layers) at scale.

## Key Contributions
- A unified masked region diffusion framework that handles three tasks (text-to-layers, image-to-layers, layers-to-layers) via selective token masking, where masked clean tokens serve as conditioning and noised tokens are denoised.
- An overflow-aware canvas layer representation that handles layers extending beyond the visible canvas boundary, preserving editability for the >60% of real designs that contain overflow content.
- Distribution matching distillation (DMD) combined with CacheDiT and sequence parallelization, achieving ~3–6 second single-image inference on 1–4× H100/H200 GPUs — up to 108.5× speedup and 23.6× memory reduction vs. Qwen-Image-Layered.
- A layer grouping augmentation strategy that randomly merges overlapping layers during training to improve robustness to ambiguous boundaries.

## Method
MRT is built on full-parameter fine-tuned Qwen-Image (60 layers, hidden dim 3584), trained in two progressive stages: ~70K iterations at 512×512 on the full 10M dataset, then ~20K iterations at 1024×1024 on 64× H200 GPUs. The core masked region diffusion mechanism concatenates latent tokens for the composed image, background, and all foreground layers; per-task conditioning is achieved by assigning known layers as masked clean tokens and denoising only the target slots via a flow-matching objective. For image-to-layers, the model uses layout bounding boxes (from a z-order-aware detector) to spatially constrain each layer's generation region, avoiding the O(K) token scaling of per-layer full-resolution approaches.

## Results
On image-to-layers, MRT achieves PSNR_merged of 27.34/25.91/25.72 dB vs. Qwen-Image-Layered's 25.81/23.06/22.18 dB across the 4–8, 8–16, and 16–32 layer buckets. In user studies against Qwen-Image-Layered, MRT wins 79.5%, 68.9%, and 82.6% for layer quality, integrity, and granularity respectively, and is preferred over Lovart, RoboNeo, and other commercial systems across all quality dimensions.

## Why It Matters
MRT demonstrates that layered image synthesis — the visual analogue of word-level text editing — can be solved at scale with a single unified model, enabling practical creative workflows where generated assets are immediately decomposable and re-editable. The regional masking design decouples compute from layer count, making the approach tractable for designs with 25+ layers. By open-sourcing a 20B-parameter model trained on 10M real-world multilingual design data, this establishes a strong public baseline for the image-to-layers decomposition task that commercial systems have only recently begun to address.

### 4. PARE: Pruning and Adaptive Routing for Efficient Video Generation

**Score: 85/100** · [arXiv:2605.27336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27336)  
**Authors:** Yutong Wang, Yunke Wang, Tianfan Xue, Yu Qiao, Yaohui Wang, Xinyuan Chen, Chang Xu

## TL;DR
PARE is a compression framework for video Diffusion Transformers that jointly prunes attention heads with spatial-temporal awareness (width) and routes transformer blocks dynamically per input and denoising timestep (depth), halving per-step compute on Wan2.1-14B while preserving generation quality.

## Problem
Video Diffusion Transformers like Wan2.1-14B require enormous compute per inference step due to wide (many heads/neurons) and deep (many blocks) architectures iterated over dozens of denoising steps. Prior compression methods apply static architectures uniformly to every input and timestep, ignoring that temporal attention heads are critical for motion coherence yet have smaller activations, and that block importance varies with both denoising stage and visual content. No existing method simultaneously addresses intra-block redundancy with spatial-temporal awareness and inter-block redundancy with input-adaptive routing.

## Key Contributions
- Spatial-temporal aware width pruning that classifies attention heads into spatial/temporal/mixed types, applies a protection multiplier (α=1.5) to temporal heads to correct magnitude bias, and uses importance-plus-diversity greedy selection for FFN neurons.
- A content-adaptive block router: a lightweight MLP conditioned on both sinusoidal timestep embedding and visual content (CLIP image embedding for I2V; noisy latent statistics for T2V) that selects which blocks to execute via top-K gating with a linear budget schedule allocating more blocks at high-noise timesteps.
- A progressive three-stage training pipeline: width-only distillation (feature matching + teacher/data velocity losses), then joint student+router optimization, then optional step distillation (Self-Forcing) for orthogonal trajectory compression.
- Demonstrated orthogonal composability: PARE's ~2× per-step compression multiplies with step distillation (50→4 steps, 2× CFG removal) for a projected ~50× total speedup.

## Method
Width pruning computes WANDA-style importance scores for self-attention and cross-attention heads with timestep weighting, scales temporal head scores by α=1.5, then greedily selects top-K heads per block; FFN neurons are scored by the product of up/down projection norms and selected with cosine-similarity diversity filtering. The block router is a 3-layer MLP mapping [SinEmb(t); e_c] to N block-wise logits, applying top-K hard gating via straight-through estimator with a budget K(t) linearly scheduled between K_min=20 and K_max=35. Training proceeds from width-only distillation first (7K steps on 4× H200), then joint width-routing training initialized from Stage I, with feature matching restricted to active blocks only.

## Results
On Wan2.1-T2V-14B, PARE (6.7B active params, 52% reduction) achieves VBench average 77.10 vs. teacher 77.70, outperforming all structural compression baselines (NeoDragon: 74.68, ICMD: 56.37, F3-Pruning: 69.84) at comparable compression ratios. On I2V-14B, PARE (7.7B, 52% reduction) scores VBench 76.24 vs. teacher 77.92, substantially beating NeoDragon (73.65), ICMD (57.79), and F3-Pruning (67.55). Combined with 4-step distillation, PARE achieves ~77.00 (T2V) and ~75.93 (I2V), competitive with full-model distillation methods at far fewer active parameters per step.

## Why It Matters
PARE directly addresses a core bottleneck for deploying large-scale video generation models: the prohibitive per-step compute of 14B-parameter DiTs. By demonstrating that spatial-temporal head specialization must be respected during pruning and that block importance varies per sample and timestep, PARE provides principled design principles generalizable beyond Wan2.1. The orthogonal composability with step distillation — yielding up to ~50× total speedup — makes high-quality real-time video generation plausibly achievable on constrained hardware.
