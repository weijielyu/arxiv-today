### 1. WorldDirector: Building Controllable World Simulators with Persistent Dynamic Memory
**Score: 88/100** · [arXiv:2607.02517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02517) · [Project Page](https://worlddirector.github.io/)

**Authors:** Hanlin Wang, Hao Ouyang, Qiuyu Wang, Wen Wang, Qingyan Bai, Ka Leong Cheng, Yue Yu, Yixuan Li, Yihao Meng, Zichen Liu, Yanhong Zeng, Yujun Shen, Qifeng Chen

## TL;DR
WorldDirector decouples LLM-driven 3D trajectory planning from video synthesis to build a video world simulator with genuine object permanence — dynamic entities retain their exact visual identities and physical trajectories even after prolonged periods off-screen, then re-enter the frame exactly where physics says they should be.

## Problem
All current video world models entangle motion simulation with pixel rendering and depend on continuous visual observation to keep dynamic objects alive. When an entity disappears from the camera view, models either lose track of it entirely or rely on diffusion priors to guess its state — resulting in trajectory collapse, frozen dynamics, or severe identity distortion upon re-entry. This conflation makes it impossible to build genuinely persistent interactive worlds.

## Key Contributions
- **Decoupled architecture**: an LLM acts as central orchestrator, translating high-level user instructions into explicit 3D bounding-box trajectories and camera paths, then injecting them as conditioning signals into video generation — physical logic is separated from pixel synthesis.
- **Appearance Binding mechanism**: RGB object features are retrieved from context frames as visual anchors, allowing the model to reconstruct the correct fine-grained appearance of any entity that re-enters the scene after an out-of-view interval.
- **Spatial-aware cross-attention**: per-entity text prompts are routed to their corresponding 2D bounding-box regions, giving independent semantic control over multiple co-existing dynamic objects within a single generation pass.
- **Causal autoregressive generation**: the system produces long-horizon videos in causal chunks, accumulating dynamic memory persistently across chunks without resetting object states.

## Method
At inference time an LLM receives user instructions (e.g., "person A walks left, exits the frame, then re-enters from the right after 10 seconds") and produces 3D bounding-box trajectories and a camera path. These 3D trajectories are projected to per-frame 2D bounding boxes and fed as spatial location conditions to a video diffusion model. Simultaneously, the Appearance Binding module retrieves stored RGB crops of each object from previous context frames and injects them as visual anchors via cross-attention. A spatial-aware cross-attention layer routes the per-entity text descriptions to the correct localized regions. The causal chunk-based inference window advances autoregressively, carrying the accumulated object state forward so memory persists across generated segments. A dedicated data curation pipeline was built to provide training signal for out-of-view dynamics and re-entry events.

## Why It Matters
Persistent world simulation — where objects continue to exist, move, and age even when the camera is not watching — is the missing ingredient between passive video generation and genuinely interactive environments usable for robotics training, game engines, and agentic AI testing. WorldDirector's LLM-orchestration design makes the "director" a first-class citizen rather than an afterthought, and the Appearance Binding mechanism solves the identity-consistency problem that has limited all prior approaches. This is a credible step toward world models where a researcher or agent can trust that the simulated world actually obeys causality.

### 2. Align4D: Alignment Is All You Need For X-to-4D Generation
**Score: 83/100** · [arXiv:2607.02516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02516) · [Project Page](https://miaoqiaowei.github.io/Align4D/)

**Authors:** Qiaowei Miao, Kehan Li, Yawei Luo, Yi Yang

## TL;DR
Align4D is a training-free framework that converts any input modality — text, image, video, or 3D — into coherent animated 4D content by discovering the precise object distances at which off-the-shelf video and multiview diffusion models naturally agree, then jointly constraining motion and geometry through synchronized alignment.

## Problem
Existing 4D generation methods (text-to-4D, image-to-4D, video-to-4D, 3D-to-4D) are each locked to a single input modality, forcing users to commit to one representation upfront and sacrificing either motion realism, geometric consistency, or both. Building a unified any-modal pipeline from scratch is cost-prohibitive: the required multi-modal 4D datasets and new architectures are beyond current data and compute budgets.

## Key Contributions
- **Object Distance Alignment (VAOD/MAOD)**: identifies the virtual camera-to-object distances at which 4D renderings best match the output distribution of a video diffusion model (VAOD) and a multiview diffusion model (MAOD) simultaneously, resolving the scale/perspective conflict between the two generative priors without retraining either.
- **Motion-Geometry Joint Alignment**: synchronizes video (for motion) and 3D (for geometry) conditioning signals to constrain both observed and unobserved views during 4D optimization, ensuring temporal coherence and spatial completeness.
- **Asynchronous Optimization**: alternates training of 3D Gaussian attributes (shape, color) and the deformation network (motion) in separate phases, preventing each from corrupting the other's gradients.
- **X4D benchmark dataset**: a new multi-modal evaluation suite integrating prompt, image, video, and 3D inputs paired with ground-truth 4D content, enabling fair comparison of X-to-4D methods across modalities.

## Method
Align4D treats the 4D generation problem as aligning a dynamic 3D Gaussian representation with two independent pretrained models: a video diffusion model (which knows about motion) and a multiview diffusion model (which knows about multi-angle 3D appearance). For each modality pair, an alignment search finds VAOD and MAOD — the rendering distances at which the 4D asset's projected frames most closely match what each diffusion model would generate. These distances define scale-consistent rendering viewpoints used throughout optimization. Motion-Geometry Joint Alignment then runs a combined loss that simultaneously (a) matches video frames from the VAOD viewpoint against the video prior and (b) matches multiview renders from the MAOD viewpoints against the multiview prior. Asynchronous Optimization separates Gaussian attribute updates from deformation network updates in alternating iterations. The entire pipeline wraps frozen, pretrained models and requires no additional training.

## Why It Matters
The X-to-4D formulation is practically significant: content creators rarely start with a single modality. Align4D's alignment insight — that the tension between video and 3D diffusion models can be resolved by finding the right object distance for each — is elegant and immediately applicable to any combination of off-the-shelf models. As video and multiview generators improve, Align4D automatically inherits their quality gains. The X4D benchmark also gives the field a standardized way to compare cross-modal 4D systems, which has been missing.
